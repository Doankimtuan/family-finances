# Financial First Principles

These are the WHY behind every domain in ViNha. Product principles describe *how we build.* These principles describe *why the domains exist.* They are the foundational beliefs about household finance that justify every design decision.

---

## Principle 1: Ownership

**Real money has a home.**

Every unit of real money the household possesses resides in an identifiable Account. You cannot plan money whose location you cannot name. Ownership is the most basic financial truth: *this money exists, and it exists here.*

Ownership justifies the Accounts domain. Without accounts, there is no ground truth. Every other domain — planning, health, inbox — depends on knowing what is owned and where.

**Domain foundation:** Accounts

---

## Principle 2: Cash Flow

**Money moves. Every movement is a fact.**

Transactions are the atoms of financial truth. Money enters (income) and money leaves (expenses). These events are not optional, not debatable, not ambiguous. A transaction happened, or it did not. There is no middle ground.

Cash flow is the pulse of household finance. You cannot assess health without knowing flow. You cannot plan without knowing what flows in and out.

**Domain foundation:** Transactions

---

## Principle 3: Liquidity

**Not all wealth is spendable now.**

Money in a fixed deposit is not the same as money in a checking account. A credit card provides spending power that is not wealth. Liquidity answers *can we spend this right now?* — a question distinct from *how much are we worth?*

Liquidity justifies the Cards domain (spending power vs. real wealth) and affects how Accounts are presented (available balance vs. total balance).

**Domain foundation:** Cards, Accounts

---

## Principle 4: Debt

**Borrowed money is an obligation, not wealth.**

Credit card balances and installment plans are real obligations. They reduce the household's true financial position. Debt is not "negative savings" — it has its own structure, rhythm, and psychology.

Debt justifies the Installments and Cards domains. Without explicit debt management, the household's true financial picture is incomplete.

**Domain foundation:** Installments, Cards

---

## Principle 5: Commitment

**A goal without a deadline is a wish.**

Commitment transforms vague aspirations into concrete targets. "I want to save for a vacation" is not a commitment. "I will allocate 3 million VND per month to the Vacation Jar to reach 36 million VND by December" is a commitment.

Commitment justifies the Goals domain. Goals are the forward-looking layer of intention — they answer "what are we working toward?"

**Domain foundation:** Goals

---

## Principle 6: Planning

**Intentions without systems are just hopes.**

A household that manually decides where every dong goes every month will burn out. Planning — recurring rules, income placement strategies — automates the routine so humans can focus on the exceptions. The system should handle the predictable; humans should handle the decisions.

Planning justifies the Planning domain. It is the "set it and forget it" layer that makes household finance sustainable.

**Domain foundation:** Planning

---

## Principle 7: Forecasting**

**Today's patterns predict tomorrow's outcomes.**

The household's recurring behavior — income, spending, saving — forms a pattern. That pattern projects forward. Forecasting is not fortune-telling; it is pattern recognition. "Based on the last 6 months, you are on track to save X by year end."

Forecasting justifies the Goals domain (target tracking) and Health domain (scenarios, projections). It is why recurring rules exist — because patterns matter.

**Domain foundation:** Goals, Health

---

## Principle 8: Risk

**Financial surprise is the enemy of financial peace.**

An emergency fund is not a luxury — it is a buffer against the unknown. Overspending without guardrails erodes trust. The system must surface risk before it becomes crisis.

Risk justifies overspend policies (BR-07), Health monitoring (early warning), and the Inbox (unresolved items = unmanaged risk).

**Domain foundation:** Health, Inbox

---

## Principle 9: Accountability

**Money decisions have consequences — make them visible.**

When a partner overspends on entertainment, the household should see it. Not to shame, but to decide. Accountability is not blame; it is shared awareness. Transparency enables collaboration.

Accountability justifies the Together domain (shared visibility) and the Inbox (decisions are surfaced, not hidden).

**Domain foundation:** Together, Inbox

---

## Principle 10: Behavior

**Systems shape habits. Habits shape outcomes.**

A household that reviews its Inbox weekly builds a habit of financial awareness. A household that performs the Month Ritual builds a habit of reflection. The system's design either encourages or discourages these behaviors.

Behavior justifies the Month Ritual domain (the monthly ceremony), the Inbox (the weekly habit), and the Health domain (the mirror that motivates).

**Domain foundation:** Month Close, Inbox, Health

---

## Principle 11: Habit

**Small, repeated actions compound.**

Checking the Home chip daily. Clearing the Inbox weekly. Performing the Month Ritual monthly. These are not features — they are the financial equivalent of brushing your teeth. The system's job is to make these habits easy and satisfying.

Habit justifies the Home surface (daily check-in), the Inbox (weekly clearing), and the Month Ritual (monthly ceremony).

**Domain foundation:** Month Close, Inbox

---

## Principle 12: Wealth Building

**Saving is not the same as wealth building.**

Setting aside money in a Jar (saving for a purchase) is not the same as putting money into an interest-bearing savings product. Wealth building involves compounding, maturity, and product selection. The former is intention; the latter is a real financial action.

Wealth building justifies the Savings domain as distinct from Jar-based savings goals. A savings product is a real asset; a savings Jar is an intention.

**Domain foundation:** Savings

---

## Principle 13: Emergency

**The unexpected will happen. Plan for it.**

Every household needs accessible funds for emergencies. This is not a "rainy day" Jar — it is a fundamental financial principle. The system should make emergency readiness visible and actionable.

Emergency justifies the Health domain (emergency fund indicators) and the Planning domain (emergency fund as a recurring allocation).

**Domain foundation:** Health, Planning

---

## Principle 14: Transparency

**Hidden money breaks trust.**

If one partner can see a transaction and the other cannot, the system has failed. Household finance requires radical transparency. Every transaction, every Jar allocation, every Inbox decision — visible to all Partners.

Transparency justifies the Together domain's shared-access model and BR-13 (material changes are partner-visible).

**Domain foundation:** Together

---

## Principle 15: Trust

**The system must earn trust before it can manage money.**

If the system confuses plans with balances, it erodes trust. If it invents numbers, it destroys trust. If it hides decisions in menus, it fails trust. Trust is built through clarity, not through features.

Trust justifies BR-01 (real ≠ virtual), BR-14 (AI does not invent money), and the entire philosophy of two financial truths.

**Domain foundation:** All domains

---

## Principle 16: Collaboration

**Household finance is a team sport.**

Two people managing shared money need shared tools. Collaboration is not a feature — it is the premise. The system's job is to make collaboration natural, not to bolt it on.

Collaboration justifies the Together domain as a first-class concern.

**Domain foundation:** Together

---

## Principle 17: Decision-Making

**Unmade decisions are financial debt.**

Every unmapped expense, every unresolved savings maturity, every unapproved policy change — these are decisions the household owes itself. The Inbox makes this debt visible and repayable.

Decision-making justifies the Inbox domain and the "Inbox zero" goal.

**Domain foundation:** Inbox

---

## Principle 18: Discipline

**Freedom within a framework.**

The Month Ritual is not a restriction — it is a release. By closing one month and opening the next, the household creates a clean slate. Discipline is not punishment; it is the structure that enables freedom.

Discipline justifies the Month Ritual domain and BR-08 (locking plan movements after approval).

**Domain foundation:** Month Close

---

## Principle 19: Reflection

**You cannot improve what you do not see.**

The Health domain exists because households need a mirror. Without reflection, patterns go unnoticed, risks accumulate, and behavior never changes. The mirror does not judge — it shows.

Reflection justifies the Health domain and its read-only relationship to all other domains.

**Domain foundation:** Health

---

## Principle 20: Simplicity

**Every domain must justify its existence.**

If a domain cannot clearly state what financial truth it represents and what user problem it solves, it should not exist. ViNha is not a feature collection — it is a coherent system of financial truths.

Simplicity justifies the domain boundary discipline enforced throughout this artifact pack.

**Domain foundation:** All domains

# Product Risks

## Financial Risks

- Users may still interpret credit limit or available credit as affordability.
- Partial payments may be misunderstood as financially safe.
- Refunds may create false confidence before issuer confirmation.
- Interest and fees may be underrepresented if provider-specific detail is unavailable.
- Card-origin installments may be double-counted or hidden if boundaries are unclear.

## UX Complexity Risks

- Statement balance, outstanding balance, remaining due, and available credit are easy to confuse.
- Too much card infrastructure language can overwhelm users.
- Manual card tracking may feel tedious for high-frequency spending.
- Shared card visibility can create partner conflict if responsibility is unclear.

## Maintenance Risks

- Issuer-specific rules vary and can change.
- Fee, interest, reward, and dispute models can grow quickly.
- Provider-import assumptions may become brittle if introduced too early.
- International card behavior could complicate Vietnam-first simplicity.

## Scalability Risks

- Multi-card households increase reconciliation and attribution complexity.
- Subscriptions and tokenized cards can hide true card usage.
- Replacement cards can fragment history if identity rules are unclear.
- Cross-domain overlap with Loans and Planning can duplicate obligations.

## Education Risks

- Users need language that distinguishes cash, credit, obligation, bill, and repayment.
- Users may not naturally know statement date or minimum payment.
- Users may overvalue rewards and undervalue interest risk.
- Partner-facing explanations must avoid blame and surveillance tones.

## Mitigation Direction

This board mitigates risk by approving only household-recognizable card obligations, modifying terminology-sensitive areas, deferring provider-heavy automation, and rejecting direct BR-01 or BR-24 violations.

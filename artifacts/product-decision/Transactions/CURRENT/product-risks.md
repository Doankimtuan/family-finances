# Product Risks

## Financial Risk

- Transfers may be double-counted as income or expense.
- Refunds may be misread as new income.
- Corrections may damage trust if they feel like hidden edits.
- Category or jar mapping may be mistaken for real money movement.
- Provider data may be delayed, duplicated, or wrong if imported later.

## UX Complexity Risk

- Too many transaction states can overwhelm ordinary users.
- Categorization can become repetitive work.
- Refund, reversal, correction, and adjustment language can be confusing.
- Search may fail if users remember merchant names that are not captured.

## Maintenance Risk

- Provider import, merchant normalization, duplicate detection, and AI classification can become long-term maintenance burdens.
- Too much transaction metadata can make the domain harder to keep stable.
- Split categorization can expand product complexity beyond household value.

## Scalability Risk

- Transaction volume grows over years.
- More accounts, cards, wallets, and household members increase ambiguity.
- Future provider integrations may create data quality and reconciliation pressure.

## Education Risk

- Users may need help understanding transfer neutrality.
- Users may not distinguish category from planning intent.
- Users may assume Health conclusions can change transaction facts.
- Users may think AI suggestions are authoritative.

## User Misunderstanding Risk

- Personal transactions in shared finance can feel like surveillance.
- Cash gaps can make the full ledger feel untrustworthy.
- Card payments can be confused with card purchases.
- Pending activity can be mistaken for final spending.

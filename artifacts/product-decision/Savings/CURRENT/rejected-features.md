# Rejected Features

## Personal/Business Distinction

Decision: REJECTED for Savings.

Why: ViNha is a household money OS, not a business accounting product.

Business value: Low for target users.

Complexity: Medium.

Risk: Scope creep into SME finance.

Expected frequency: Low.

Maintenance cost: Medium.

Alternative: Keep business funds outside household Savings or classify at Account/Ledger level if the whole product expands later.

Release: Never in Savings.

## Automatic Recurring Saving Transfer

Decision: REJECTED for Savings.

Why: Recurring intent belongs to Planning. Actual recurring bank transfer execution would move ViNha toward banking automation.

Business value: Medium, but outside Savings ownership.

Complexity: High.

Risk: Real money movement without adequate provider authority; BR-01 confusion.

Expected frequency: Monthly.

Maintenance cost: High.

Alternative: Plan can remind or intend; Accounts/Ledger records actual transfers.

Release: Never in Savings.

## Provider Marketplace or Rate Shopping

Decision: REJECTED.

Why: This would reposition ViNha as a financial product marketplace.

Business value: Medium for yield chasers, low for core household OS.

Complexity: High.

Risk: Stale data, advice liability, partner incentives.

Expected frequency: Occasional.

Maintenance cost: High.

Alternative: Informational rate change context for user's existing product.

Release: Never as Savings core.

## Regulated Investment-Like Advice

Decision: REJECTED.

Why: Savings is safety/liquidity. Investment advice belongs outside this domain and may require regulated treatment.

Business value: Not appropriate.

Complexity: Very high.

Risk: Legal, trust, and product identity risk.

Expected frequency: Occasional.

Maintenance cost: Very high.

Alternative: Health can read and narrate risk; future Investment domain can handle investment products.

Release: Never in Savings.

# Pain Points

## Current Frustrations

- Financial signals are scattered across bank apps, wallet apps, SMS, email, screenshots, receipts, and chat.
- One partner often lacks context for the other's spending.
- Forgotten decisions make month-end review difficult.
- Provider messages may be vague or duplicated.
- Families may know that something happened but not how to classify it.
- Savings and bill decisions may arrive at inconvenient times.

## Confusing Concepts

- Difference between notification and decision.
- Difference between transaction and review item.
- Difference between reading an item and resolving it.
- Difference between acknowledgement and financial action.
- Difference between expired reminder and paid obligation.
- Difference between suggestion and confirmed household decision.

## Unnecessary Complexity Risks

- Too many item types may feel technical.
- Multiple end states can feel like accounting language.
- Confidence scores may be misunderstood as correctness.
- Archive history may be unnecessary for casual users unless it supports trust.
- A shared queue can feel like surveillance if privacy norms are unclear.

## Missing Visibility

- Why an item appeared.
- What source created it.
- Whether another partner has seen or understood it.
- Whether the item still matters.
- Whether a reminder means unpaid, due, already paid, or merely informational.
- Whether duplicate-looking items represent one event or several.

## Potential Misunderstandings

- "Inbox zero" may be misread as "everything is financially fine."
- Dismissal may be used as deletion.
- Auto-resolution may be trusted too much.
- Payment reminders may be confused with payments.
- Provider imports may be treated as complete truth.
- Partner visibility may be interpreted as partner agreement.

No solutions are proposed in this validation phase.

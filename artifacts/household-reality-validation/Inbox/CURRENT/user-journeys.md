# User Journeys

## Happy Path

1. A financial event appears.
2. The household recognizes why it needs attention.
3. The responsible partner has enough context.
4. The item is resolved quickly.
5. The household sees the queue become smaller.
6. The owning financial record or plan remains trustworthy.

Behavioral validation:

- This path matches real households when the item is obvious, recent, and low-conflict.
- It is most common for repeated merchants, salary-related activity, known bills, and expected savings events.

## Common Path

1. Several items accumulate over a few days.
2. One partner checks the queue during a quiet moment.
3. Obvious items are handled first.
4. Ambiguous items are left for later or require a partner question.
5. Some items age before the household resolves them.
6. The household eventually catches up during a weekly or month-end review.

Behavioral validation:

- This is likely the dominant young-household path.
- It reflects busy work schedules, mixed bank/wallet usage, and partial partner involvement.

## First-Time Usage

1. The household sees a list of items needing review.
2. They try to understand whether the list is notifications, tasks, or financial decisions.
3. They test the concept with obvious transactions first.
4. Trust grows if resolving items makes records clearer.
5. Trust drops if the queue feels like duplicated bank notifications.

Behavioral validation:

- The concept is understandable, but terminology needs validation.
- Users may need to experience the benefit before they value the habit.

## Long-Term Usage

1. Inbox becomes part of weekly or salary-cycle financial maintenance.
2. Repeated items become easier to recognize.
3. The household expects fewer surprises.
4. Stale or ambiguous items become signals of weak financial discipline or missing provider context.
5. Historical decisions help the household remember why something was categorized or acknowledged.

Behavioral validation:

- Long-term fit is strong if review volume remains manageable.
- Long-term risk increases if Inbox becomes a backlog.

## Recovery Path

1. Household ignores Inbox during travel, illness, conflict, or busy work.
2. Items accumulate.
3. Reports or plans feel less trustworthy.
4. Household returns to the queue and processes the most important or easiest items.
5. Some old items may be resolved from memory; others remain uncertain.

Behavioral validation:

- Recovery is realistic and necessary.
- Users need the domain to tolerate imperfect behavior without pretending old context is always recoverable.

## Shared-Context Path

1. One partner sees an unfamiliar item.
2. They ask the other partner for context.
3. The other partner recognizes the event or supplies missing meaning.
4. The item is resolved or dismissed.
5. The household's shared understanding improves.

Behavioral validation:

- This is a core shared-finance behavior.
- It validates Inbox as a partner accountability surface, not only a personal review list.

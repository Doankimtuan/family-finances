# Emotional Journey

## Application and First Use

Common feelings:

- Excitement from approval and perceived financial progress.
- Pride from being trusted by a bank.
- Uncertainty about fees, due dates, and correct usage.
- Mild anxiety about overspending.

Trust is gained when terms feel clear and the first bill matches expectations.

Trust is lost when the first statement contains unexplained fees, dates, or amounts.

## Everyday Spending

Common feelings:

- Convenience when card payment is fast.
- Satisfaction from rewards or discounts.
- Low friction because money does not leave the bank immediately.
- Hidden anxiety if the user knows the bill is accumulating.

Trust is gained when spending remains visible and explainable.

Trust is lost when card spending becomes invisible until the due date.

## Statement Closing

Common feelings:

- Clarity if the statement matches remembered purchases.
- Stress if the amount is larger than expected.
- Defensiveness if a partner questions purchases.
- Regret if discretionary spending is visible in one large bill.

Trust is gained when the statement becomes a shared factual reference.

Trust is lost when users cannot explain the bill.

## Repayment

Common feelings:

- Relief after full payment.
- Stress when salary timing is tight.
- Shame when only minimum payment is possible.
- Achievement when the household avoids interest.

Trust is gained when payment status is confirmed.

Trust is lost when money leaves the bank but the issuer still shows the bill unpaid.

## Error, Refund, or Dispute

Common feelings:

- Confusion while waiting for refund.
- Suspicion after unfamiliar charges.
- Urgency after fraud or card loss.
- Frustration when provider language is technical.

Trust is gained when the household can follow the final outcome.

Trust is lost when pending, posted, refunded, and reversed states are unclear.

## Long-Term Use

Common feelings:

- Confidence when cards are controlled and useful.
- Fatigue when too many cards, fees, and promotions accumulate.
- Dependence when the household uses credit to bridge recurring cash-flow gaps.
- Relief or pride when cards support goals without becoming debt traps.

Long-term trust depends on predictability, shared visibility, and a clear distinction between convenience and obligation.

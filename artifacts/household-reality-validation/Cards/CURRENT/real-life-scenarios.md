# Real-Life Scenarios

## Best Case

Scenario:

A newly married couple uses one credit card for planned household purchases, pays the full statement balance before the due date, and uses rewards without carrying interest.

User expects:

- Purchases to be visible.
- Statement amount to match remembered spending.
- Due date to be clear.
- Repayment to reduce the obligation.

Successful outcome:

- Household gains convenience and rewards without debt stress.
- Both partners understand what is owed and when it is paid.

## Typical Case

Scenario:

A young household uses a mix of debit card, QR transfer, e-wallet, and credit card. The card is used for online shopping, groceries, subscriptions, and occasional installments.

User expects:

- Card activity to be easy to remember.
- The bill to be manageable after salary arrives.
- Refunds and cashback to reduce the perceived cost.

Successful outcome:

- Household avoids surprise due dates.
- Card spending is understood well enough to plan monthly cash flow.

## Worst Case

Scenario:

The household treats the credit limit as spare money, pays only the minimum, misses the true cost of fees and interest, and loses trust between partners over unexplained purchases.

User expects:

- The card to provide short-term relief.
- Minimum payment to keep the situation under control.
- Future salary to solve the bill.

Successful outcome:

- Household understands the actual outstanding obligation and stops the uncertainty from growing.

## Unexpected Case

Scenario:

A merchant refund arrives after the statement closes, or a hotel hold temporarily reduces available credit. The household sees numbers that do not match the original purchase memory.

User expects:

- Refunds to cancel the original charge.
- Holds to disappear automatically.
- Available credit to make sense.

Successful outcome:

- Household can distinguish pending, posted, refunded, and still-due amounts.

## Emergency Case

Scenario:

A medical, travel, family-support, or urgent home repair cost is paid by credit card because bank cash is insufficient or not immediately available.

User expects:

- The card to bridge a short-term cash-flow gap.
- Partners to know the repayment pressure.
- Due dates and interest risk to be visible.

Successful outcome:

- Household can treat the card use as a real obligation and coordinate repayment without losing financial trust.

## Supplementary-Card Case

Scenario:

One partner or relative uses a supplementary card for household or personal purchases.

User expects:

- The primary cardholder to know what was spent.
- The household to separate shared and personal usage.
- The final bill to remain explainable.

Successful outcome:

- Spending attribution and repayment responsibility are understood by the people affected.

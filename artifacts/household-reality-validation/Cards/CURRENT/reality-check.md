# Reality Check

## Real-World Behavior vs Current Domain Understanding

The Phase 1 Cards domain broadly matches household reality. Young households do use cards as payment instruments, but their lived understanding is uneven. Debit cards are often understood as bank-account access, while credit cards are understood through monthly bills, limits, due dates, and anxiety more than formal card-network concepts.

The discovered model is strongest where it focuses on credit-card obligation, statement timing, repayment, refunds, and household trust. It is weaker if too much card-infrastructure detail is treated as user-facing household concern.

## Already Validated

- Cards solve real household problems because they affect payment convenience, repayment timing, shared trust, and monthly cash flow.
- Credit-card outstanding must be separate from cash-like account balance.
- Statement date, due date, limit, repayment, refund, fee, and installment concepts are financially real.
- Young households can understand due date, amount owed, and payment responsibility.
- Partner visibility matters when one person's card funds shared expenses.
- Card repayment is emotionally different from ordinary spending.
- The model remains relevant over 5-10 years because household card usage tends to become more complex with income, children, travel, online subscriptions, and larger purchases.

## Assumptions Remaining

- Users will tolerate entering statement date, due date, and limit manually.
- Users will understand statement balance versus outstanding balance if terms are carefully chosen.
- Supplementary-card usage is common enough in young households to deserve domain attention.
- Card-linked installments should stay visibly connected to Cards rather than being understood only as Loans.
- Cashback and rewards matter enough to track beyond simple statement credits.
- Debit-card identity matters independently from the underlying bank account.

## Still Needs Confirmation

- How young Vietnamese users naturally describe credit card concepts in Vietnamese.
- How many target households use credit cards regularly versus mostly debit cards, QR, and bank transfers.
- How often users miss due dates, pay minimum, carry balances, or use installments.
- How users interpret refunds that post after statement close.
- Whether users expect household tracking to match issuer statements exactly.
- Whether card data sensitivity concerns are understood by users or only by institutions.
- How partners negotiate personal-card spending inside shared finance.

## Over-Engineering Check

Likely useful:

- Credit-card obligation, due date, statement amount, repayment, and refund awareness.
- Separation between card purchase and card repayment.
- Partner-visible shared-card spending.

Potentially over-detailed for many users:

- Acquirer, clearing, BIN, network, interchange, and settlement infrastructure.
- Full card lifecycle state machines.
- Detailed interest formula mechanics.
- Prepaid-card depth.
- Manual reward-point accounting.

## Missing Check

Likely missing from current understanding:

- Vietnamese-language terminology validation.
- Trust behavior between partners.
- Manual-work tolerance for frequent small card purchases.
- How users distinguish issuer-confirmed truth from household-estimated truth.
- Subscription and card-on-file behavior.
- Payment status lag between source bank and card issuer.

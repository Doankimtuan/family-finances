# Reality Validation

## Product Setup

Reflects real behavior: Yes. Households need provider, amount, term, rate, and maturity.

Too complicated: Legal owner and provider rules may feel heavy for small deposits.

Missing: Purpose reference, legal depositor, and product evidence.

Will users use it: Yes if progressive.

5-10 year survival: Yes.

## Funding

Reflects real behavior: Yes. Salary and bonus funding are central.

Too complicated: No.

Missing: Pending, failed, and reversed funding.

Will users use it: Yes.

5-10 year survival: Yes.

## Active Holding

Reflects real behavior: Yes, especially for checking principal, maturity, and safety.

Too complicated: Provider risk and insurance may be advanced.

Missing: Liquid versus locked context.

Will users use it: Yes if presented as household safety, not finance jargon.

5-10 year survival: Yes.

## Interest

Reflects real behavior: Yes, but users care more about "how much will we get" than formulas.

Too complicated: Compounding detail can be hidden unless needed.

Missing: Provider-confirmed posted interest versus estimate.

Will users use it: Yes around maturity and comparison.

5-10 year survival: Yes.

## Maturity

Reflects real behavior: Very strongly.

Too complicated: No. This is one of the best-fit capabilities.

Missing: Grace periods and passive provider auto-renewal behavior.

Will users use it: Yes if Inbox stays decision-focused.

5-10 year survival: Yes.

## Settlement

Reflects real behavior: Yes.

Too complicated: No.

Missing: Pending settlement, failed settlement, partial payout, provider confirmation.

Will users use it: Yes when products mature.

5-10 year survival: Yes.

## Early Withdrawal

Reflects real behavior: Yes, especially emergencies and family support.

Too complicated: Penalty formulas may be too technical.

Missing: Emotional and partner context.

Will users use it: Rarely, but when needed it is critical.

5-10 year survival: Yes.

## Renewal and Switching

Reflects real behavior: Yes.

Too complicated: Recommendations must stay simple.

Missing: Liquidity and provider trust context.

Will users use it: Yes at maturity.

5-10 year survival: Yes.

## Provider Management

Reflects real behavior: Partly. Users do not think in provider catalogs, but they do care which bank holds money.

Too complicated: Yes if exposed too early.

Missing: Trust, insurance, and bank-app reality.

Will users use it: Indirectly.

5-10 year survival: Yes as infrastructure.

## Legal and Compliance

Reflects real behavior: Yes for edge cases; invisible in daily life.

Too complicated: Yes for small accounts.

Missing: Joint/family ownership and inheritance context.

Will users use it: Not daily, but critical in disputes.

5-10 year survival: Yes.

## Notifications and Inbox

Reflects real behavior: Yes if reserved for real decisions.

Too complicated: No.

Missing: Triage for urgency and family impact.

Will users use it: Yes if not noisy.

5-10 year survival: Yes.

## Analytics

Reflects real behavior: Partly. Families value runway and progress more than analytics.

Too complicated: Can become too complex.

Missing: Household-language liquidity insight.

Will users use it: Occasionally.

5-10 year survival: Yes if Health-read-only.

## Reconciliation

Reflects real behavior: Yes, but mainly when something feels wrong.

Too complicated: Advanced.

Missing: Provider statement evidence.

Will users use it: Rarely, but trust-critical.

5-10 year survival: Yes.

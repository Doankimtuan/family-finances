# Target Users

## Primary Users

Primary users are young adults and couples aged 20-35 who are trying to understand whether their household finances are stable.

They may have:

- Monthly salary income.
- Irregular freelance or business income.
- Shared rent, bills, loans, and family support.
- Several bank accounts, cards, wallets, and cash pockets.
- Limited formal financial planning experience.
- Strong emotional need for reassurance and control.

## Secondary Users

Secondary users include:

- A partner who does not manage daily tracking but wants shared visibility.
- A spouse who manages bills while the other manages income or savings.
- A single person preparing for marriage or shared finance.
- A young parent managing financial pressure for children.
- A household member supporting parents or siblings.

## Household Situations

| Situation | Validation relevance |
| --- | --- |
| Single | Financial Health helps build self-awareness before shared obligations. |
| Dating / cohabiting | Shared expenses begin before formal household rules are clear. |
| Newly married | Partners need a shared language for condition and pressure. |
| Young family | Child, healthcare, rent, and family-support costs increase uncertainty. |
| Family with children | Recurring school, medical, childcare, and future goals make resilience more important. |
| Family supporting relatives | Outflows may be emotionally required but financially invisible. |
| Variable-income household | Health must be understood as condition under uncertainty, not a fixed judgment. |

## Financial Maturity Levels

| Level | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Low maturity | Checks bank balance, remembers bills mentally, avoids details under stress. |
| Emerging maturity | Tracks some spending, knows main obligations, wants simple reassurance. |
| Shared maturity | Partners discuss goals, debts, and safe spending more regularly. |
| Advanced household maturity | Compares months, plans for shocks, and understands trade-offs across domains. |

## Life Stages

- First job and independent spending.
- First shared rent or cohabitation.
- Marriage preparation.
- Newly married household setup.
- Pregnancy or childbirth.
- Raising young children.
- Buying a motorbike, car, or home.
- Supporting parents.
- Building long-term emergency and retirement confidence.

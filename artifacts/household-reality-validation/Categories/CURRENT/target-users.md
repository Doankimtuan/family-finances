# Target Users

## Primary Users

Primary users are young Vietnamese households aged 20-35 who share at least part of their financial life with a partner.

They may:

- Review spending together.
- Split responsibilities for daily purchases.
- Use multiple payment methods.
- Track money loosely through bank notifications, wallet history, notes, chat messages, or memory.
- Need shared language for "what this money was for."

## Secondary Users

Secondary users include:

- Single people preparing for marriage or shared living.
- One partner who manages household records more actively.
- A partner who spends often but reviews less often.
- Family members whose transfers, support, gifts, or reimbursements affect category interpretation.
- Future children or dependents whose needs create new category meaning.

## Household Situations

## Single

Single users may use categories to understand personal habits before shared finance begins. Category language is usually personal and informal.

## Dating Or Cohabiting

Partners may share rent, food, travel, gifts, and household purchases without fully merging accounts. Categories help explain shared versus personal spending.

## Newly Married

Newly married couples often begin discussing recurring obligations, family support, savings, and household norms. Categories become a shared vocabulary.

## Young Family

Young families add baby care, health, education, childcare, groceries, housing, transport, and insurance pressure. Category consistency becomes more important.

## Family With Children

Categories may expand around school, medical care, activities, family travel, clothes, tutoring, and gifts. Long-term comparability becomes more valuable.

## Financial Maturity Levels

## Low Maturity

Users rely on memory, bank balances, and recent notifications. They understand broad labels but may not maintain detailed categories.

## Medium Maturity

Users review transactions periodically, recognize recurring patterns, and want clear spending summaries.

## High Maturity

Users care about category consistency, historical reports, reimbursements, transfers, business expenses, and partner accountability.

## Life Stages

- First job and salary management.
- Moving out or renting together.
- Wedding preparation.
- Marriage and shared household setup.
- Pregnancy and baby expenses.
- Childcare and education.
- Buying motorbike, car, or home.
- Supporting parents or relatives.
- Growing income and more complex accounts.

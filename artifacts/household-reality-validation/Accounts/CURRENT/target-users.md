# Target Users

## Primary Users

Primary users are young Vietnamese partners who share some or all household financial responsibility.

Common traits:

- Age 20-35.
- Salaried, freelance, small-business, or mixed-income.
- Mobile-bank and e-wallet users.
- Comfortable with transfers and QR payments.
- Often still building financial routines.
- May not have formal joint accounts.
- Need shared visibility without turning household money into a corporate process.

## Secondary Users

Secondary users include:

- Less-active partners who need quick confidence, not detailed bookkeeping.
- Spouses who contribute income but do not manage daily records.
- Partners who pay bills from personal accounts.
- Relatives who affect money flows through support, gifts, loans, or informal custody.
- Future children whose expenses change household account usage.

## Household Situations

Single:

- Tracks salary account, cash, wallets, savings, and personal obligations.
- May use the domain before partnership to build habits.

Dating or committed partners:

- May split rent, food, trips, or shared purchases.
- Personal accounts remain legally separate.
- Trust depends on visibility and fairness.

Newly married:

- Money practices are being negotiated.
- One partner may become the default operator.
- The household starts asking "what do we have together?"

Young family:

- More recurring obligations appear.
- Cash, bank transfer, school, healthcare, family support, and emergency funds become more important.
- Account clarity becomes less optional.

Family with children:

- Multiple spending channels increase.
- A single real position matters because expenses become less predictable.
- Long-term saving and liquidity concerns grow.

## Financial Maturity Levels

Low maturity:

- Knows balances by checking apps.
- Tracks little or nothing manually.
- Needs simple labels and low effort.

Developing maturity:

- Tracks main expenses and account totals.
- Understands cash versus bank money.
- May still confuse credit availability with affordability.

High maturity:

- Reconciles records.
- Separates liquidity, savings, card obligations, and household plans.
- Notices stale balances and transfer duplication.

## Life Stages

- First job and independent living.
- Moving in together.
- Marriage planning.
- Childbirth and childcare.
- First major asset purchase.
- Supporting parents.
- Career growth and income diversification.
- Mortgage, car loan, education, or investment planning.

# Real-Life Scenarios

## Best Case

Scenario:

A newly married couple keeps one salary account, one shared spending account, one cash wallet, one e-wallet, and one savings account. Both partners know these containers and update major transactions.

User expectation:

- The total real position feels close to bank and cash reality.
- Each account label is recognizable.
- Planning starts from a trusted money number.

Successful outcome:

- Both partners can explain where household money is.
- No one confuses account balance with jar allocation.

## Typical Case

Scenario:

One partner tracks the main bank account and cash. The other partner sometimes pays groceries or delivery from an e-wallet. Some transactions are missed until later.

User expectation:

- The system should still provide useful orientation.
- Small differences are acceptable if major money is clear.

Successful outcome:

- The household knows the main real position.
- Discrepancies are explainable without conflict.

## Worst Case

Scenario:

Money is split across many bank accounts, wallets, credit cards, cash, and family transfers. One partner has weak visibility. Several records are stale.

User expectation:

- Users expect the truth to be hard but still want a way to understand it.
- The less-active partner expects transparency.

Successful outcome:

- The household identifies which containers are real and which numbers are unreliable.
- Trust is not further damaged by confusing real money with plans or credit.

## Unexpected Case

Scenario:

A QR transfer is sent to the wrong recipient, a wallet payment fails but later reverses, or a card authorization disappears.

User expectation:

- The household expects account reality to eventually match the external provider.
- The record should not make a temporary event look permanent.

Successful outcome:

- The household can recognize the account affected.
- The final money position can be understood after the external event settles.

## Emergency Case

Scenario:

A medical expense, job loss, family emergency, or urgent travel need forces the household to ask how much money is usable immediately.

User expectation:

- They need real money clarity quickly.
- They care less about categories and more about location, liquidity, and confidence.

Successful outcome:

- The household can identify cash, bank funds, wallet funds, savings constraints, and credit obligations without mixing them together.

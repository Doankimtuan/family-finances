# Behavior Analysis

## Common Habits

- Checking bank apps before large expenses.
- Keeping small cash outside formal records.
- Using e-wallets for convenience and promotions.
- Treating salary account as the default source of truth.
- Naming accounts by use or bank nickname.
- Letting one partner operate finances while the other reviews occasionally.
- Mentally earmarking money even before using jars or formal plans.

## Decision Patterns

- Users ask "which account has enough?" before "which category is this?"
- Users trust institution apps most when numbers conflict.
- Users simplify under stress to total cash-like money.
- Users consider personal accounts household-relevant when those accounts pay shared expenses.
- Users may exclude private discretionary money even in a shared household.

## Typical Mistakes

- Forgetting cash spending.
- Treating credit limit as available money.
- Counting a transfer as new income.
- Ignoring pending transactions.
- Leaving unused accounts in totals.
- Recording an expense against the wrong account.
- Assuming a savings product is as liquid as a bank account.
- Ignoring wallet balances until they are needed.

## Financial Awareness

Low-awareness users understand "money in bank" and "cash" but may not understand balance types, liquidity, or card debt.

Medium-awareness users understand accounts and transfers but may still struggle with reconciliation and credit-card timing.

High-awareness users understand institution balances, statement cycles, liquidity, and household net position.

## Emotional Factors

- Account totals can create calm or panic quickly.
- Hidden or forgotten accounts can trigger suspicion.
- Wrong balances can feel like personal failure.
- Seeing all money in one place can create relief.
- Credit-card and loan-adjacent accounts can create shame or avoidance.
- Reconciliation can feel satisfying when discrepancies are explained.

## Manual Work Users Accept

- Adding main bank accounts.
- Naming accounts.
- Updating major cash movements.
- Checking balances before important decisions.
- Correcting obvious mistakes.
- Reviewing account totals monthly.

## Manual Work Users Hate

- Recording every tiny cash purchase.
- Maintaining many small wallet balances.
- Re-entering data already visible in a bank app.
- Resolving duplicates caused by imports or transfers.
- Classifying account metadata that has no obvious household value.
- Rechecking balances when nothing changed.

# Decision Points

## Create

Users decide whether a future purpose is important enough to track.

Why it matters:

- Creation turns a vague wish into a shared household intention.
- Too many creations can dilute attention.

## Name

Users decide how to describe the goal.

Why it matters:

- Naming carries emotional meaning and helps partners align.
- Generic names can reduce motivation.

## Set Target Amount

Users estimate how much the goal needs.

Why it matters:

- The amount shapes perceived feasibility.
- Wrong estimates can create false confidence or discouragement.

## Set Target Date

Users decide whether the goal has timing pressure.

Why it matters:

- Deadlines change urgency.
- Open-ended goals behave differently from event-driven goals.

## Contribute

Users decide whether to count money toward a goal.

Why it matters:

- Contribution creates perceived progress.
- It may or may not correspond to a real money movement.

## Edit

Users revise name, target, date, or status as reality changes.

Why it matters:

- Editing keeps the goal aligned with life.
- Unexplained edits can affect partner trust.

## Pause

Users decide to stop pursuing a goal temporarily.

Why it matters:

- Pause preserves the intention without pretending progress continues.
- It often reflects stress, debt, emergency, or priority change.

## Resume

Users decide the household can pursue the goal again.

Why it matters:

- Resume signals regained capacity or renewed commitment.

## Complete

Users decide the goal has reached its intended state.

Why it matters:

- Completion can mean funded, achieved, purchased, or no longer needed depending on household interpretation.
- Ambiguous completion can mislead future review.

## Cancel

Users decide the goal should no longer be pursued.

Why it matters:

- Cancellation may be practical but emotionally sensitive.
- It can represent changed priorities, not failure.

## Review

Users decide whether current progress still reflects reality.

Why it matters:

- Review protects trust.
- Review reveals stale targets, duplicated money, and unrealistic deadlines.

## Approve Or Reject Shared Meaning

Partners decide whether a goal is truly household-owned.

Why it matters:

- Shared goals affect fairness, visibility, and trade-offs.
- Personal goals may need different expectations than household goals.

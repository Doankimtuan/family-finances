# Cross-Domain Impact

## Accounts

Transactions explain why account balances changed. Accounts provide the real container affected by each transaction.

## Cards

Card transactions create purchases, refunds, statement obligations, and later card payments. Households can confuse card purchases with repayment transactions.

## Savings

Savings deposits, withdrawals, maturity payouts, and interest may appear as transactions when real money moves.

## Loans

Loan disbursements, repayments, fees, and interest-related payments interact with transaction history. The loan schedule itself is separate from the transaction event.

## Goals

Goals may be affected by transactions when money is saved, spent, or redirected. A goal is an intention; the transaction is the real event.

## Planning

Planning consumes transaction facts to compare real activity with household intention. Planning does not own transaction truth.

## Inbox

Inbox naturally receives transactions that need household decision, such as unmapped expenses, income placement, unfamiliar activity, or items needing review.

## Health

Health interprets transaction patterns as signals. It should depend on transaction evidence without replacing the factual record.

## Categories

Categories give household meaning to transactions. Category meaning can change without changing the original money movement.

## Together

Shared finance requires partner visibility, review, questions, and agreement. Transactions can support trust or create tension depending on sensitivity and interpretation.

## Integrations

Banks, wallets, card issuers, and third-party providers may provide read-only transaction information. Provider data can improve completeness but introduces delay, mismatch, and duplicate risk.

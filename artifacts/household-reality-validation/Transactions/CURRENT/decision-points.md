# Decision Points

## Record

Users decide whether to record a transaction at all.

Why it matters: unrecorded activity weakens household memory, especially for cash and informal transfers.

## Review

Users decide whether a transaction is understood.

Why it matters: unresolved activity creates uncertainty and later disagreement.

## Categorize

Users decide what the transaction was for.

Why it matters: category meaning drives household reflection and later planning interpretation.

## Map To Planning Intent

Users decide whether a transaction belongs to a household jar, goal, bill, or other virtual purpose.

Why it matters: mapping connects real events to household intention but must not be confused with real money movement.

## Ask Partner

Users decide whether to question another person about a transaction.

Why it matters: this can create clarity or emotional tension depending on tone, timing, and sensitivity.

## Correct

Users decide whether the record is wrong enough to clarify.

Why it matters: correction protects trust when amount, date, account, category, or note does not match reality.

## Refund Recognition

Users decide whether incoming money relates to a prior expense.

Why it matters: treating refunds as ordinary income distorts the household story.

## Transfer Interpretation

Users decide whether movement between people or accounts is neutral, shared expense, support, reimbursement, loan, or income.

Why it matters: transfers are financially simple but socially meaningful.

## Ignore

Users decide to leave a small or unclear transaction unresolved.

Why it matters: ignoring low-stakes items is normal, but too much ignored activity lowers trust.

## Reconcile

Users decide whether to compare records against bank, wallet, card, cash, or receipt evidence.

Why it matters: reconciliation restores confidence after doubt, month-end review, or major financial events.

## Approve Or Reject Imported Meaning

Users may decide whether suggested meaning from a system, partner, or provider is correct.

Why it matters: household meaning often differs from merchant or provider classification.

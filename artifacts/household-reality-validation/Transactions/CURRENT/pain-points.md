# Pain Points

## Current Frustrations

- Money moves through too many places: bank, wallet, card, cash, partner, and family.
- Provider descriptions are hard to understand.
- Cash spending disappears from memory.
- Refunds and cancellations are delayed.
- Small expenses add up invisibly.
- Card payments confuse actual spending.
- Shared expenses and personal expenses blur.
- Users remember merchants, not formal categories.

## Confusing Concepts

- Transfer versus expense.
- Refund versus income.
- Reimbursement versus salary.
- Card purchase versus card bill payment.
- Cash withdrawal versus cash spending.
- Pending authorization versus final transaction.
- Category versus planning intent.
- Personal spending versus shared household spending.

## Unnecessary Complexity

Complexity appears unnecessary when:

- Users must classify every low-value transaction.
- Users must use formal accounting language.
- Users must understand provider settlement mechanics for ordinary review.
- Users must distinguish too many statuses for low-risk activity.
- Users must maintain perfect transaction history to gain basic value.

## Missing Visibility

Households often lack visibility into:

- Small cash expenses.
- Partner-paid recurring bills.
- Subscription renewals.
- Refund arrival.
- Wallet promotions, fees, and discounts.
- Transfers with unclear purpose.
- Spending across multiple accounts and providers.
- Whether an item is already reviewed or still uncertain.

## Potential Misunderstandings

- "If it is in the transaction list, it must be final."
- "A refund is extra income."
- "A transfer to my own wallet is spending."
- "A credit card payment is another expense."
- "The app balance is wrong because one cash transaction is missing."
- "Categorization changes the real money."
- "My partner is monitoring me rather than helping manage the household."

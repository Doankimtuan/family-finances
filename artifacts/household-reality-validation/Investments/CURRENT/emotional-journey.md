# Emotional Journey

## Beginning

Common emotions:

- Curiosity.
- Optimism.
- Fear of missing out.
- Pride at becoming "financially grown up."
- Anxiety about losing money.

Trust is gained when:

- The household agrees what money is safe to invest.
- The user understands that investment value is uncertain.
- Partners are honest about who controls the asset.

Trust is lost when:

- A partner invests shared money without discussion.
- The investment is described as safe when it is not.
- The first record is vague or exaggerated.

## Holding Period

Common emotions:

- Confidence during gains.
- Stress during market drops.
- Boredom during long-term holding.
- Temptation to compare with friends.
- Avoidance when performance is poor.

Trust is gained when:

- Values are explained with date and source.
- Losses are acknowledged early.
- Household cash needs remain protected.

Trust is lost when:

- Market value is presented as spendable cash.
- One partner hides losses.
- Records become stale and nobody knows what is true.

## Review

Common emotions:

- Relief when the story is clear.
- Tension if partners disagree on risk.
- Regret over timing.
- Achievement if investments support a long-term goal.
- Uncertainty when provider or family information is incomplete.

Trust is gained when:

- The household can separate facts from opinions.
- Users can explain contribution, current value, liquidity, and risk in ordinary language.

Trust is lost when:

- Review exposes previously hidden decisions.
- Numbers cannot be traced to any source.
- Investment performance is blamed without context.

## Exit

Common emotions:

- Relief when cash is needed and available.
- Satisfaction after realized gain.
- Regret after realized loss.
- Anxiety over whether selling was too early.
- Closure when a confusing asset is finally resolved.

Trust is gained when:

- Sale or redemption proceeds reconcile with the household's cash picture.
- Fees, delay, and loss are not hidden.

Trust is lost when:

- Expected value does not match actual proceeds.
- Exit takes longer than the household assumed.
- A partner discovers that money was less liquid than promised.

# Behavior Analysis

## Common Habits

- Users check investment values inside provider apps, not in household finance records.
- Users remember invested principal better than units, cost basis, or fees.
- Users discuss gains more readily than losses.
- Users often track gold by purchase receipt, family memory, or shop quote.
- Users may invest irregularly after salary, bonus, Tet money, or peer influence.
- Users may stop tracking when the market is down.
- Users treat fund investing as calmer and stock/crypto investing as more active.

## Decision Patterns

- Social proof strongly influences first investment.
- Higher income increases willingness to invest.
- Marriage and children often reduce appetite for speculation.
- Emergency needs override long-term investment intention.
- Trust determines whether investment information is shared.
- Losses cause avoidance, defensiveness, or sudden liquidation.
- Gains can create overconfidence.

## Typical Mistakes

- Counting transferred cash as both bank money and investment money.
- Counting market value as available cash.
- Treating unrealized gains as spendable.
- Ignoring settlement timing.
- Forgetting fees, tax, and buy/sell spread.
- Assuming bond or insurance-linked products are as safe as bank deposits.
- Confusing fund NAV changes with bank interest.
- Using investment money for short-term goals without acknowledging risk.
- Hiding investment losses until a household decision exposes them.

## Financial Awareness

Low awareness users:

- Understand "I put in 10 million" and "now it says 11 million."
- Do not understand realized versus unrealized gain.
- Need plain language around risk and liquidity.

Medium awareness users:

- Understand stocks, funds, gold, and deposits as different.
- Still rely on app totals more than formal records.
- May not understand tax, settlement, or corporate actions.

High awareness users:

- Understand asset allocation, cost basis, dividends, and liquidity.
- May expect more detail than a household partner needs.
- Can overcomplicate shared financial conversations.

## Emotional Factors

- Pride when investments rise.
- Shame or secrecy when investments fall.
- Anxiety when household cash is low but investments are illiquid.
- Friction when partners have different risk tolerance.
- Relief when long-term money is clearly separated from daily money.
- Regret after selling too early, buying too late, or following bad advice.

## Manual Work Users Accept

- Recording a broad investment account or holding.
- Updating occasional manual values for gold or informal assets.
- Confirming contribution amount.
- Reviewing a major sale, withdrawal, or loss.
- Adding notes for family investments or unclear assets.

## Manual Work Users Hate

- Entering every trade manually.
- Updating daily market prices.
- Reconstructing corporate actions.
- Calculating tax, fees, and return formulas.
- Splitting small dividend or fund events into complex categories.
- Maintaining data that already exists in broker or fund apps.

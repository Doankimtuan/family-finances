# Target Users

## Primary Users

Young partners managing shared finance:

- Age 20-35.
- Dating seriously, living together, newly married, or raising young children.
- Vietnam first.
- Usually manage money through individual bank accounts, e-wallets, QR transfers, cash, and informal partner agreements.
- Need shared visibility without turning the relationship into surveillance.

Common primary-user types:

- Daily money manager: tracks bills, transfers, recurring costs, and balances.
- Less-engaged partner: wants clarity and trust without handling every detail.
- Household steward: sets routines and keeps the family financially organized.
- Equal co-manager: both partners actively review and decide.

## Secondary Users

Secondary users influence household reality even when they may not use the product:

- Parents receiving support.
- Parents providing help for rent, wedding, childcare, house purchase, or emergency.
- Siblings involved in family obligations.
- Children whose needs change planning and spending.
- Employers and clients whose payment timing affects household rhythm.
- Banks, e-wallets, merchants, and billers that create financial evidence.

## Household Situations

Single:

- User may prepare for future shared finance.
- Together is less urgent unless the user expects partnership or family support.

New couple:

- Shared finance may begin with rent, food, dates, subscriptions, or travel.
- Boundaries between personal and shared money are still fluid.

Newly married:

- More likely to discuss salary, savings, debt, family obligations, and monthly contribution rules.
- Household identity becomes more concrete.

Young family:

- Childcare, rent, medical costs, insurance, and savings needs make shared visibility more important.
- One partner may become the main operator due to time pressure.

Family with children:

- Long-term planning becomes more serious.
- Household finance often includes school, healthcare, grandparents, home, and car decisions.

## Financial Maturity Levels

Low maturity:

- Uses memory, chat messages, screenshots, and ad hoc transfers.
- Needs basic shared understanding.

Medium maturity:

- Has recurring bills, savings goals, and informal contribution agreements.
- Wants predictable partner visibility.

High maturity:

- Reviews monthly patterns, manages multiple accounts, and understands tradeoffs.
- Still needs trust, attribution, and shared assumptions.

## Life Stages

- First job or early career.
- Moving in together.
- Engagement or wedding planning.
- Newly married.
- First child.
- Career growth.
- Supporting parents.
- Buying vehicle or home.
- Handling debt, illness, or income disruption.

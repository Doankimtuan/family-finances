# Cross-Domain Impact

This document describes natural interactions only. It does not redesign boundaries.

## Accounts

Planning depends on account context to understand real money position. Accounts provide where money actually is. Planning provides what money is intended to do.

Interaction risk: planned jars may be mistaken for account balances.

## Transactions

Transactions provide evidence of actual income, expense, transfers, refunds, and corrections. Planning uses transaction facts to understand whether expectations matched reality.

Interaction risk: expected recurring activity may be mistaken for completed transaction activity.

## Goals

Goals are part of Planning when they express intended future outcomes. They may interact with Savings when real products hold money for the goal.

Interaction risk: goal progress may be mistaken for confirmed savings product balance.

## Categories

Categories explain spending meaning. Planning may use category information to understand purpose and variance.

Interaction risk: category labels may be confused with planning jars.

## Inbox

Inbox is naturally involved when a planning-related item requires a discrete household decision, clarification, or review.

Interaction risk: users may not distinguish routine planning maintenance from a decision that needs Inbox attention.

## Health

Health reads planning and real money facts to produce interpretation. Planning can influence Health context, but Health does not own planning intention.

Interaction risk: Health interpretations may feel like financial advice if phrased too strongly.

## Together

Together governs household membership, shared rights, and partner-visible policies. Planning depends on shared context and agreement.

Interaction risk: shared visibility may create tension if privacy expectations are unclear.

## Cards

Cards provide statement, due date, credit limit, and repayment pressure. Planning may consider upcoming payment pressure.

Interaction risk: credit limit may be mistaken for usable household money.

## Loans

Loans provide contract and repayment truth. Planning may consider expected repayment pressure and future affordability.

Interaction risk: planned payoff intention may be mistaken for loan balance truth.

## Savings

Savings provides real product truth such as balance, rate, term, maturity, renewal, and withdrawal state. Planning may express purpose and intention.

Interaction risk: a savings goal may be mistaken for a real savings product, or vice versa.

## External Providers

Banks, wallets, billers, and other providers may provide facts or evidence. Planning may read or interpret those facts.

Interaction risk: provider delays or missing data may make planning assumptions look confirmed.

# Pain Points

## Current Frustrations

- Bank balance looks safe but committed money is hidden.
- Partners remember different obligations.
- Salary timing does not match bill timing.
- Cash and wallet spending are scattered.
- Irregular income breaks simple monthly plans.
- Family support appears suddenly.
- Seasonal costs such as Tet, school, travel, weddings, or insurance distort the month.
- Goals are easy to raid during stress.
- Review happens too late to prevent anxiety.

## Confusing Concepts

- Jar versus account.
- Planned amount versus real balance.
- Goal progress versus savings product balance.
- Expected bill versus paid bill.
- Reallocation versus real transfer.
- Recurring rule versus actual transaction.
- Calendar pressure versus obligation truth.
- Health insight versus advice.

## Unnecessary Complexity

- Too many jars or categories.
- Formal budgeting terms that users do not use at home.
- Detailed variance analysis for users who only need practical clarity.
- Rollover logic before the household understands planned versus real.
- Long-term scenario modeling before basic monthly rhythm is trusted.
- Treating every small expense as equally important.

## Missing Visibility

- Upcoming pressure before payday.
- Different partner salary dates.
- Irregular income confidence.
- Seasonal or annual obligations.
- Family-support commitments.
- Cash gaps.
- Stale assumptions.
- Whether a planned item has become a real transaction.
- Whether a goal is protected by real savings or only intended.

## Potential Misunderstandings

- Users may believe a planned jar holds actual money.
- Users may think automatic allocation means automatic transfer.
- Users may treat expected income as confirmed.
- Users may assume recurring bills are paid because they appear in the plan.
- Users may blame a partner when the real problem is missing or late information.
- Users may interpret planning warnings as judgment.

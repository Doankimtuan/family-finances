# Target Users

## Primary Users

Young Vietnamese households age 20-35 who have one or more active borrowing obligations or are preparing to take one.

Primary users usually need to answer:

- How much do we still owe?
- What is due this month?
- Can our salary cover repayment safely?
- Did we already pay?
- Will this loan affect shared plans?

## Secondary Users

- Partner who does not manage the loan but is affected by repayment pressure.
- Family member who lends money, receives repayment, or provides support.
- Co-borrower or guarantor.
- User without a current loan who wants to understand future borrowing capacity.
- Household member reviewing past loans for long-term planning.

## Household Situations

Single:

- May borrow for phone, motorbike, education, relocation, medical cost, or emergency.
- Often tracks alone and may tolerate simple manual tracking.

Dating / partially shared finances:

- Loan may remain personal but affect shared plans.
- Disclosure can be sensitive if one partner has hidden obligations.

Newly married:

- Existing individual loans become household-relevant.
- Couples start coordinating salary, rent, savings, and repayment.

Young family:

- Loan pressure competes with childcare, rent, groceries, school fees, insurance, and parental support.
- Reliability and due-date awareness become more important.

Family with children:

- Loans may shift from consumer purchases to car, home, education, or family emergency needs.
- Long-term affordability matters more than short-term access.

## Financial Maturity Levels

Low maturity:

- Understands monthly payment but not total repayment.
- May confuse loan payment with ordinary expense.
- May not know interest type or fee rules.

Medium maturity:

- Tracks lender, remaining amount, due date, and payment source.
- Understands that rate changes and late fees matter.
- Still may rely on provider app or reminders.

High maturity:

- Reviews amortization, early payoff, floating-rate exposure, and refinancing pressure.
- Cares about evidence, statements, and partner alignment.

## Life Stages

- First salary and first financed purchase.
- Moving out or renting.
- Marriage planning.
- New child or medical expense.
- Buying motorbike or car.
- Buying apartment or house.
- Supporting parents.
- Growing income and considering early payoff.

# Lifecycle

## Lifecycle Overview

```text
Recognized
  -> Active
  -> Under Review
  -> Exited
  -> Archived
```

Alternative paths:

```text
Recognized -> Cancelled -> Archived
Active -> Impaired -> Exited -> Archived
Active -> Transferred Out -> Archived
Under Review -> Active
Under Review -> Corrected -> Active / Exited / Archived
```

## Beginning

The lifecycle begins when a household recognizes that an asset is an investment holding.

Common beginnings:

- Cash was contributed to buy a stock, fund, bond, gold holding, crypto holding, private/family investment, or insurance-linked investment.
- A partner discloses an existing holding.
- A household receives an investment as gift, inheritance, employer benefit, or family arrangement.
- A previously ambiguous asset is reclassified as investment.

Required business facts at beginning:

- Holding identity is known enough for household recognition.
- Broad asset class is known or explicitly unknown.
- Ownership/visibility context is known or explicitly uncertain.
- Contribution amount is known, estimated, or marked unknown.
- Current value is known, estimated, or marked unknown with valuation date/source when available.

## Normal Operation

During normal operation:

- Holding remains owned or considered household-relevant.
- Estimated value may change without cash moving.
- Valuation date/source may become stale.
- Contributions may increase exposure.
- Partial exits may realize part of the position.
- Investment income may occur through dividends, coupons, or distributions.
- Household may review liquidity, risk, and visibility context.

Normal operation never:

- Posts market value changes as cash.
- Creates planning capacity from unrealized value.
- Generates investment recommendations.
- Executes trades automatically.

## Changes

Changes may include:

- Holding name or classification correction.
- Contribution update.
- Manual valuation update.
- Valuation source update.
- Liquidity context update.
- Risk context update.
- Ownership/visibility context update.
- Household purpose note update.
- Reclassification between investment and another domain after review.

Every change must preserve the difference between factual cash movement and value interpretation.

## Completion

Completion occurs when the investment holding no longer remains active because it has fully exited.

Completion examples:

- Full sale.
- Full redemption.
- Bond repayment.
- Insurance-linked surrender or maturity.
- Private investment repayment.
- Gold sale.
- Write-off after confirmed loss or no recoverable value.

Completion result:

- Realized outcome becomes known or marked uncertain.
- Active exposure ends.
- History remains available.

## Termination

Termination means the active business lifecycle ends.

Terminal states:

- Cancelled.
- Exited.
- Written Off.
- Transferred Out.
- Archived.

Archived means historical memory remains but no active investment behavior continues.

## Recovery

Recovery occurs when missing, stale, incorrect, disputed, or uncertain investment information becomes clear enough to continue.

Recovery examples:

- Missing statement found.
- Partner clarifies ownership.
- Manual value corrected.
- Provider confirms redemption.
- Family-held investment is repaid, written down, or reclassified.
- Mistaken classification is corrected.

## Exceptional Situations

Exceptional situations include:

- Failed purchase.
- Failed redemption.
- Stale value.
- Unknown contribution.
- Wrong asset type.
- Partner conflict.
- Margin/leverage exposure.
- Scam or fraud suspicion.
- Provider outage.
- Family dispute.
- Market crash.
- Emergency cash need.

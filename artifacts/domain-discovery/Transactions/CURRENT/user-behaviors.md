# User Behaviors

## Common Behaviors

- Check recent activity after making a payment.
- Remember large transactions and forget small frequent expenses.
- Use transaction descriptions as memory cues.
- Search for a merchant when a balance feels lower than expected.
- Ask a partner what an unfamiliar transaction was.
- Delay categorization until the end of the week or month.
- Treat cash withdrawals as "spent" even when the cash is still partly unused.
- Treat transfers to a spouse as either support, shared spending, or neutral movement depending on household norms.
- Review salary, bills, rent, school, and loan payments more carefully than small daily purchases.

## Expectations

- The latest important activity should be easy to scan.
- Amount direction should be unmistakable.
- The account or wallet involved should be visible.
- Familiar merchants should be recognizable.
- Household members should see the same factual record.
- Corrections should not make money history feel untrustworthy.
- Search should find notes, merchants, categories, accounts, and remembered words.

## Frustrations

- Provider descriptions are cryptic or inconsistent.
- Cash transactions are missing.
- Bank or wallet notifications disappear before review.
- Refunds arrive days later and are hard to connect to the original payment.
- Transfers between own accounts can be mistaken for expenses.
- Recurring subscriptions surprise the household.
- One partner may feel blamed when every transaction is surfaced without context.
- Categorization can become tedious when many transactions are similar.

## Mistakes

- Double-counting credit card payments and card purchases.
- Recording the same manual transaction twice.
- Forgetting fees, tips, shipping, foreign exchange differences, or wallet discounts.
- Recording transfer support to family as ordinary spending without context.
- Misclassifying income reimbursements as salary.
- Treating a pending authorization as final spending.
- Backdating based on memory rather than provider evidence.

## Decision Patterns

- Small expenses are often categorized quickly or ignored.
- Large expenses are reviewed for correctness and household impact.
- Unexpected transactions trigger investigation.
- End-of-month review often prioritizes "where did money go?" over formal accounting accuracy.
- Couples negotiate meaning: one transaction may be "groceries" to one person and "family support" to another.

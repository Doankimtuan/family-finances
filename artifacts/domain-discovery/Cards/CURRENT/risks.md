# Risks

## Business Risks

- Treating cards as ordinary accounts can distort household financial reality.
- Collapsing debit and credit cards into one model can hide debt.
- Ignoring card billing cycles can produce incorrect cash-flow expectations.
- Provider terms vary by issuer, card product, and market.
- Card network, issuer, and regulatory rules can change.
- Credit-card installments can overlap with Loan or Debt concepts.

## Financial Risks

- Users may treat credit limit as spendable household money.
- Minimum payment may be mistaken for responsible full repayment.
- Late payment can trigger fees, interest, and credit consequences.
- Cash advances can be materially more expensive than purchases.
- FX markup and delayed posting can change final cost.
- Refund timing can leave a payment obligation still due.
- Promotional installments can obscure total cost.
- Overdue card debt can become long-term high-interest debt.

## UX Risks

- Users may not understand the difference between statement balance, outstanding balance, current due, and available credit.
- Statement cycles can feel abstract compared with transaction dates.
- Card repayment can look like a second expense if original card spending was already recorded.
- Partial payments, refunds, and disputes can make the visible balance feel untrustworthy.
- Supplementary-card spending can create household tension if attribution is unclear.

## Misunderstanding Risks

- Debit card, ATM card, credit card, and prepaid card may be used interchangeably in casual speech.
- "Balance" is ambiguous for cards.
- "Paid" can mean user initiated transfer, bank debited source account, or issuer posted payment.
- "Refunded" can mean merchant approved, acquirer processed, issuer posted, or statement adjusted.
- Cashback can be understood as discount, income, credit, or reward depending on timing.

## Future Scalability Risks

- Multi-card households increase reconciliation complexity.
- Provider integrations may produce inconsistent status models.
- International expansion requires country-specific terminology, regulation, currency, and dispute rules.
- Tokenized wallets and card-on-file subscriptions make card identity less visible.
- Installment, BNPL, credit line, and card debt boundaries may blur over time.

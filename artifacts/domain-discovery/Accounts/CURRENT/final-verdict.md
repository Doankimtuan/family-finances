# Final Verdict

## Is This Domain Correctly Understood?

Yes, at the discovery level.

Accounts are real-world financial containers and obligation records. Their primary responsibility is to locate money or financial position in reality before planning, categorization, health interpretation, or household decisions occur.

The existing product philosophy already understands the most important principle: real account balances must not be confused with virtual planning allocations.

## Knowledge Gaps

Areas needing validation before design begins:

- Which Vietnamese account labels users naturally understand: payment account, bank account, checking, salary account, wallet, cash, term deposit.
- How often target households keep truly shared accounts versus individually owned accounts used for shared expenses.
- Whether users expect credit cards to appear in an account list, a card list, or both.
- How much e-wallet tracking matters compared with bank and cash tracking.
- Whether households want to track account holder identity or only household relevance.
- How often manual balances become stale in real use.
- How families handle money temporarily held by relatives.
- Whether multi-currency is real for the target segment now or only future.
- What institution metadata is necessary for recognition without creating setup burden.

## Validation Needed Before Design

- Interview young Vietnamese couples about account naming and money-scattering patterns.
- Review actual anonymized household account lists.
- Observe how users reconcile cash, bank apps, and wallet apps.
- Validate whether credit card and savings-product placement creates confusion.
- Validate whether users distinguish current balance, available balance, and planned money.
- Confirm regulatory and provider constraints before any future data integration.

## Confidence Score

Confidence: 0.86

Reason: The core financial model is highly stable and aligns with existing product philosophy. Confidence is reduced by Vietnam-specific behavioral variation, integration uncertainty, and possible terminology ambiguity around payment accounts, e-wallets, credit cards, and savings products.

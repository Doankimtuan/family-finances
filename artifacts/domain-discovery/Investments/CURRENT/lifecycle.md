# Lifecycle

## Beginning

An investment lifecycle begins when the household decides to expose money to an investment asset.

Common beginnings:

- Open a securities, fund, gold, insurance, or other investment account.
- Transfer money from a bank account.
- Buy a security, fund unit, bond, gold, or private asset.
- Receive an investment asset as gift, inheritance, employer benefit, or family arrangement.
- Convert an existing informal asset into a trackable holding.

## Middle

During the holding period, the investment may change without new household cash movement.

Common middle events:

- Market price changes.
- NAV updates.
- Dividends, coupons, or distributions.
- Additional purchases or contributions.
- Partial sale or redemption.
- Fees, charges, taxes, or custody costs.
- Corporate actions such as split, rights issue, merger, delisting, or tender offer.
- Account transfer between brokers or custodians.
- Manual valuation updates.
- Partner review or household discussion.

## End

The lifecycle ends when the household no longer owns the investment holding.

Common endings:

- Full sale.
- Full redemption.
- Maturity or repayment of bond principal.
- Surrender or termination of insurance-linked product.
- Asset transferred out of household ownership.
- Private investment written off.
- Physical asset sold, gifted, lost, or reclassified.

## Possible Exits

- Convert investment back to bank cash.
- Move proceeds to savings product.
- Use proceeds for spending, loan repayment, goal funding, or family support.
- Transfer holdings to another custodian.
- Stop tracking because the household no longer considers the asset relevant.

## Possible Interruptions

- Trading halt.
- Settlement delay.
- Withdrawal restriction.
- Missing statement.
- Provider outage.
- Account lock, KYC issue, or regulatory review.
- Suspended fund redemption.
- Delisting or issuer default.
- Family dispute over informal asset.
- Partner disagreement over risk.

## Possible Recoveries

- Later statement confirms holding.
- Provider data refresh resolves stale value.
- Correction clarifies quantity, cost, price, or account.
- Settlement completes.
- Corporate action proceeds are posted.
- Asset is reclassified after better information.
- Household writes down, writes off, or archives a no-longer-verifiable asset.

# User Behaviors

## Common Behaviors

- Starts investing after seeing friends, colleagues, bank staff, or online communities discuss returns.
- Uses a bank, broker, fund app, or fintech wallet rather than a household finance system.
- Treats invested money as "separate" from monthly spending money.
- Checks investment apps frequently during market volatility and rarely during calm periods.
- Records only cash transfers into or out of investment accounts, not each holding.
- Remembers original contribution more clearly than current allocation or fees.
- Uses simple mental labels such as "stocks", "fund", "gold", "long-term money", or "for the kids."
- One partner may be more risk-taking; the other may focus on capital safety.

## Expectations

- Know how much was put in.
- Know whether the household is up or down.
- Know whether money can be withdrawn quickly.
- Separate long-term investments from emergency cash.
- Avoid accidental double counting between bank cash and portfolio value.
- See investment truth without receiving risky advice.
- Preserve household trust when one partner manages investments.

## Frustrations

- Broker and fund apps show different numbers, formats, and terminology.
- Market value changes every day, making household net worth feel unstable.
- Dividends, rights issues, stock splits, and fund distributions are confusing.
- Family or informal investments lack statements.
- Gold purchase records may be paper-based or missing.
- Currency, fees, and taxes are hard to estimate.
- Investment losses can create partner tension or secrecy.

## Mistakes

- Confusing transfer into a brokerage account with investment profit.
- Counting invested principal as liquid cash.
- Counting both broker cash and bank transfer if the transfer is not reconciled.
- Treating unrealized gain as spendable money.
- Forgetting fees, taxes, and settlement timing.
- Mixing speculation with goal funding without acknowledging risk.
- Recording a dividend as salary-like income without context.
- Ignoring concentration risk because individual holdings are familiar.
- Treating insurance-linked investment surrender value as equal to premiums paid.

## Decision Patterns

- Safety-first: keep most money in deposits or gold; invest small amounts.
- Return-chasing: react to recent market gains or social proof.
- Habit-based: monthly fund contribution after salary.
- Event-based: invest bonus, Tet money, sale proceeds, or leftover cash.
- Goal-protective: avoid investing money needed for tuition, birth, rent deposit, or home purchase soon.
- Partner-negotiated: one partner requests permission or explains before moving a large sum.
- Recovery-driven: pause investing after losses, then restart cautiously.

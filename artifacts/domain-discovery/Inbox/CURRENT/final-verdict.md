# Final Verdict

## Is This Domain Correctly Understood?

Yes, at the discovery level.

Inbox is best understood as the household's decision queue: the place where real-world financial signals become visible, bounded, accountable attention. It does not own money movement, provider truth, transaction truth, savings terms, or planning allocations.

## Knowledge Gaps

Remaining knowledge gaps:

- Actual Vietnamese household tolerance for review volume.
- How often young families process financial decisions together versus individually.
- Which provider channels matter most: bank app, SMS, wallet app, email, invoice, screenshot, or chat.
- Whether payment reminders feel like valid Inbox decisions or generic notifications.
- How households interpret dismissal, acknowledgement, expiration, and auto-resolution.
- How often missing context requires partner conversation.
- Whether invoice and receipt evidence matters for ordinary households or only household-business users.

## Validation Needed Before Design Begins

Before business design begins, validate:

- Real household review cadence and backlog tolerance.
- Top Inbox source events in Vietnam.
- Decision language that users understand without technical explanation.
- Boundary between reminders and decisions.
- Partner accountability expectations.
- Trust level for suggestions and auto-resolution.
- Evidence needs for receipts, e-invoices, and provider messages.

## Confidence Score

Confidence: 0.84

Rationale:

- Strong confidence in the core model of Inbox as unresolved financial attention.
- Strong repository alignment with the decision-queue philosophy.
- Moderate uncertainty around Vietnam-specific provider channels, invoice behavior, and household tolerance for volume.
- Moderate boundary risk where reminders and notifications can blur.

# Final Verdict

## Is This Domain Correctly Understood?

Yes, at discovery level.

The Loans domain is best understood as scheduled borrowing obligations that convert present value into future repayment pressure. Its core truth is not the purchase, not the account balance, and not a generic expense. Its core truth is the household's liability to repay principal, interest, and related charges under real or socially agreed terms.

## Knowledge Gaps

Known gaps before business design:

- Which loan types are most common for the target users of this product.
- How often users track informal family/friend loans versus formal lender loans.
- Whether users have reliable access to contracts, repayment schedules, and provider statements.
- How Vietnamese users understand and name fixed monthly vs reducing-balance repayment.
- Whether rate-change anxiety is common enough to require deeper modeling.
- How often early payoff, restructuring, delinquency, and refinancing occur in the target segment.
- Whether provider integration is realistically available for the lenders users actually use.

## Validation Needed Before Design Begins

- Interview young Vietnamese households with at least one active loan.
- Review real anonymized loan contracts and statements from banks, finance companies, and store financing providers.
- Validate terminology in Vietnamese with target users.
- Validate whether users distinguish remaining principal from outstanding balance.
- Validate current product assumptions against actual payment and rate-change patterns.
- Confirm regulatory interpretation with qualified local legal expertise before making compliance-sensitive claims.

## Confidence Score

Confidence: 0.82

Reason: Core household finance concepts, current product observations, and Vietnam lending patterns are well understood. Confidence is lower for lender-specific fee rules, current regulatory nuance, provider integration availability, and exact target-user prevalence.

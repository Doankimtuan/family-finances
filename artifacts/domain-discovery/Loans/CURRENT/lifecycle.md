# Lifecycle

## Beginning

A loan begins when a household receives money, goods, services, or purchasing power with an obligation to repay later.

Common starting events:

- Loan application or informal request.
- Approval or agreement.
- Contract signing or verbal acceptance.
- Disbursement to borrower, merchant, school, hospital, seller, or another party.
- First schedule or repayment expectation becomes known.

## Middle

During the active period, the loan has an outstanding obligation.

Natural events:

- Scheduled payment becomes due.
- Payment is made.
- Lender posts the payment.
- Principal reduces.
- Interest accrues or is charged.
- Fees may be charged.
- Rate may change.
- Schedule may be revised.
- Household may pay early, partially, late, or ahead of schedule.
- Household may reconcile records against lender statements.

## End

A loan ends when the lender's claim is settled or otherwise extinguished.

End states:

- Fully repaid.
- Early payoff.
- Cancelled before meaningful disbursement.
- Written off or forgiven.
- Refinanced into another obligation.
- Transferred to another lender.
- Defaulted and moved into collections or legal handling.
- Informally forgiven or converted into a gift.

## Possible Exits

- Normal completion.
- Early settlement.
- Refinance.
- Consolidation into another loan.
- Sale or transfer of collateral.
- Restructuring due to financial hardship.
- Dispute or cancellation.

## Possible Interruptions

- Missed payment.
- Partial payment.
- Provider app outage.
- Salary delay.
- Rate change.
- Job loss.
- Medical emergency.
- Family conflict.
- Collateral issue.
- Payment made but not recognized.
- Disbursement cancelled or delayed.

## Possible Recoveries

- Catch-up payment.
- Rescheduled repayment.
- Grace-period recovery.
- Fee waiver.
- Family support.
- Income recovery.
- Early closure after asset sale.
- Correction of lender or household record.

No implementation detail is implied by this lifecycle.

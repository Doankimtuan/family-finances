# User Behaviors

## Common Behaviors

- Users name goals around real-life outcomes rather than financial categories.
- Users often estimate target amounts with round numbers.
- Users create goals when motivation is high, then update progress irregularly.
- Users may treat leftover salary as goal progress even when the money stays in the main account.
- Users may contribute more after bonuses, gifts, or months with lower spending.
- Users may pause goals silently when household pressure increases.
- Users may merge goals mentally when a bigger priority appears.
- Users often discuss goals with a partner only when the amount is large or the deadline is near.

## Expectations

- A goal should feel separate from ordinary spending.
- Progress should be easy to understand.
- The household should be able to see what the goal is for.
- A goal should tolerate real-life changes.
- Completed goals should feel acknowledged.
- Cancelled or paused goals should not feel like personal failure.
- Goal language should not imply guaranteed money unless backed by real account facts.

## Frustrations

- Not knowing whether goal money is actually available.
- Seeing progress that does not match bank reality.
- Losing track after cash spending.
- Partner disagreement about priority.
- Feeling judged when a goal falls behind.
- Confusing emergency fund, savings account, jar, and goal.
- Deadlines becoming unrealistic after inflation, job changes, or family events.

## Mistakes

- Counting the same money toward multiple goals.
- Treating a target as a commitment without checking monthly capacity.
- Forgetting previous manual contributions.
- Mistaking planned progress for real balance.
- Choosing too many active goals.
- Not adjusting the target after prices change.
- Completing a goal before the money is actually available.

## Decision Patterns

- Safety goals usually outrank lifestyle goals during uncertainty.
- Time-sensitive goals outrank vague long-term goals.
- Family obligations may override personal goals.
- Debt pressure often competes with goal funding.
- A visible deadline increases action.
- Partners may trade off fairness, urgency, emotional meaning, and affordability rather than pure financial optimization.

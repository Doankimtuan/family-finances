# Domain Purpose

## Why This Domain Exists

Goals exist because households do not save only for abstract wealth. They save for concrete future outcomes: emergency protection, a motorbike, a baby fund, rent deposit, wedding cost, school fees, family support, travel, home appliances, debt-free milestones, or a buffer before career change.

In real life, a goal turns money from "leftover balance" into a named promise. It creates a reason not to spend, a shared reference for partners, and a way to compare today's wants with tomorrow's needs.

## Core Responsibility

Goals own named future intentions.

The domain's core responsibility is to describe a target outcome, its expected amount, optional timing pressure, progress interpretation, lifecycle state, and household meaning.

Goals do not own bank balances, cash balances, savings product truth, investment performance, debt balances, payment execution, transaction truth, or advisory recommendations.

## Financial Principle

The financial principle behind Goals is purpose-based accumulation.

A goal is a household commitment to reserve or accumulate capacity for a future purpose. It may be supported by real money in cash, accounts, savings products, or investments, but the goal itself remains an intention unless and until factual money movement is recorded by the owning money domain.

## Value Provided

Goals provide:

- Motivation for saving.
- Shared partner clarity.
- A named reason for trade-offs.
- Visibility into preparation progress.
- A way to protect important future expenses from daily spending.
- A bridge between monthly planning and longer household milestones.

The value is behavioral, communicative, and prioritization-oriented. It is not financial execution.

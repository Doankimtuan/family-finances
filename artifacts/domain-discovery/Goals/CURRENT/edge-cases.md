# Edge Cases

This document identifies real-world edge cases only. It does not propose solutions.

## Cancellation

- Goal is no longer wanted.
- Goal is replaced by another household priority.
- Relationship or household structure changes.
- Goal becomes emotionally sensitive.

## Correction

- Target amount was entered incorrectly.
- Contribution was recorded twice.
- Contribution was recorded against the wrong goal.
- Target date was wrong.
- Goal status was changed accidentally.

## Refund

- Purchase for the goal is refunded.
- Deposit for a goal is returned.
- Vendor refund arrives after the goal was marked complete.
- Partial refund changes the true cost of the goal outcome.

## Expiration

- Target date passes.
- Promotion or travel window expires.
- School, rent, or event deadline is missed.
- A savings product associated with the goal matures.

## Rate Changes

- Bank savings rate changes.
- Loan or card cost changes and competes with goal funding.
- Inflation changes estimated target amount.
- Foreign exchange rate changes affect travel, study, or import goals.

## Missing Information

- Cash contribution cannot be verified.
- Partner contributed from a private account.
- Provider data is unavailable.
- The household cannot remember whether money was already used.
- Price quote is uncertain.

## Manual Adjustment

- Household decides to count existing money as progress.
- Household reduces or increases the target.
- Household resets progress after using the money.
- Household merges two goals.
- Household splits one goal into separate purposes.

## Unexpected Events

- Job loss.
- Medical emergency.
- Family support request.
- Theft or lost cash.
- Bank outage.
- Failed transfer.
- Cancelled event.
- Vendor price increase.
- Pregnancy, school admission, relocation, or family ceremony.

## Boundary Confusion

- Goal progress is mistaken for bank balance.
- Savings product balance is mistaken for goal progress.
- Same money is counted toward multiple goals.
- A jar and goal represent similar purpose with different meanings.
- A debt payoff intention is mistaken for actual debt reduction.

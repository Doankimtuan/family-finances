# Real-World Analysis

## Vietnam Context

Young Vietnamese households often pursue goals while managing mixed payment channels, monthly salary cycles, family obligations, and rising digital banking usage. Goal saving may happen through one or more practical containers:

- Cash kept at home.
- A main bank account.
- A separate bank account.
- E-wallet balance.
- Flexible savings account.
- Term deposit or certificate of deposit.
- Gold or foreign currency held informally.
- Informal contributions from relatives.
- A spreadsheet, notes app, or chat message that tracks progress without moving money.

The same word "saving" can mean a behavior, a bank product, a household intention, or a specific account balance. This makes boundary clarity important.

## Common Practices

- Set goals after a life event, stress event, payday conversation, or upcoming deadline.
- Save after salary arrives, often before discretionary spending.
- Use one partner as the operator while both partners care about the result.
- Keep one or two serious goals visible and many smaller goals informal.
- Round target amounts rather than calculate exact future cost.
- Treat emergency fund, child expenses, wedding, travel, home setup, motorbike, rent deposit, and family support as common goal types.
- Pause goals when debt, illness, job loss, Tet spending, school fees, or family support takes priority.
- Keep money in the same bank account while mentally treating part of it as reserved.
- Use separate accounts or term deposits when self-control or partner trust requires stronger separation.

## Real-World Variations

### Timing

- Short-term goals under 3 months.
- Medium-term goals over 3-24 months.
- Life-event goals over multiple years.
- Date-driven goals such as rent deposit, school fee, wedding, Tet travel, childbirth, insurance premium, or tuition.
- Open-ended goals such as emergency fund or family support buffer.

### Funding Behavior

- Fixed monthly amount.
- Percent of salary.
- Leftover sweep after bills.
- Bonus or Tet bonus allocation.
- Irregular top-ups.
- Partner-specific contributions.
- Family gift or support contribution.
- One-time transfer into a savings product.

### Goal Types

- Protection goals: emergency fund, medical reserve, job transition buffer.
- Purchase goals: phone, appliance, motorbike, furniture, laptop.
- Life goals: wedding, baby fund, house move, home deposit.
- Family goals: parent support, sibling education, family event.
- Annual pressure goals: Tet, insurance, school, vehicle maintenance.
- Experience goals: travel, holiday, celebration.
- Obligation-adjacent goals: credit-card payoff intention or loan prepayment preparation.

## Industry Standards And Concepts

Common personal-finance concepts that influence goal interpretation:

- Pay yourself first: save toward important goals before discretionary spending.
- Emergency fund: liquid reserve for unexpected events.
- Sinking fund: accumulation for known future expenses.
- Envelope budgeting: separate money by purpose.
- Target date: the date when a goal becomes relevant.
- Target amount: expected amount needed for the outcome.
- Progress percentage: funded amount divided by target amount.
- Opportunity cost: money assigned to one goal cannot serve another purpose at the same time.
- Liquidity: how easily goal money can be accessed.

These concepts are useful for discovery, but households often use partial, informal versions.

## International Differences That Affect Product Design

International differences matter when they change interpretation:

- In open-banking-heavy markets, goal progress may be tied directly to provider balances. In Vietnam, manual and mixed-container tracking remains important.
- In tax-advantaged savings markets, goals may map to regulated account wrappers. For this household domain, wrapper truth belongs outside Goals.
- In markets with mature robo-advisory culture, goals may become investment planning. That is outside this discovery unless later validated and separately governed.

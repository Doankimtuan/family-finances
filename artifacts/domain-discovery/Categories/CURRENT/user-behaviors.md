# User Behaviors

## Common Behaviors

- Users categorize only when a transaction is visible or when they are reviewing the month.
- Users start with broad labels and add specific labels after repeated frustration.
- Users remember context through merchant names, notes, banking notifications, and chat history.
- Users often ask a partner what a transaction was for.
- Users accept approximate categories for small items.
- Users care more about recurring categories than rare one-off categories.
- Users may leave unclear transactions uncategorized until later.
- Users often use family-specific categories that formal systems do not predict.

## Expectations

- Category assignment should be fast.
- Common categories should be obvious.
- The app should remember repeated patterns.
- Both partners should see the same meaning.
- Reports should match ordinary language.
- The household should be able to correct wrong labels.
- Categories should not feel like bookkeeping homework.

## Frustrations

- Too many categories make selection slow.
- Too few categories hide important patterns.
- Similar labels create confusion, such as Dining, Restaurants, Coffee, and Delivery.
- Imported labels from banks or cards can be wrong.
- One supermarket or e-commerce transaction may cover groceries, baby supplies, home goods, and gifts.
- Cash spending is hard to reconstruct.
- Partner disagreement can occur when one person classifies a purchase differently.
- Old category changes can make historical reports feel inconsistent.

## Mistakes

- Creating duplicate categories with near-identical meaning.
- Using payment method as a category.
- Treating a transfer as income or expense.
- Categorizing loan repayment as the original purchase.
- Treating a refund as new income.
- Creating too many narrow categories after one unusual purchase.
- Trusting auto-classification without review.
- Renaming categories casually and losing longitudinal comparability.

## Decision Patterns

- High-value transactions get more careful categorization.
- Recurring merchants get remembered category choices.
- Emotional categories, such as family support or baby expenses, receive more attention than neutral categories.
- Small convenience purchases are often grouped broadly.
- Households trade precision for speed during busy periods.
- Monthly review is where many category decisions become shared household decisions.

# Risks

## Business Risks

- Categories become the center of the product and weaken the real-ledger / virtual-planning distinction.
- Category vocabulary becomes too rigid for Vietnamese household life.
- Provider categories are mistaken for household truth.
- Household-specific categories become hard to compare across users or templates.
- Category complexity distracts from higher-value household decisions.

## Financial Risks

- Users confuse categories with balances.
- Users treat categorized spending as planned spending.
- Transfers, savings contributions, loan repayments, and reimbursements are misclassified as income or expense.
- Refunds are counted as ordinary income.
- Mixed purchases distort category totals.
- Historical category changes break period comparisons.

## UX Risks

- Manual categorization becomes repetitive work.
- Category list becomes too long.
- Category list becomes too generic.
- Wrong automatic or provider classification erodes trust.
- Users cannot find the right category quickly.
- Partners disagree about category meaning.
- Users feel judged by category names or reports.

## Misunderstanding Risks

- "Food" may include groceries, dining, coffee, delivery, or family meals.
- "Home" may mean rent, utilities, repairs, furniture, or household supplies.
- "Family" may mean gift, allowance, reimbursement, loan, medical support, or shared bill.
- "Health" may mean pharmacy, insurance, hospital, gym, or baby care.
- "Other" may become a dumping ground.
- "Business" may mix with household money.

## Future Scalability Risks

- Too many historical categories reduce report usefulness.
- Imported providers may use incompatible category taxonomies.
- Merchant normalization becomes difficult with Vietnamese abbreviations and payment intermediaries.
- Category mappings may drift as jars, goals, products, and household life stages evolve.
- Multi-currency, travel, and cross-border payments may introduce different classification expectations.
- AI or rule-based suggestions may become hard to audit if category reasoning is opaque.

# User Behaviors

## Common Behaviors

- Users check whether they are "okay" by looking at the main bank balance.
- Partners often compare this month to last month informally, using memory rather than records.
- Families delay looking at money when the situation feels stressful.
- Medical, school, Tet, travel, and family-support expenses are often remembered only when close.
- Users may treat available credit as backup money even when it increases risk.
- People often know a payment is coming but underestimate its timing conflict with other obligations.
- Emergency savings may exist as a mental rule rather than a separate account.

## Expectations

- Health should feel calm, plain, and non-judgmental.
- Users expect the signal to be based on real household facts, not generic advice.
- Users expect visible reasons behind any condition label or score.
- Users expect Health to notice obvious pressure from debt, unpaid decisions, missing setup, and low visibility.
- Users do not expect Health to require financial expertise.

## Frustrations

- "We earn enough, but money still disappears."
- "I do not know if this balance is safe to spend."
- "My partner and I are using different mental numbers."
- "Insurance exists, but I still do not know what we would pay."
- "Every app shows transactions, but none tells us whether we are stable."
- "A score feels fake if it cannot explain itself."

## Mistakes

- Confusing planned money with actual available money.
- Ignoring cash, wallet balances, informal loans, or family obligations.
- Counting the same buffer for multiple purposes.
- Treating one good salary month as a new normal.
- Underestimating healthcare, maternity, child, and elder-care exposure.
- Paying debt on time while neglecting emergency reserves.
- Delaying decisions until they become urgent.

## Decision Patterns

- Liquidity-first: "Can we get through the month?"
- Obligation-first: "What must be paid before salary?"
- Family-first: "Who needs help?"
- Shock-first: "What if someone gets sick?"
- Debt-first: "Can we reduce monthly pressure?"
- Avoidance-first: "Do not look until payday."
- Partner-alignment-first: "Can we agree what is safe?"

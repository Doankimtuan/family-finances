# Lifecycle

## Beginning

Health begins when the household has enough visible facts to interpret condition. This may start with one account, a basic plan, recent transactions, known obligations, or open decisions.

Early Health is usually incomplete. It may reflect setup maturity as much as financial condition.

## Middle

Health evolves as household facts change:

- Income arrives.
- Spending occurs.
- Plans are created or ignored.
- Obligations become due.
- Debt is repaid or grows.
- Inbox decisions are resolved or delayed.
- Medical, family, school, Tet, travel, or emergency events appear.

The middle state is continuous. Health is rarely "finished"; it is a changing interpretation.

## End

Health ends only when the household context ends or can no longer be assessed.

Examples:

- User leaves the product.
- Household dissolves or splits.
- Data access is removed.
- Required source facts are deleted.
- Product no longer supports the domain.

## Possible Exits

- Household stops tracking.
- Household migrates to another tool.
- Household members separate financial lives.
- Health becomes unavailable because source domains are unavailable.

## Possible Interruptions

- Missing account data.
- Incomplete cash tracking.
- Partner stops contributing information.
- External provider sync fails.
- A major shock invalidates prior assumptions.
- Insurance or loan records are delayed.
- Month boundary is unclear.

## Possible Recoveries

- Source facts become visible again.
- Household updates missing records.
- Partners resolve open decisions.
- A shock event is recorded and understood.
- Historical comparison resumes after enough new data exists.

Recovery means the interpretation becomes useful again. It does not imply the household's finances are healthy.

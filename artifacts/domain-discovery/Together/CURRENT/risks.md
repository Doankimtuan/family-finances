# Risks

## Business Risks

- Together becomes too narrow and the product feels like single-user finance software.
- Together becomes too broad and the product drifts into enterprise permissions.
- Household membership is confused with legal ownership.
- Partner role language creates perceived hierarchy.
- Policy changes are treated as settings rather than trust events.
- The domain takes responsibility for disputes it cannot resolve.

## Financial Risks

- A user assumes shared visibility means shared legal authority over bank accounts.
- A partner acts on incomplete household scope.
- Personal obligations remain hidden until they affect shared cash flow.
- A policy appears to authorize a financial action that another domain must actually enforce.
- Former members retain access or appear to retain responsibility.

## UX Risks

- A less-engaged partner feels monitored instead of included.
- The active finance manager feels slowed down by collaboration overhead.
- Invitations feel like account control rather than partnership.
- Policy language is too abstract for ordinary household use.
- Household settings feel cold or administrative.
- Sensitive information appears without enough context.

## Misunderstanding Risks

- "Household" is mistaken for legal marriage.
- "Admin" is mistaken for money owner.
- "Partner" is mistaken for romantic partner only.
- "Policy" is mistaken for automatic enforcement in every domain.
- "Shared" is mistaken for no personal boundaries.
- "Visibility" is mistaken for consent to every future use.

## Future Scalability Risks

- Multi-household support may introduce complex switching, leakage, and consent issues.
- External data integrations may require finer consent than household membership alone.
- Cross-border households may have conflicting currency, privacy, and banking assumptions.
- Family support patterns may pressure the domain toward extended-family membership models.
- Separation, divorce, or coercive-control scenarios may require safety-sensitive handling.
- Audit history may become large and legally sensitive over years.

## Trust Risks

- Hidden policy changes erode confidence.
- Missing attribution creates conflict.
- Over-notification creates fatigue.
- Under-notification creates surprise.
- Overly granular controls imply mistrust.
- Overly broad controls create privacy harm.

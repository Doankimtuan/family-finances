# Boundaries

## What Belongs Here

Planning owns:

- Household intention.
- Jars or envelopes as purpose-based planning concepts.
- Goal intention and progress interpretation.
- Recurring expected inflows and outflows.
- Planned allocation of income.
- Planning-period review.
- Planned capacity movements.
- Calendar awareness for expected obligations.
- Assumptions that affect household planning.
- Shared interpretation of what money is meant to do.

## What Belongs In Another Domain

| Concern | Owning domain |
| --- | --- |
| Real account balance | Accounts / Ledger |
| Real transaction fact | Transactions / Ledger |
| Cash movement | Transactions / Accounts |
| Credit-card limit, statement, and due truth | Cards |
| Loan contract, schedule, rate, and repayment truth | Loans |
| Term savings product, rate, maturity, withdrawal truth | Savings |
| Review queue and one-decision workflow | Inbox |
| Financial health score and interpretation | Health |
| Household membership and permissions | Together / Tenancy |
| Authentication | Auth / Tenancy |
| External provider truth | Provider domain or integration |

## Where Integrations Happen

Planning integrates with:

- Accounts for real position context.
- Transactions for actual spending and income evidence.
- Cards for due pressure.
- Loans for repayment pressure.
- Savings for goal/product distinction and maturity context.
- Inbox for unresolved planning decisions.
- Health for read-only interpretation of plan and fact.
- Together for partner-visible policies and shared rights.

## Where Ownership Changes

Ownership changes when an intention becomes a fact.

- A planned expense becomes a Transaction when money actually moves.
- A planned transfer becomes ledger truth when executed.
- A planned loan payment belongs to Loans and Transactions once paid or confirmed.
- A planned savings contribution belongs to Savings and Transactions once funded.
- A planned review item belongs to Inbox when it requires a discrete household decision.

Planning may keep context, but it must not rewrite the owning domain's truth.

# Lifecycle

## Beginning

Planning begins when the household forms an intention about money.

Typical starting points:

- New household setup.
- Salary received or expected.
- First shared rent or bill.
- New child, marriage, move, school term, job change, or debt.
- A goal appears.
- A recurring obligation is recognized.
- A month begins.

## Middle

During active use, planning changes as reality changes.

Natural middle states:

- Allocate expected income.
- Track recurring obligations.
- Maintain jars or envelopes.
- Monitor goals.
- Notice mismatch between plan and actual spending.
- Discuss trade-offs with partner.
- Adjust after emergency or overspend.
- Pause or deprioritize an intention.
- Review upcoming dates.

## End

A planning item naturally ends when the intention is no longer active.

End states:

- Goal completed.
- Goal cancelled.
- Jar archived.
- Recurring obligation ended.
- Month reviewed and locked.
- Household stops using a planning category.
- Real-world obligation moves to another domain, such as a settled loan or closed savings product.

## Possible Exits

- Planned expense becomes a real transaction.
- Planned repayment is represented by loan or card payment truth.
- Planned saving becomes savings product truth.
- Goal is completed, cancelled, or replaced.
- Household changes planning method.
- Household dissolves or membership changes.

## Possible Interruptions

- Income delayed or missing.
- Emergency expense.
- Partner disagreement.
- Incorrect amount or date.
- Provider information missing.
- Cash spending not recorded.
- Debt payment changes.
- Rate or fee changes.
- Large seasonal event such as Tet, travel, school enrollment, wedding, funeral, or medical event.

## Possible Recoveries

- Correct the planning record.
- Reopen discussion before month close.
- Reallocate intention.
- Mark a goal or jar paused.
- Resume a recurring rule.
- Acknowledge mismatch in review.
- Start a new month with revised assumptions.

No lifecycle step implies implementation behavior. This document only describes the natural domain pattern.

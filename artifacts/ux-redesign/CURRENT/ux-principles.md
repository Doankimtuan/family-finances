# UX Principles

## Canonical Rules

1. One primary purpose per screen.
2. One primary action per state.
3. Progressive disclosure for complex financial setup.
4. Minimum reasonable steps for common actions.
5. Show consequences before meaningful financial confirmation.
6. Never hide real money movement.
7. Do not confirm harmless reversible actions.
8. Confirm destructive, irreversible, real-money, closed-period, or partner-visible actions.
9. Do not show disabled "coming soon" actions.
10. Avoid technical and banking terminology unless needed for correctness.
11. Preserve context across module transitions.
12. Every flow defines loading, success, failure, empty, recovery, offline, and stale states.

## Product Meaning

| Area | UX meaning | Must not imply |
|---|---|---|
| Home | What needs attention now. | That all modules need setup immediately. |
| Money | Real containers, real movement, financial product facts. | Plan capacity or financial advice. |
| Plan | Household intention and allocation. | Real account balances or cash movement. |
| Inbox | Decisions only. | Generic notifications or marketing alerts. |
| Together | Household people, access, policies, preferences. | Relationship judgment. |
| Health | Read-only mirror of visible facts. | Commands, optimization, or advice. |
| Investments | Risk-bearing holding context. | Spendable cash, recommendation, or automated trade. |

## Interaction Posture

- First-time users should see three setup essentials before advanced configuration.
- Recurring users should capture known income/expense/transfer in about 15 seconds.
- Screens should ask one plain-language question.
- Primary actions should sit within thumb reach on mobile.
- Complex forms should teach through sequencing, not long explanations.

# Behavior Analysis

## Common Habits

- Goals are often named after outcomes, not categories.
- Targets are estimated with round numbers.
- Contributions cluster around salary, bonus, Tet bonus, gifts, or low-spend months.
- People use mental accounting even when money stays in one account.
- Couples often discuss big goals but not every small contribution.
- Users may track goals enthusiastically at first and less consistently later.

## Decision Patterns

- Emergency and family-support goals outrank lifestyle goals during stress.
- Date-driven goals receive more attention than open-ended goals.
- Visible progress encourages continued saving.
- Too many goals reduce commitment.
- Partner agreement matters more for large or emotional goals.
- Debt, rent, childcare, and family obligations often override personal goals.

## Typical Mistakes

- Counting the same money toward several goals.
- Treating a contribution record as a bank transfer.
- Forgetting to reduce progress after using goal money.
- Completing a goal because the target is reached, even though the purchase has not happened.
- Underestimating final cost.
- Not revising target amount after price changes.
- Keeping stale goals active.

## Financial Awareness

Low-awareness users understand "save for X" but may not understand the distinction between goal progress and real balance.

Developing users understand that goal money should be protected but may not reconcile progress across accounts, wallets, cash, and savings products.

High-awareness users expect clear separation between intention, real money, provider product, and transaction history.

## Emotional Factors

- Goals are motivating because they attach money to meaning.
- Falling behind can feel personal.
- Partner contribution differences can create fairness concerns.
- Emergency interruptions can create guilt.
- Completion creates relief and pride.
- Overstated progress damages trust quickly.

## Manual Work Users Accept

- Naming a meaningful goal.
- Entering a rough target.
- Adding occasional contributions.
- Updating a deadline when the goal matters.
- Pausing or cancelling when life changes.
- Adding short notes for meaningful events.

## Manual Work Users Hate

- Reconciling every small cash movement.
- Maintaining many active goals.
- Explaining repeated missed contributions.
- Splitting one transfer across many purposes.
- Updating stale goals with no immediate benefit.
- Re-entering facts already visible in a bank or wallet app.

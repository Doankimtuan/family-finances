# Pain Points

## Current Frustrations

- "We want to save, but the money disappears into daily spending."
- "We do not know if this goal is actually funded."
- "My partner and I remember different numbers."
- "We had to use the money for something urgent."
- "The target changed, and now the old progress feels wrong."
- "We saved in several places and cannot see the full picture."

## Confusing Concepts

- Goal progress versus bank balance.
- Goal contribution versus real transfer.
- Emergency fund versus savings account.
- Goal versus jar.
- Savings product versus savings goal.
- Completed goal versus completed purchase.
- Paused goal versus failed goal.

## Unnecessary Complexity

- Too many goal states or labels can feel heavy.
- Detailed source attribution may feel like bookkeeping if the household only needs approximate motivation.
- Priority ranking can feel premature when a household has only one or two goals.
- Investment-style goal language can exceed the financial maturity of many young households.

## Missing Visibility

- Whether progress is verified or manual.
- Whether the same money is counted elsewhere.
- Whether a target date is now unrealistic.
- Whether goal money is liquid enough to use.
- Whether partner contributions are visible, private, or sensitive.
- Whether external savings products are supporting the goal.

## Potential Misunderstandings

- Users may assume "funded" means cash is available.
- Users may assume a goal controls the underlying money.
- Users may assume a completed goal means payment happened.
- Users may assume progress is automatically updated from bank transactions.
- Users may assume cancelling erases past effort.

# Target Users

## Primary Users

Primary users are young Vietnamese partners who share some future financial intentions even when their accounts remain separate.

Common traits:

- Age 20-35.
- Salaried, freelance, small-business, or mixed-income.
- Mobile-bank and e-wallet users.
- Comfortable with QR transfers and chat-based coordination.
- Still building shared money habits.
- Often manage goals through memory, notes, spreadsheets, bank sub-accounts, cash, or term deposits.
- Need motivation and clarity without turning family life into formal financial planning.

## Secondary Users

Secondary users include:

- Single people preparing for marriage or independent living.
- Less-active partners who want confidence that future priorities are protected.
- Spouses who contribute money but do not maintain records.
- Relatives who give or receive support connected to goals.
- Future children whose needs create education, healthcare, childcare, and housing goals.

## Household Situations

Single:

- Saves for independence, emergency buffer, travel, motorbike, phone, education, or family support.
- May not distinguish account balance from goal progress.

Dating or committed partners:

- Save for trips, rent deposit, shared purchases, wedding, or moving in.
- Money remains personally owned, but goals may already feel shared.

Newly married:

- Negotiates which goals are household goals and which remain personal.
- May start emergency fund, home setup, childbirth, debt repayment, or family-support goals.

Young family:

- Goal pressure increases through childcare, medical needs, school fees, housing, vehicle, and emergency buffer.
- Short-term goals compete with monthly bills.

Family with children:

- Goals become less optional and more time-sensitive.
- Education, insurance premiums, health buffer, housing, and transport become recurring long-term themes.

## Financial Maturity Levels

Low maturity:

- Thinks in simple outcomes: "save for this."
- Updates progress irregularly.
- May count money by memory.

Developing maturity:

- Separates some goal money from daily spending.
- Uses notes, bank accounts, wallets, or spreadsheets.
- Understands target and deadline but may not reconcile consistently.

High maturity:

- Distinguishes goal intention, account balance, savings products, and transactions.
- Reviews goals against cash flow and obligations.
- Notices stale targets, duplicated progress, and unfunded assumptions.

## Life Stages

- First job and independent living.
- Dating and shared experiences.
- Moving in together.
- Wedding preparation.
- Early marriage.
- Childbirth and childcare.
- Supporting parents.
- Buying a motorbike, car, or home.
- Career growth and income diversification.
- Education, healthcare, and long-term family stability.

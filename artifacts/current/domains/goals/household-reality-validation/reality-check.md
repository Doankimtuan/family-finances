# Reality Check

## Real-World Behavior vs Current Domain Understanding

The current domain understanding says Goals are named future intentions, not money containers. Young-household behavior supports this strongly.

Households already create goal-like mental structures:

- "This money is for emergencies."
- "We are saving for the wedding."
- "This bonus goes toward the motorbike."
- "School fees are coming."
- "We cannot touch that money."

The mental model is real. The main validation challenge is whether users can keep goal progress separate from real money truth over time.

## Already Validated

- Goals solve a real problem: future priorities are hard to protect from daily spending.
- Users naturally understand named targets.
- Goal language is more intuitive than abstract budgeting for many households.
- Shared goals matter for partners and young families.
- Emergency fund and major-purchase goals are realistic.
- Pausing, completion, and cancellation reflect real household behavior.
- Goals can survive 5-10 years because family responsibilities grow over time.
- The distinction between goal intention and real balance is financially necessary.

## Assumptions Remaining

- Whether "funded amount" is understood as intention rather than cash.
- Whether users expect contributions to represent real transfers.
- Whether Vietnamese users prefer "muc tieu", "quy", "tiet kiem", or another term in household context.
- Whether partner-specific contribution visibility builds trust or creates pressure.
- Whether users will maintain manual progress after the initial motivation fades.
- Whether separate goal types are needed for comprehension or would feel over-engineered.
- Whether completion means funded, purchased, paid, or emotionally done.

## Still Needs Confirmation

- Interviews with Vietnamese couples aged 20-35 about goals from the last 12 months.
- Review of actual household goal artifacts: notes, spreadsheets, bank screenshots, chat messages.
- Terminology testing for goal, progress, funded amount, contribution, pause, complete, and cancel.
- Observation of payday behavior around savings goals.
- Longitudinal evidence of manual goal maintenance.
- Research on cash, wallet, bank, savings product, and family contribution tracking.
- Research on whether current Plan Goals language is understood correctly.

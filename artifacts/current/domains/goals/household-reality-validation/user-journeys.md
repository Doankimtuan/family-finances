# User Journeys

## Happy Path

The household agrees on a clear future purpose, estimates a target amount, contributes after salary or bonus, checks progress occasionally, and uses the money when the goal arrives.

Behavioral markers:

- The goal has shared meaning.
- Target amount feels realistic.
- Contributions match household capacity.
- Partners trust the progress interpretation.
- Completion feels like achievement.

## Common Path

The household creates or discusses a goal, contributes irregularly, forgets some updates, pauses during pressure, and later revises the target or deadline.

Behavioral markers:

- The goal remains useful even when imperfect.
- Progress is approximate.
- Monthly obligations often interrupt.
- Partners may not discuss every change.
- The goal survives because it is emotionally meaningful.

## First-Time Usage

A user starts with a familiar goal such as emergency fund, travel, laptop, motorbike, wedding, or baby fund. They expect simple language: what it is for, how much is needed, how much has been set aside, and whether they are close.

Behavioral markers:

- Users understand named targets quickly.
- Users may not immediately understand whether progress is real money or intention.
- Users expect the goal to be forgiving if they estimate poorly.

## Long-Term Usage

Over years, the household adds, pauses, completes, cancels, and replaces goals as life changes. Goals become a memory of household priorities as well as a forward-looking tool.

Behavioral markers:

- Goal types shift from purchases and travel toward children, housing, health, education, and family support.
- Older goals may lose relevance.
- Trust depends on whether progress still feels truthful.
- Too many active goals can dilute attention.

## Recovery Path

The household falls behind, uses goal money for an emergency, discovers progress was overstated, or decides the goal no longer matters. Recovery behavior is usually emotional before it is procedural.

Behavioral markers:

- Users want to avoid blame.
- Partners need a shared explanation.
- Pausing or revising can preserve trust.
- Cancelling can be rational but may feel like failure.

## Shared Partner Path

One partner proposes a goal and the other negotiates urgency, amount, timing, and contribution. The household may agree explicitly or simply let one partner operate.

Behavioral markers:

- Fairness matters.
- Visibility can build trust or create pressure.
- Emotional meaning can outweigh pure financial logic.

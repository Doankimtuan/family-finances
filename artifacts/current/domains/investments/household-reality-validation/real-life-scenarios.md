# Real-Life Scenarios

## Best Case

Scenario:

A dual-income couple invests a modest monthly amount into an open-ended fund and occasionally buys listed stocks. They keep emergency cash separate.

User expects:

- Know total contributed amount.
- Know approximate current value.
- Know that invested money is long-term.
- Understand when proceeds become cash.

Successful outcome:

- Investment information gives confidence without disrupting monthly money management.

## Typical Case

Scenario:

One partner has a securities app, some fund units, and a small gold holding. The other partner knows "we have some investments" but not exact details.

User expects:

- Quickly understand whether the household is broadly up or down.
- Avoid double-counting transfers.
- See which assets are easy or hard to liquidate.

Successful outcome:

- The household can discuss investment exposure without needing professional-level portfolio analysis.

## Worst Case

Scenario:

A partner uses household surplus for speculative stocks, crypto, margin, or a private deal. The value drops sharply or becomes hard to verify.

User expects:

- Know what money was put in.
- Know what remains.
- Know whether more money is owed or at risk.
- Rebuild trust through factual clarity.

Successful outcome:

- The household separates facts from blame enough to understand the financial impact.

## Unexpected Case

Scenario:

A corporate action, dividend, stock split, fund distribution, bond delay, or insurance surrender value appears and the household does not understand it.

User expects:

- Know whether the event creates cash, changes quantity, changes value, or changes ownership.
- Avoid recording it as ordinary income or spending by mistake.

Successful outcome:

- The event is recognized as investment-related and does not corrupt the household's everyday money picture.

## Emergency Case

Scenario:

The household needs cash for medical care, job loss, family support, rent deposit, or childbirth.

User expects:

- Know which investments can become cash quickly.
- Know whether selling means loss, fee, tax, delay, or partner disagreement.
- Avoid assuming market value equals available money.

Successful outcome:

- The household understands liquidity reality before relying on investment value.

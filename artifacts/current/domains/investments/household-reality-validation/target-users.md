# Target Users

## Primary Users

Primary users are young Vietnamese household members who already invest, are starting to invest, or need to understand a partner's investment position.

Common traits:

- Age 20-35.
- Salaried, freelance, small-business, or mixed-income.
- Mobile-bank, e-wallet, broker-app, and fund-app users.
- Comfortable transferring money digitally.
- Often inexperienced with investment terminology.
- May rely on friends, family, online communities, bank staff, or social media for investment cues.
- May not separate personal investing from household investing clearly.

## Secondary Users

Secondary users include:

- Less-active partners who need confidence, not trading detail.
- Spouses who contribute household money but do not make investment choices.
- Parents or relatives who influence gold, land, family-business, or insurance decisions.
- Future children whose education and care costs change investment horizon.
- Household members affected by investment loss, illiquidity, or secrecy.

## Household Situations

Single:

- Experiments with stocks, funds, crypto, gold, or small recurring investments.
- May treat investment as self-improvement or future security.
- Usually tolerates more personal risk because obligations are lighter.

Committed partners:

- May keep investment accounts separate.
- Shared spending exists before shared investment rules.
- One partner may not know exact positions.

Newly married:

- Financial visibility becomes more sensitive.
- Investment losses, speculative behavior, or hidden accounts can affect trust.
- Long-term asset building becomes part of household identity.

Young family:

- Investment decisions compete with emergency cash, childcare, rent, insurance, and home-buying plans.
- Risk tolerance often becomes more conservative.
- Liquidity matters more than before.

Family with children:

- Education, healthcare, home, and retirement needs lengthen the horizon.
- Investment decisions increasingly need partner alignment.
- Household may want to protect core safety money from market risk.

## Financial Maturity Levels

Low maturity:

- Knows contribution amount more than current value.
- Confuses gain, cash, and value.
- Follows social proof.
- May not understand fees, liquidity, or risk.

Developing maturity:

- Separates emergency cash from invested money.
- Understands some asset classes.
- Checks broker/fund apps directly.
- Can explain why an investment exists but may not track performance rigorously.

High maturity:

- Separates account cash, holdings, cost basis, income, and realized gain/loss.
- Understands liquidity and concentration risk.
- Reviews portfolio periodically.
- Can explain investment decisions to a partner.

## Life Stages

- First job and surplus income.
- Moving in together.
- Marriage planning.
- First major bonus or Tet surplus.
- Pregnancy or childbirth.
- Child education planning.
- Buying a motorbike, car, or home.
- Supporting parents.
- Income growth and career change.
- Business ownership or family investment participation.
- Long-term wealth, retirement, or inheritance planning.

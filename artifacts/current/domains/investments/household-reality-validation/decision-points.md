# Decision Points

## Create / Recognize Investment

Why it matters:

- Establishes that money has moved from cash or savings into a risk-bearing asset.
- Clarifies whether the asset belongs to the household, one partner, or extended family.

## Classify Asset

Why it matters:

- Stocks, funds, bonds, gold, crypto, insurance-linked products, and private investments behave differently.
- Wrong classification causes wrong expectations about safety, liquidity, and value.

## Contribute More

Why it matters:

- Converts household surplus or personal money into investment exposure.
- Affects liquidity and partner trust.

## Pause Contributions

Why it matters:

- Often happens after market losses, income changes, pregnancy, job loss, or major expenses.
- Separates investment habit from household cash pressure.

## Sell / Redeem / Withdraw

Why it matters:

- Converts uncertain investment value into real cash.
- May realize gain, loss, fee, tax, or regret.
- Timing can affect household decisions.

## Hold

Why it matters:

- A deliberate hold can be long-term discipline.
- An accidental hold can hide avoidance, stale data, or forgotten assets.

## Edit / Correct

Why it matters:

- Investment records are easy to misstate because of units, prices, settlement dates, fees, and corporate actions.
- Corrections preserve household understanding over time.

## Review

Why it matters:

- Market values, risk, partner expectations, and life priorities change.
- Review distinguishes active confidence from passive neglect.

## Approve

Why it matters:

- Shared money investments may require explicit partner consent.
- Approval can protect trust when risk is meaningful.

## Reject

Why it matters:

- A partner may reject using household money for risk-bearing assets.
- Rejection may reflect liquidity needs, risk tolerance, or prior bad experience.

## Reclassify

Why it matters:

- A product may be misunderstood as savings, insurance, investment, or family support.
- Reclassification prevents wrong financial interpretation.

## Write Down / Write Off

Why it matters:

- Private, failed, or unverifiable investments may no longer represent realistic household value.
- Emotionally difficult but important for truthful household position.

## Transfer Ownership Or Custody

Why it matters:

- Assets may move between broker accounts, partners, relatives, or custodians.
- Ownership and control are not always the same.

## Close / Archive

Why it matters:

- Sold, redeemed, expired, or irrelevant investments should stop appearing as active exposure.
- History remains important for household memory and trust.

# Reality Check

## Real-World Behavior

Young households rarely build formal financial-health models. They ask simpler questions:

- Are we okay until payday?
- Can we afford this without regret?
- What is making money feel tight?
- Are we prepared if someone gets sick?
- Are we both seeing the same situation?
- Did this month improve or get worse?

They use bank balances, memory, partner conversations, debt due dates, family obligations, and recent stress as informal signals.

## Current Domain Understanding

Phase 1 understands Health as:

- A read-only financial condition mirror.
- An interpreter of source-domain facts.
- A signal of stability, pressure, resilience, and attention.
- Not medical health management.
- Not financial, insurance, or medical advice.
- Not automation or money movement.

## Already Validated

- The problem is real: separate financial facts do not create household understanding by themselves.
- The domain boundary is realistic: users need reflection, but actions belong elsewhere.
- Read-only behavior supports trust.
- Partner-shared language is valuable.
- Medical expense exposure is relevant in Vietnam-first household reality.
- Health can remain useful over 5-10 years if it reflects durable household concerns.

## Assumptions Remaining

- Users will understand "Health" as financial health without confusion.
- Users will trust a score or level if the explanation is clear.
- Users will accept a partial signal when data is incomplete.
- Users will not interpret Health as advice.
- Partners will use Health constructively rather than as blame evidence.
- Medical and insurance context can be discussed safely without crossing advisory boundaries.

## Still Needs Confirmation

- Vietnam-first terminology in Vietnamese and English.
- Direct household interviews with ages 20-35.
- Whether numeric scores create anxiety or motivation.
- How households perceive "strong" when they still feel financial stress.
- Manual correction tolerance after missing data or stressful events.
- Real partner behavior during disagreements.
- How strongly medical-cost concerns influence ordinary financial-health perception.

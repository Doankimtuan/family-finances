# Behavior Analysis

## Common Habits

- Checking only the main account balance.
- Mentally subtracting known bills.
- Waiting until payday to feel safe.
- Avoiding money review after stressful spending.
- Relying on one partner to remember obligations.
- Treating family help as a backup plan.
- Treating insurance as protection without knowing practical limits.

## Decision Patterns

- Short-term safety beats long-term optimization.
- Salary rhythm shapes household confidence.
- Debt payment dates feel more urgent than savings goals.
- Medical and family obligations override ordinary plans.
- Partners may prioritize harmony over full transparency.
- Users often need a visible reason before accepting a negative signal.

## Typical Mistakes

- Confusing credit limit with available money.
- Counting planned money as spendable cash.
- Forgetting annual or seasonal expenses.
- Ignoring small recurring drains.
- Underestimating medical out-of-pocket exposure.
- Assuming a partner has the same financial picture.
- Treating one positive month as proof of stability.

## Financial Awareness

Most young households understand pressure before they understand formal ratios. They can usually identify rent, salary, debt, and large expenses, but may not naturally connect them into a durable financial-health model.

The domain matches a real awareness gap: users feel condition emotionally before they can explain it financially.

## Emotional Factors

- Pride when the household feels stable.
- Anxiety when facts are missing.
- Shame when the signal feels like judgment.
- Relief when pressure is named clearly.
- Defensiveness when partner behavior appears implicated.
- Trust when the signal matches lived experience.

## Manual Work Users Accept

- Confirming important missing facts.
- Reviewing obvious decisions.
- Recording major expenses.
- Maintaining core accounts.
- Updating debt or medical events that matter.

## Manual Work Users Hate

- Re-entering information already visible elsewhere.
- Categorizing every small transaction.
- Explaining every variance.
- Maintaining complex scoring inputs.
- Updating abstract financial concepts they do not use in daily life.
- Doing work that does not visibly improve understanding.

# User Journeys

## Happy Path

The household has visible accounts, recent transactions, a basic plan, and few unresolved decisions. Partners look at Health, understand why the condition is steady or strong, and use it as a calm check-in.

Behavioral success:

- Users trust the signal because the reasons are recognizable.
- Partners use the same facts during conversation.
- Health reduces ambiguity without taking over decisions.

## Common Path

The household has partial data. Some cash, family obligations, medical concerns, or debts are missing. Health still helps them notice setup gaps, unresolved decisions, or tracking rhythm, but users treat it cautiously.

Behavioral success:

- Users understand Health as a partial mirror.
- Users do not mistake the signal for complete truth.
- The domain prompts attention without creating shame.

## First-Time Usage

Users want to know "Are we okay?" They may not know what financial health means. They expect simple language, visible reasons, and no lecture.

Behavioral success:

- Users understand that Health is about household finance, not medical health.
- Users understand it reads existing household facts.
- Users do not feel punished for incomplete setup.

## Long-Term Usage

Over months and years, the household uses Health as a periodic check. The meaning changes as income grows, children arrive, debt increases, or expenses become more complex.

Behavioral success:

- Health remains useful as a relationship and family-management ritual.
- Users still recognize the signal as grounded in their reality.
- Health does not become repetitive noise.

## Recovery Path

After stress, illness, job loss, overspending, missed debt, or avoidance, users return to understand what is visible now.

Behavioral success:

- Health helps them re-enter the financial picture calmly.
- Users can see pressure without feeling blamed.
- Partners can restart conversation from facts rather than memory.

# Pain Points

## Current Frustrations

- Users know their balance but not whether the household is safe.
- Partners hold different mental versions of the financial picture.
- Debt, plans, and upcoming bills are hard to combine mentally.
- Medical and family emergencies make ordinary planning feel fragile.
- Existing finance apps can feel transactional rather than reflective.
- Users may not know whether stress comes from low income, high spending, debt, or poor visibility.

## Confusing Concepts

- Financial Health versus medical health.
- Score versus diagnosis.
- Read-only observation versus recommendation.
- Plan intention versus real money.
- Emergency buffer versus available balance.
- Insurance coverage versus full financial protection.
- Credit limit versus household resilience.

## Unnecessary Complexity

- Too many factors before users trust the basic signal.
- Overly precise scores when data is incomplete.
- Technical ratios without household-language explanation.
- Medical insurance details that cross into advice.
- Complex trend interpretation before stable tracking exists.

## Missing Visibility

- Real liquidity across cash, accounts, and wallets.
- Debt and card pressure.
- Informal family obligations.
- Medical out-of-pocket exposure.
- Insurance reimbursement uncertainty.
- Partner-specific knowledge gaps.
- Seasonal and annual pressure.

## Potential Misunderstandings

- Users may think Health can fix a problem.
- Users may expect Health to know facts that were never recorded.
- A strong signal may be interpreted as permission to spend.
- A weak signal may feel like blame.
- AI or scenario language may be read as instruction.

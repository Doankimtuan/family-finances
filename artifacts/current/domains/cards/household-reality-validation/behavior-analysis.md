# Behavior Analysis

## Common Habits

- Checking issuer apps or SMS notifications after large card purchases.
- Paying credit-card bills near salary day.
- Using debit cards for ATM access, POS payments, and account-linked spending.
- Using credit cards for online purchases, cashback, travel, appliances, and installments.
- Mentally grouping card spending by merchant or purpose, not by statement cycle.
- Keeping one default card saved in e-commerce apps.
- Ignoring unused cards until annual fees, expiry, or suspicious transactions appear.
- Asking a partner about unfamiliar card charges.

## Decision Patterns

- Users choose card payment when it is convenient, accepted, discounted, or reward-bearing.
- Users choose QR or bank transfer when merchants prefer it or card acceptance is weak.
- Users decide repayment amount based on cash on hand, salary timing, partner contribution, and anxiety level.
- Users may prioritize paying cards before discretionary spending when they understand interest risk.
- Users may avoid checking statements when debt feels shameful or confusing.
- Users trust issuer apps most when household records conflict.

## Typical Mistakes

- Treating credit limit as household money.
- Confusing statement date with due date.
- Recording card repayment as a new expense after already recording the card purchase.
- Missing annual fees, late fees, cash-advance fees, or FX costs.
- Assuming minimum payment prevents interest.
- Assuming refunds remove the need to pay the current statement.
- Forgetting card-on-file subscriptions.
- Converting a purchase to installments without understanding total future pressure.

## Financial Awareness

Low-awareness users understand cards as ways to pay but may not understand credit, posting, statementing, or minimum payment.

Medium-awareness users understand paying the bill but may not understand interest calculation, refund timing, dispute flow, or installment impact.

High-awareness users understand limit, utilization, statement cycles, full payment, rewards, and tradeoffs between convenience and debt.

## Emotional Factors

- A clean card bill creates confidence.
- A surprise charge creates suspicion or embarrassment.
- An upcoming due date creates stress when salary timing is tight.
- Full repayment creates relief.
- Interest or late fees create regret.
- Rewards create satisfaction and can encourage more spending.
- Card declines create urgency and loss of confidence.
- Shared card visibility can build trust or expose conflict.

## Manual Work Users Accept

- Adding a main credit card.
- Entering limit, statement date, and due date if it helps avoid missed payment.
- Recording large card purchases.
- Marking a card repayment.
- Correcting obvious wrong transactions.
- Reviewing monthly statement totals.
- Adding a note for unusual or shared purchases.

## Manual Work Users Hate

- Recording every small tap-to-pay or online transaction.
- Reconstructing old statement cycles.
- Manually splitting pending, posted, refunded, and disputed statuses.
- Entering card metadata they do not recognize.
- Tracking reward points manually.
- Re-entering information already visible in issuer apps.
- Resolving duplicated purchase and repayment records.

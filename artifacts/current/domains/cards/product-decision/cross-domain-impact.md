# Cross-Domain Impact

## Accounts

Accounts provide real money sources for debit-card spending and credit-card repayment. Credit-card available credit must not appear as real account balance. Linked repayment accounts may affect household cash visibility.

## Transactions

Transactions record card purchases, repayments, refunds, fees, interest, cashback, and corrections. Card purchases and repayments must remain distinct so spending is not double-counted.

## Cards

Cards owns issuer identity, card type interpretation, credit-card limit, statement cycle, due date, statement amount, paid amount, remaining due, billing-period association, refund awareness, and card history.

## Loans

Loans should not own revolving credit-card balances. Card-origin installments require clear interaction, but revolving card debt remains Cards behavior unless a separate approved loan-like obligation is intentionally created elsewhere.

## Planning

Planning reads card due dates and expected repayment amounts for household cash-flow awareness. Planning owns virtual allocation or preparation for repayment, not card obligation truth.

## Goals

Goals can be affected by card repayment pressure. Goals should not be reduced or mutated by Cards directly except through real money movements recorded elsewhere.

## Inbox

Inbox may surface card due-date reminders, unfamiliar-card review items, refund review, or repayment confirmation needs. Inbox owns user decisions, not card financial truth.

## Health

Health may read utilization, repayment pattern, interest burden, and card reliance as risk signals. Health cannot create, edit, close, repay, or reconcile cards.

## Categories

Categories help interpret what card purchases were for. Merchant names may not match household meaning, so category interpretation remains separate from card obligation.

## Together

Together affects visibility and trust for shared card spending, partner review, and household responsibility. Cards should not decide household permissions or privacy policy.

# Product Risks

## Financial Risk

- Users may believe resolving an Inbox item moved real money.
- Expired reminders may be mistaken for paid obligations.
- Auto-resolution may repeat a wrong household decision.
- Wrong source linkage can create false trust.

## UX Complexity Risk

- Too many states can feel technical.
- Too many item kinds can make Inbox feel like a system feed.
- Deferral and archive can become confusing if users do not understand the difference.
- Suggestions can be read as certainty.

## Maintenance Risk

- Each item kind creates long-term taxonomy and edge-case burden.
- Provider/evidence features can expand source complexity rapidly.
- Cross-domain routing can blur ownership if not tightly governed.

## Scalability Risk

- High transaction volume can turn Inbox into backlog.
- Multiple provider channels can create duplicates.
- Historical items can grow without remaining useful.

## Education Risk

- Users must understand Inbox is not a bank inbox.
- Users must understand notification, reminder, decision, and transaction are different.
- Users must understand Health reads but does not act.

## User Misunderstanding Risk

- Empty Inbox may be mistaken for complete financial safety.
- Acknowledgement may be mistaken for payment.
- Dismissal may be mistaken for deletion.
- Partner visibility may be mistaken for partner consent.

## Emotional Risk

- Backlog can create shame.
- Partner-targeted items can create blame.
- Analytics about partner behavior can damage trust; this is why partner coordination analytics are rejected.

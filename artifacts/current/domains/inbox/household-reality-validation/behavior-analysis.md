# Behavior Analysis

## Common Habits

- Users notice bank and wallet notifications but do not always process them.
- Young households review money in bursts rather than continuously.
- Partners often rely on memory and chat history for context.
- Low-value items are often ignored unless they affect a budget.
- High-value or unfamiliar items trigger more careful checking.
- One partner frequently becomes the financial operator.

## Decision Patterns

- Immediate decision when the item is obvious.
- Partner consultation when ownership or purpose is unclear.
- Avoidance when there are too many items.
- Fast dismissal when the item looks irrelevant.
- More scrutiny when the amount is high, the merchant is unknown, or the due date is near.
- Pattern recognition for repeated merchants and recurring bills.

## Typical Mistakes

- Resolving items just to reduce the queue.
- Forgetting why a transfer happened.
- Confusing bank notifications with complete records.
- Treating reminders as proof that payment occurred.
- Missing refunds or counting them as new income.
- Letting old items become impossible to interpret.
- Assuming one partner has the same financial context as the other.

## Financial Awareness

Inbox can increase awareness because it surfaces open loops. Users become more aware of:

- Spending that lacks purpose.
- Bills that require attention.
- Savings decisions that would otherwise auto-renew or be forgotten.
- Partner-created spending that needs household context.
- Repeated patterns that become predictable.

Awareness can turn negative if the queue is too large or too noisy.

## Emotional Factors

- Confidence when the queue is small.
- Anxiety when items accumulate.
- Relief when unclear items are resolved.
- Irritation when the queue contains low-value noise.
- Suspicion when a partner's transaction is unfamiliar.
- Shame when old items reveal neglected financial habits.
- Trust when history matches memory.

## Manual Work Users Accept

Users are likely to accept manual work when:

- The item is financially meaningful.
- The action is quick.
- The context is recent.
- The benefit is visible.
- The decision prevents confusion with a partner.
- The item affects a budget, savings decision, bill, or report.

## Manual Work Users Hate

Users are likely to dislike manual work when:

- Items are repetitive and obvious.
- The queue contains duplicate provider signals.
- The item lacks enough context to decide.
- Every small transaction feels equally important.
- The same decision must be made repeatedly.
- Resolution feels like bookkeeping rather than household clarity.

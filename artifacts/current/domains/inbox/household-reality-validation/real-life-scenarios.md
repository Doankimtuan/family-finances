# Real-Life Scenarios

## Best Case

Scenario:

A couple receives several predictable items: salary income, grocery QR payments, an electricity bill reminder, and a savings maturity notice. Each item is recent and recognizable.

User expects:

- The items are easy to understand.
- The decision required is obvious.
- Both partners can trust the result.

Successful outcome:

- The queue returns to a calm state.
- Records and plans feel current.
- No partner feels blamed or surprised.

## Typical Case

Scenario:

After a busy week, the household has several small payments from bank apps, e-wallets, cash notes, and delivery orders. Some are obvious; some are vague.

User expects:

- Obvious items can be handled quickly.
- Ambiguous items can wait until context is available.
- The queue does not mix marketing messages with real decisions.

Successful outcome:

- Most items are processed.
- Remaining items are understood as genuinely unresolved.
- The household still trusts its financial picture.

## Worst Case

Scenario:

The household ignores the queue for several weeks. There are many old transactions, reminders, possible duplicates, and unclear transfers between partners and relatives.

User expects:

- The system does not pretend certainty where memory is weak.
- The household can see which items still matter.
- Old items do not silently corrupt planning.

Successful outcome:

- The household regains enough clarity to continue.
- Known mistakes are visible.
- Trust can recover even if some historical context is incomplete.

## Unexpected Case

Scenario:

A merchant refund appears, but the original purchase was categorized weeks earlier. A separate e-wallet notification and bank notification may describe the same refund.

User expects:

- The item is not mistaken for new income.
- The household can compare it against memory or evidence.
- Duplicate-looking signals are not emotionally alarming.

Successful outcome:

- The household understands what happened.
- The financial meaning of the refund is not confused with fresh spending capacity.

## Emergency Case

Scenario:

A family emergency causes large transfers, cash withdrawals, medical payments, and temporary suspension of normal review habits.

User expects:

- The queue can hold unresolved attention without adding shame.
- Important items remain visible after the emergency.
- Partner visibility helps coordination.

Successful outcome:

- The household can reconstruct decisions later.
- Urgent real-life care is not blocked by financial administration.
- Recovery is possible without losing all trust in records.

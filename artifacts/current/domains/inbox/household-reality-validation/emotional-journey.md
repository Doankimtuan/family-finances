# Emotional Journey

## Beginning

Users may feel:

- Curiosity when a new item appears.
- Mild anxiety if the item is unfamiliar.
- Confidence if the item is expected.
- Irritation if the item seems unnecessary.

Trust is gained when the item clearly explains why it exists.

Trust is lost when the item feels like a duplicate, ad, or vague bank message.

## Middle

Users may feel:

- Focus when the decision is simple.
- Uncertainty when context is missing.
- Suspicion when a partner's spending is unfamiliar.
- Fatigue when many small items accumulate.
- Pressure when due dates or maturity dates are close.

Trust is gained when the item matches memory and provider evidence.

Trust is lost when the household cannot tell whether the item is financial truth, reminder, suggestion, or noise.

## Resolution

Users may feel:

- Relief when an item is resolved.
- Achievement when the queue becomes small.
- Confidence when records feel up to date.
- Regret when old items reveal forgotten spending.
- Concern if they are unsure but process the item anyway.

Trust is gained when resolution has a clear consequence and history.

Trust is lost if resolution appears to move or change money without the user understanding what happened.

## Backlog

Users may feel:

- Avoidance.
- Shame.
- Overwhelm.
- Resignation.
- Low trust in reports.

Backlog is the highest emotional risk for Inbox. A large queue can convert a helpful decision system into a symbol of failure.

## Long-Term

Users may feel:

- Calm when Inbox becomes a habit.
- Shared confidence when both partners participate.
- Independence when repeated items become predictable.
- Friction if review never becomes lighter.

Long-term trust depends on whether Inbox feels like household clarity rather than administrative punishment.

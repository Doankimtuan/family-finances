# Emotional Journey

## Before Joining

Common feelings:

- Curiosity.
- Caution.
- Hope for easier coordination.
- Anxiety about judgment.
- Concern about privacy.

Trust is gained when:

- The user understands what joining means.
- The invitation feels respectful.
- The household boundary feels intentional.

Trust is lost when:

- Joining feels like surveillance.
- Role language implies hierarchy.
- The user does not understand what becomes visible.

## Early Household Setup

Common feelings:

- Relief that shared finance has a place.
- Awkwardness discussing money rules.
- Mild stress over choosing policies.
- Optimism about being organized.

Trust is gained when:

- Both partners feel equally included.
- Policies feel understandable.
- The household name and member list feel accurate.

Trust is lost when:

- One partner appears to control the other.
- Setup exposes sensitive facts too abruptly.
- The second partner feels like an afterthought.

## Normal Use

Common feelings:

- Calm when assumptions are stable.
- Confidence when both partners see the same context.
- Background reliance on the system.
- Occasional irritation when one partner does more work.

Trust is gained when:

- Shared information stays consistent across domains.
- Material changes are visible.
- The product does not overreact to every small action.

Trust is lost when:

- A policy change surprises someone.
- Access state feels unclear.
- One partner feels monitored or excluded.

## Stress Events

Common feelings:

- Anxiety.
- Defensiveness.
- Urgency.
- Regret.
- Fear of blame.

Trust is gained when:

- The household can reconstruct what happened.
- Member and policy history are clear enough.
- The product does not pretend emotional conflict is simple.

Trust is lost when:

- History is ambiguous.
- Former or unsafe access remains unclear.
- Sensitive data appears to the wrong person.

## Long-Term Household Life

Common feelings:

- Achievement when the household matures.
- Relief from fewer money misunderstandings.
- Pride in shared progress.
- Fatigue if the product becomes administrative.

Trust is gained when:

- Together remains stable while life changes.
- The domain supports continuity across marriage, children, jobs, and family obligations.

Trust is lost when:

- The household outgrows the boundary model.
- Old members, policies, or assumptions become confusing.

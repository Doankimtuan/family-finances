# Real-Life Scenarios

## Best Case

A newly married couple agrees that all salary, rent, food, bills, and savings decisions should be visible. One partner creates the household and invites the other. They both understand shared policies and rarely need to revisit them.

User expects:

- Joining feels simple and respectful.
- Both partners can see household context.
- Policy changes are not hidden.

Successful outcome:

- Both partners feel included.
- Shared finance feels calmer.
- The household has one trusted boundary.

## Typical Case

One partner tracks money carefully. The other partner mostly trusts them but wants to know whether the month is okay. They use individual bank accounts, e-wallets, and transfers. Family support happens irregularly.

User expects:

- The active partner can manage details.
- The less-engaged partner can still understand important household facts.
- Personal and shared money are not confused casually.

Successful outcome:

- Fewer repeated questions.
- Fewer surprises.
- Household assumptions remain visible enough.

## Worst Case

One partner uses shared visibility to monitor, judge, or control the other. Hidden debt or hidden spending appears. A member wants to leave or restrict access.

User expects:

- Sensitive financial access should not create harm.
- Former or unsafe access should not feel permanent.
- The product should not pretend a relationship conflict is only a settings issue.

Successful outcome:

- Trust boundary is not misunderstood.
- Household membership and visibility remain serious concepts.
- Safety-sensitive cases are recognized as real.

## Unexpected Case

The household is financially shaped by people outside the couple: parents send money, siblings borrow money, or grandparents need medical support. These people affect finance but are not natural product partners.

User expects:

- The household can still understand its financial reality.
- Not every financially relevant person becomes a member.
- External family influence does not blur partner visibility.

Successful outcome:

- Together remains focused on the household trust boundary.
- Family influence is recognized without redefining membership too broadly.

## Emergency Case

One partner loses a job, has medical expenses, or must support a parent urgently. The household changes spending rules and starts reviewing money more closely.

User expects:

- The household can quickly understand who is acting and which assumptions changed.
- The partner not making the change can still see what happened.
- Emergency changes do not disappear into memory.

Successful outcome:

- The household can recover shared understanding after stress.
- Trust is preserved by clear visibility.

# Product Risks

## Financial Risk

- Users may confuse household visibility with legal authority over accounts.
- Policy settings may be misunderstood as automatic money movement.
- External provider consent, when future-reconsidered, may expose personal financial data too broadly.

## UX Complexity Risk

- Role language may feel hierarchical.
- Policy history may feel like an audit console.
- Membership history may feel emotionally charged.
- Too much recovery or lifecycle handling may make Together feel administrative.

## Maintenance Risk

- Household lifecycle cases can expand rapidly.
- Multi-household and complex family structures would add scope across every domain.
- Consent and export/deletion concerns require ongoing legal and security discipline.

## Scalability Risk

- One-household scope is maintainable now but may constrain future family-support use cases.
- Historical member interpretation grows more complex over years.
- Policy event volume may grow if materiality is not constrained.

## Education Risk

- Users may not understand Household, Partner, Admin, Policy, and Together consistently.
- Young households with low financial confidence may need concrete wording.
- Vietnamese-language terminology requires validation.

## User Misunderstanding Risk

- Admin may be read as owner.
- Partner may be read as romantic spouse only.
- Household may be read as legal family.
- Shared may be read as all personal facts become visible.
- Former member may be read as still responsible or still able to access data.

## Safety Risk

- Shared visibility can be unsafe in coercive or abusive relationships.
- Partner attribution can become blame-oriented.
- Exit and separation cases are high-stakes and under-researched.

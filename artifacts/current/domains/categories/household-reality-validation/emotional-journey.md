# Emotional Journey

## Beginning

Users often feel mild optimism when categories make spending understandable. Familiar labels create early confidence.

Trust is gained when:

- The first categories match everyday language.
- The household can quickly classify obvious transactions.
- Category summaries confirm what users already suspected.

Trust is lost when:

- Category names feel formal, foreign, or judgmental.
- Users cannot find the right label.
- Categories look like budgets or accounts.

## Daily Use

Daily categorization can feel quick and satisfying when the transaction is obvious. It can also feel like small administrative work.

Common feelings:

- Confidence for familiar merchants.
- Annoyance for repeated manual choices.
- Uncertainty for unclear bank descriptions.
- Mild guilt for discretionary spending.
- Relief when a transaction is easy to explain.

Trust is gained when category meaning is predictable.

Trust is lost when users feel they are doing bookkeeping instead of household management.

## Partner Review

Shared review introduces social emotion.

Common feelings:

- Accountability when spending is visible.
- Defensiveness when a category feels accusatory.
- Relief when a partner can explain unclear transactions.
- Frustration when one person does all category maintenance.
- Closeness when categories support calm conversation.

Trust is gained when categories help explain behavior without blame.

Trust is lost when labels create judgment, surveillance, or recurring arguments.

## Monthly Reflection

Category summaries can create insight and motivation.

Common feelings:

- Achievement when spending matches household expectations.
- Surprise when a category is higher than expected.
- Regret when discretionary spending is visible.
- Anxiety when essential categories rise.
- Clarity when recurring pressure becomes visible.

Trust is gained when totals match lived experience.

Trust is lost when reports are obviously distorted by missing, wrong, or overly broad categories.

## Long-Term Use

Over time, category stability can create confidence. Drift can create doubt.

Common feelings:

- Pride from seeing spending become understandable.
- Fatigue from ongoing cleanup.
- Nostalgia or seriousness around life-stage categories.
- Uncertainty when old labels no longer fit current family life.

Trust is gained when categories remain useful through change.

Trust is lost when historical comparisons feel unreliable.

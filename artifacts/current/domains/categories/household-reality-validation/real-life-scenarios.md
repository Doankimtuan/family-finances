# Real-Life Scenarios

## Best Case

A couple pays rent, groceries, transport, utilities, and salary through mostly clear bank and wallet records. Categories are broad and familiar.

User expects:

- Fast categorization.
- Monthly summaries that match memory.
- Both partners understand the labels.

Successful outcome:

- The household can answer what they spent money on without arguing over meaning.

## Typical Case

The household uses bank transfers, VietQR, cash, wallet payments, delivery apps, and e-commerce. Some transactions are clear; some require memory.

User expects:

- Obvious items to be easy.
- Unclear items to wait for later review.
- Small items not to require excessive precision.

Successful outcome:

- Most meaningful spending is categorized well enough for monthly conversation.

## Worst Case

Transactions pile up across many sources. Category names overlap. One partner classifies inconsistently. Cash spending is forgotten.

User expects:

- The system not to make the confusion worse.
- Historical records to remain interpretable.
- Wrong labels to be correctable.

Successful outcome:

- The household can recover enough meaning to trust high-level patterns, even if some details remain imperfect.

## Unexpected Case

A relative sends money, asks for repayment, or receives support. The transaction could be gift, reimbursement, loan, support, or shared bill.

User expects:

- Category language to respect real household context.
- The transaction not to be mistaken for ordinary salary or normal spending.

Successful outcome:

- Both partners understand the reason behind the family-related money movement.

## Emergency Case

A medical expense or urgent family support payment happens quickly, possibly with cash, transfer, card, and reimbursement later.

User expects:

- The event can be understood after the crisis.
- Category meaning can be corrected when more information is available.
- Reports do not distort normal monthly spending without context.

Successful outcome:

- The household preserves the event's meaning and can separate emergency pressure from ordinary patterns.

## Mixed Purchase Case

A supermarket or e-commerce order contains groceries, household supplies, baby items, and gifts.

User expects:

- A reasonable way to classify the purchase.
- The category summary not to feel wildly misleading.

Successful outcome:

- The household accepts the level of approximation or records enough context to remember the mixed purpose.

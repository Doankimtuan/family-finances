# Decision Points

## Create Category

Users decide whether a new label is needed.

Why it matters:

- Too many categories fragment understanding.
- Too few categories hide meaningful household patterns.
- New life stages often require new vocabulary.

## Select Category

Users decide what a transaction was for.

Why it matters:

- This is the core act that turns transaction facts into household meaning.
- Incorrect selection weakens reports and partner trust.

## Leave Uncategorized

Users decide not to classify yet.

Why it matters:

- This preserves uncertainty.
- Accumulated unknowns reduce confidence in category summaries.

## Edit Category Assignment

Users decide to correct a transaction's meaning.

Why it matters:

- Corrections are common and financially healthy.
- Repeated corrections may indicate unclear vocabulary.

## Rename Category

Users decide to change the label used for a meaning.

Why it matters:

- Better wording can improve shared understanding.
- Historical comparability may become less clear.

## Archive Or Stop Using Category

Users decide a category should not be used for future activity.

Why it matters:

- Old categories can clutter selection.
- Historical transactions may still need the old meaning.

## Merge Meaning

Users decide that two category labels represent the same household meaning.

Why it matters:

- Duplicate meanings fragment reports.
- Merging meaning can affect how past behavior is interpreted.

## Accept Provider Or Suggested Category

Users decide whether an external or inferred label is believable.

Why it matters:

- Trust is gained when suggestions match memory.
- Trust is lost when wrong labels silently shape reports.

## Override Category

Users decide that household meaning differs from provider or previous classification.

Why it matters:

- Household meaning may be more accurate than merchant type.
- Override behavior reveals the real taxonomy users need.

## Review Category Summary

Users decide whether category totals match reality.

Why it matters:

- This is where categories become useful for household awareness.
- A confusing summary can undermine confidence in the whole record.

## Approve Shared Meaning

Partners decide whether a category label is acceptable for shared interpretation.

Why it matters:

- Categories are social language, not only data.
- Disagreement can create friction around trust and fairness.

## Reject Or Question Meaning

Users decide that a category does not explain a transaction well enough.

Why it matters:

- Questioning prevents false certainty.
- Too many questions make categorization feel burdensome.

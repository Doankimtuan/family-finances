# Reality Check

## Real-World Behavior vs Current Domain Understanding

Phase 1 understands Loans as scheduled borrowing obligations with principal, lender, repayment schedule, interest, payments, and lifecycle states.

Real household behavior supports this strongly.

Young households may not speak in formal terms like amortization, but they naturally understand:

- We owe someone.
- We must pay every month.
- This affects salary and plans.
- Missing payment is bad.
- Completion is a relief.

## Already Validated

- Loans solve a real household problem: future income is already committed.
- Users naturally understand lender, amount owed, due date, monthly payment, and completion.
- Monthly affordability is the dominant mental model for young households.
- Partner visibility is important for trust.
- Vietnam-first households use both formal and informal loans.
- Separating Loans from ordinary expenses is behaviorally important.
- Separating scheduled Loans from open-ended Debts is behaviorally plausible.
- Separating Loans from credit-card revolving behavior is financially important.
- The domain remains relevant over 5-10 years as households take larger obligations.

## Assumptions Remaining

- How much amortization detail ordinary users want.
- Whether users can reliably provide interest rate and method.
- Whether fixed monthly and reducing-balance terms are understandable in Vietnam-first language.
- Whether family/friend scheduled loans should be tracked with the same emotional framing as bank loans.
- How often young households experience floating-rate changes in active product use.
- Whether early payoff is common enough to deserve deep attention in later phases.
- How much manual tracking users tolerate for small BNPL or store-financing plans.

## Still Needs Confirmation

- Interviews with Vietnamese users age 20-35 who have active loans.
- Observation of real loan contracts, app screens, SMS reminders, and repayment habits.
- Partner interviews about disclosure and shared responsibility.
- Vietnamese terminology testing for principal, outstanding balance, schedule, rate change, and early payoff.
- Evidence of how users handle missed, partial, or late payments.
- Evidence of how often provider data conflicts with user-entered records.

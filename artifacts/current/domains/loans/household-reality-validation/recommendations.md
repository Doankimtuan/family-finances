# Recommendations

This document contains classified observations only. It does not redesign the business, create Product Decisions, define implementation, or prioritize roadmap.

## Validated

- Loans are a real and recurring household concern.
- The domain solves a real problem: visible repayment obligation.
- Young households naturally understand monthly payment, lender, due date, and remaining obligation.
- Shared finance makes partner visibility important.
- Vietnam-first behavior includes formal bank/finance-company loans and informal family/friend loans.
- Loan repayment must be distinguishable from ordinary expense.
- The domain can remain meaningful for 5-10 years because borrowing complexity tends to increase with household maturity.

## Needs Review

- Whether the domain is too detailed for low-maturity users if amortization concepts are foregrounded.
- Whether family/friend loans need different language from institutional loans in future product decisions.
- Whether current domain terminology cleanly separates remaining principal from total outstanding balance.
- Whether credit-card installment language could confuse the Loans/Card boundary.
- Whether monthly-only repayment is sufficient for Vietnam-first household reality.

## Needs Research

- How target users describe loan concepts in Vietnamese without prompting.
- How often young households track informal loans.
- Whether users can obtain reliable payment schedules from providers.
- How often payments are partial, late, reversed, or posted later than the bank transfer.
- How often users face promotional-to-floating rate changes.
- How much manual tracking is acceptable for small installments.
- How couples negotiate private versus shared loan responsibility.

## Future Opportunity

- Better understanding of long-term mortgage behavior.
- Better understanding of children-related borrowing pressure.
- Better understanding of refinancing and restructuring behavior.
- Better understanding of lender-confirmed versus household-estimated truth.
- Better understanding of family-support obligations that blur gift, debt, and loan.
- Better understanding of how credit-history awareness changes with age and income.

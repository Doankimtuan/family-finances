# Product Risks

## Financial Risk

- Recorded remaining principal may be mistaken for lender-confirmed outstanding balance.
- Early payoff estimate may be mistaken for formal payoff quote.
- Interest, fee, insurance, and penalty handling may be incomplete.
- Payment recorded by household may not be posted by lender.

## UX Complexity Risk

- Amortization detail can overwhelm low-maturity users.
- Fixed monthly, reducing balance, promotional rate, and floating rate terminology can confuse users.
- Family loans need sensitive wording because the obligation is social as well as financial.

## Maintenance Risk

- Loan types, rate structures, and lender practices may expand over time.
- Provider-specific rules can create unbounded complexity if brought in too early.
- Advanced features such as refinancing, consolidation, and regulatory normalization carry long-term upkeep cost.

## Scalability Risk

- Monthly-only assumptions may need review later.
- Mortgage and vehicle loans may introduce collateral, guarantor, and insurance complexity.
- Multiple concurrent small installments can create clutter if not handled carefully in later phases.

## Education Risk

- Users may not understand principal versus outstanding balance.
- Users may not understand that promotional rates end.
- Users may not understand that loan repayment is not the same as original spending category.

## User Misunderstanding Risk

- Credit-card revolving balances may be confused with Loans.
- Virtual payoff intentions may be confused with real repayment.
- Health summaries may be mistaken for actions if not read-only.

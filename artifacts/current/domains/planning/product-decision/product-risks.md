# Product Risks

## Financial Risk

- Users may believe planned jar amounts are real reserved cash.
- Users may assume expected income has arrived.
- Users may assume recurring expectations are paid.
- Users may interpret calendar projection as provider-confirmed due truth.
- Debt payoff intention may be mistaken for loan or card balance truth.
- AI explanations may create false certainty if not strictly grounded in facts.

## UX Complexity Risk

- Jars, capacity, reallocation, rollover, and variance may be too abstract.
- Month review may feel like homework.
- Too many planning concepts may overwhelm low-maturity households.
- Future seasonal and irregular income support may increase setup burden.

## Maintenance Risk

- Recurring assumptions can become stale.
- Seasonal expense planning can create long-tail complexity.
- Provider-assisted detection and forecasting require ongoing reliability work.
- Cross-domain facts must preserve ownership boundaries as domains evolve.

## Scalability Risk

- Multiple periods, family support, education, insurance, and extended-family needs can stretch the simple Planning model.
- Long-term needs may tempt Planning to become wealth, advice, tax, or insurance planning.
- More provider integration increases stale-data and consent complexity.

## Education Risk

- Users need to understand planned amount versus balance.
- Users need to understand goal intention versus Savings product truth.
- Users need to understand expected bill versus paid bill.
- Vietnamese terminology for jars, review, allocation, and reallocation remains unvalidated.

## User Misunderstanding Risk

- Shared planning may feel like surveillance.
- Mismatch language may feel judgmental.
- Family-support planning may expose sensitive partner expectations.
- Emergency reallocation may be misunderstood as actual transfer.

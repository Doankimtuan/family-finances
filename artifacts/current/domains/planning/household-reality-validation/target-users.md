# Target Users

## Primary Users

Primary users are young adults aged 20-35 who participate in household financial decisions.

They may be:

- One partner who manages most household money.
- Both partners sharing decisions.
- A young parent coordinating income, bills, childcare, and savings.
- A person preparing to merge finances after marriage or moving in together.

The strongest fit is households that already feel tension between real account balance and committed money.

## Secondary Users

Secondary users include:

- A partner who checks the plan but rarely edits it.
- A family member who affects obligations through support, shared housing, childcare, loans, or emergency help.
- A household admin who manages shared policies.
- A future user who starts single and later becomes part of a shared household.

## Household Situations

### Single

The user is building habits before marriage or cohabitation. Planning is usually personal, salary-cycle based, and focused on rent, bills, savings, and major purchases.

Validation fit: moderate.

### Newly Married Or Living Together

The household is learning what is shared, what remains personal, and how to coordinate obligations. Planning helps reveal expectations before conflict appears.

Validation fit: strong.

### Young Family

The household has a child or is preparing for one. Planning becomes more serious because childcare, school, medical, insurance, housing, and family support create recurring pressure.

Validation fit: very strong.

### Family With Children

The household needs more calendar awareness and longer-term trade-offs. Planning is less about perfect categories and more about protecting important commitments.

Validation fit: very strong.

## Financial Maturity Levels

### Low Maturity

Users check balances, remember bills mentally, and react when money feels tight. They may not use formal budgeting terms.

Planning fit: useful if concepts remain practical and concrete.

### Medium Maturity

Users already track expenses, separate money, or discuss monthly plans. They understand goals and recurring obligations.

Planning fit: strong.

### High Maturity

Users use spreadsheets, personal finance apps, separate accounts, or envelope methods. They may expect more nuance around variance, irregular income, and long-term planning.

Planning fit: strong, with higher expectations.

## Life Stages

- First job or early career.
- Moving out.
- Living with partner.
- Marriage planning.
- First rented home.
- Pregnancy or newborn.
- Childcare and school years.
- First car or motorbike upgrade.
- Supporting parents.
- Buying a home.
- Growing income and asset complexity.

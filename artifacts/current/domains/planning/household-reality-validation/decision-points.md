# Decision Points

## Create

Users decide whether a plan, jar, goal, or recurring expectation is important enough to track.

Why it matters:

- Reveals what the household considers financially meaningful.
- Determines whether the plan reflects real priorities.

## Name

Users decide how to label a jar, goal, or recurring obligation.

Why it matters:

- Household recognition depends on everyday language.
- Poor labels reduce partner understanding.

## Classify

Users decide whether something is spending, saving, buffer, income, bill, goal, family support, or other intention.

Why it matters:

- Classification shapes how the household interprets priority and pressure.
- Misclassification can blur fact and intention.

## Allocate

Users decide how expected money is assigned to purposes.

Why it matters:

- Allocation is the core household planning act.
- It determines what feels safe, protected, or flexible.

## Edit

Users decide to change amount, timing, name, goal target, or allocation.

Why it matters:

- Real life changes often.
- Editing keeps intention aligned with current reality.

## Pause

Users decide an intention is temporarily inactive.

Why it matters:

- Helps households handle income shock, emergency, or shifting priority.
- Avoids pretending every goal remains equally active.

## Resume

Users decide to make a paused intention active again.

Why it matters:

- Represents recovery after stress or interruption.
- Restores household confidence.

## Archive Or Cancel

Users decide an intention no longer matters.

Why it matters:

- Keeps the active plan understandable.
- Prevents old intentions from creating false pressure.

## Reallocate

Users decide to move planned capacity from one purpose to another.

Why it matters:

- Reflects trade-off.
- Must remain understood as virtual, not a real money transfer.

## Review

Users decide to compare the plan with what happened.

Why it matters:

- Review turns planning into household learning.
- It reveals missed obligations and unrealistic assumptions.

## Approve

Users decide a planning period is understood enough to close or accept.

Why it matters:

- Creates shared confidence.
- May reduce future arguments about what the month meant.

## Correct

Users decide that prior planning information was wrong or incomplete.

Why it matters:

- Preserves trust.
- Normalizes imperfect household data.

## Reject Or Challenge

Users decide not to accept a partner-created plan, allocation, or assumption.

Why it matters:

- Shared finance requires agreement.
- Disagreement can reveal priority, privacy, or trust issues.

## Withdraw Or Fund In Real Life

Users decide to execute real money movement related to a plan.

Why it matters:

- Ownership changes to Accounts, Transactions, Savings, Cards, or Loans once real money moves.
- Planning remains intention context, not real money truth.

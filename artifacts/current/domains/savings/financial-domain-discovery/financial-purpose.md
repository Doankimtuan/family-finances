# Financial Purpose

## Why Savings Exists

Savings exists to convert household surplus into protected future liquidity. It solves the problem that money sitting in a daily spending account is too easy to spend, may earn little or no yield, and may not be consciously assigned to future resilience.

Savings products are not the same as savings intentions. A jar can express "emergency fund" or "new home deposit." A savings product is a real institution-held balance or contract that may or may not correspond to that intention.

## Financial Problems It Solves

- Preserves surplus from daily consumption.
- Earns low-risk interest or yield.
- Creates maturity checkpoints for deliberate reallocation.
- Improves household resilience through emergency liquidity.
- Helps households match money to time horizons.
- Reduces idle cash in low-yield accounts.
- Creates a formal record of principal, rate, provider, and expected settlement.

## Who Needs It

- Households with surplus after obligations and planned spending.
- Families building emergency reserves.
- Couples coordinating shared future commitments.
- Users managing fixed deposits, term deposits, high-yield savings, flexible savings, or manually tracked bank products.
- Users who need maturity reminders and renewal decisions.
- Users who need to know where real savings money is, not only what it is intended for.

## When It Is Used

- When money is moved from a liquid account into a savings product.
- When a provider pays interest.
- When a fixed-term product approaches maturity.
- When the household must choose to renew, switch, withdraw, or settle.
- When early withdrawal is considered due to emergency or opportunity cost.
- When reconciling provider-reported savings balances.
- When assessing household liquidity, yield, and concentration risk.

## When It Is Not Used

- To create a virtual allocation or budget intention.
- To move money between jars.
- To represent investment risk products such as stocks, funds, crypto, or securities accounts.
- To model debt payoff or installment obligations.
- To give regulated financial advice.
- To fabricate balances, interest, or settlement outcomes that the provider has not confirmed.
- To let Health write back recommendations or money movements.

## Behavior It Should Encourage

- Preserve emergency liquidity before discretionary long-term locking.
- Match product term to actual household time horizon.
- Review maturity deliberately.
- Compare expected yield with liquidity cost.
- Recognize provider and deposit insurance concentration.
- Keep real account truth separate from plan intention.
- Treat early withdrawal as a conscious decision with explicit cost.

## Behavior It Should Discourage

- Treating all savings as spendable cash.
- Double-counting savings as both a real balance and a jar balance.
- Auto-renewing without household awareness.
- Chasing yield without considering liquidity, provider risk, and deposit insurance limits.
- Hiding savings ownership from a partner when the product is household-relevant.
- Treating projected interest as already earned.
- Treating provider marketing rates as guaranteed after product creation unless contractually locked.

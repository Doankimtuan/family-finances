# Household Personas

## Newly Married Dual-Income Couple

Income: Two office salaries, often monthly, with Tet bonus or performance bonus.

Expenses: Rent, food, commuting, gifts, family visits, wedding debt or wedding recovery costs.

Saving habits: Saves immediately after salary when disciplined; otherwise saves whatever remains near month end. May open short 1-3 month bank deposits.

Debt habits: Usually credit card or family borrowing before formal loans.

Financial priorities: Emergency fund, rental deposit, future baby, first apartment.

Risk tolerance: Medium-low. They may chase higher interest, but fear losing liquidity.

## Couple With First Child

Income: One or two salaries; may temporarily drop to one income during pregnancy, birth, or childcare.

Expenses: Hospital, nutrition, childcare, diapers, vaccinations, grandparents support, larger housing.

Saving habits: More purpose-driven but more often interrupted.

Debt habits: May use installment payments for appliances, phone, motorbike, or medical expenses.

Financial priorities: Medical cushion, childcare stability, education reserve.

Risk tolerance: Low. Liquidity matters more than rate.

## Couple Buying First Apartment

Income: Stable professional salaries, sometimes with family support.

Expenses: Down payment, bank mortgage, furniture, maintenance, legal/admin fees.

Saving habits: Aggressive down-payment saving; may hold multiple term deposits matching expected payment milestones.

Debt habits: Mortgage or pre-mortgage borrowing from relatives.

Financial priorities: Down payment certainty, monthly cash-flow safety, debt approval.

Risk tolerance: Low on principal, medium on lock-up if payment timing is known.

## Couple Paying Mortgage

Income: Usually dual income, but vulnerable to job loss or rate changes.

Expenses: Mortgage, utilities, food, child/family support, transport.

Saving habits: Maintains buffer for installments; may pause long-term savings during high-interest periods.

Debt habits: Formal bank debt dominates.

Financial priorities: Never miss mortgage, build emergency buffer, refinance if rate changes.

Risk tolerance: Low. Savings is insurance against default.

## Freelancer or Gig-Income Family

Income: Irregular, project-based, platform-based, or seasonal.

Expenses: Normal household expenses plus business tools, insurance gaps, tax uncertainty.

Saving habits: Saves irregular lumps after large payments; prefers flexible savings or short terms.

Debt habits: Avoids formal debt when income documentation is weak; may borrow from relatives.

Financial priorities: Income smoothing and emergency liquidity.

Risk tolerance: Low on liquidity, medium on rate if funds are surplus.

## Single-Income Household

Income: One salary supports both partners or family members.

Expenses: Rent, groceries, transport, family obligations, medical risk.

Saving habits: Conservative; may keep cash/liquid savings over term deposits.

Debt habits: Avoids new debt unless necessary.

Financial priorities: Emergency fund and job-loss buffer.

Risk tolerance: Low.

## Young Household Supporting Parents

Income: One or two salaries, often with periodic transfers to parents.

Expenses: Parent medical support, sibling support, hometown visits, ceremonies.

Saving habits: Savings is repeatedly interrupted by family needs.

Debt habits: Family borrowing/lending is common and emotionally complex.

Financial priorities: Preserve dignity, support parents, avoid household conflict.

Risk tolerance: Low, but may keep too little for own household.

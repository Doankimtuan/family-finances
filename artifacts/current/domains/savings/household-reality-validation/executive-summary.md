# Executive Summary

Savings strongly fits young Vietnamese household behavior, but only if ViNha treats it as practical family resilience rather than as a bank product catalog.

For couples aged 20-35, savings is usually not a neat financial instrument strategy. It is salary protection, rent/deposit protection, pregnancy protection, child protection, parent-support protection, motorbike/home down-payment preparation, and emotional safety. The household often saves irregularly, uses several bank apps or e-wallet pockets, renews term deposits passively, and withdraws early when family events override yield.

The Phase 1 conclusion remains valid: Savings must be real money, not a jar. The Phase 2 validation adds behavioral nuance: households need the product to support fragmented, emotional, irregular, family-driven saving. A young family may not know whether they are optimizing yield, building an emergency fund, preparing for a baby, or simply trying not to spend the leftover salary. ViNha should help them see what the savings product is doing in household life without pretending the savings product itself is the goal.

Most current Savings capabilities reflect real behavior: funding, maturity, renewal, early withdrawal, provider/package tracking, and Inbox reminders all map to real situations. The most important missing behavior is not another financial product. It is household context: why the savings exists, who depends on it, how painful it would be to break it, and whether the family can survive the lock-up period.

Final validation: Savings should remain a core Money domain capability. It can survive 5-10 years if it stays grounded in family liquidity, maturity decisions, emergency exceptions, partner visibility, and provider-confirmed truth.

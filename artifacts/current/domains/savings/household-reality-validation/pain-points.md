# Pain Points

| Rank | Pain Point | Severity | Why It Matters |
|---:|---|---:|---|
| 1 | Hard to know what money is safely available | Very High | Families confuse bank balance, savings, and planned obligations. |
| 2 | Emergency destroys savings plan | Very High | Medical, job, and family shocks force withdrawals. |
| 3 | Forget maturity date | High | Passive renewal or idle money is common and costly. |
| 4 | Savings fragmented across providers | High | Digital deposits make multiple small products easy to forget. |
| 5 | Partner coordination is hard | High | One partner may save, spend, or withdraw without shared understanding. |
| 6 | Early withdrawal cost is unclear | High | Households may panic or avoid action due to uncertainty. |
| 7 | Cannot tell savings purpose from savings product | Medium-High | A bank product may be mentally tied to baby, rent, or emergency. |
| 8 | Family support disrupts plans | Medium-High | Financial decisions are relational, not purely individual. |
| 9 | Interest rates change | Medium | Households may renew at poor terms or chase rate without context. |
| 10 | Deposit insurance and provider risk are invisible | Medium | More important as savings grows. |
| 11 | Manual tracking diverges from bank truth | Medium | Creates distrust in the product. |
| 12 | Too many tiny decisions | Medium | Decision fatigue leads to ignored reminders. |

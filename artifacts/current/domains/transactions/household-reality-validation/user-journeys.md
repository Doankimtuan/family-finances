# User Journeys

## Happy Path

1. Money moves through bank, wallet, card, or cash.
2. The transaction is visible or recorded soon after.
3. The account, direction, amount, date, and purpose are clear.
4. The household understands whether it was expected.
5. The transaction supports planning or review without argument.
6. Later review confirms that the household memory and financial record match.

Successful outcome: the transaction helps answer "what happened to our money?"

## Common Path

1. Several payments happen during the week.
2. Some are remembered; some are forgotten.
3. One partner checks recent activity after noticing a lower balance.
4. Notes, merchant names, or categories help reconstruct the story.
5. A few items remain unclear and require partner discussion.
6. The household accepts approximate understanding for small items and higher confidence for large items.

Successful outcome: the household reaches enough clarity to move on.

## First-Time Usage

1. The user starts with recent salary, rent, food, transport, shopping, wallet, or card activity.
2. They expect transactions to look like bank or wallet history.
3. They may be confused by whether to record cash withdrawal, cash spending, credit card payment, or partner transfer.
4. They learn that transactions are facts, while categories and planning labels explain meaning.

Successful outcome: the user trusts the record as a financial memory aid.

## Long-Term Usage

1. The household builds months or years of activity history.
2. Repeated merchants, salary, rent, utilities, school, and subscriptions become recognizable patterns.
3. The household reviews less frequently when life is stable.
4. Review increases during stress: job change, baby, house purchase, medical event, debt, or unexpected cash shortage.
5. Historical transactions become evidence for decisions and conversations.

Successful outcome: transactions remain useful without becoming daily burden.

## Recovery Path

1. The household discovers a wrong amount, duplicate, missing cash item, refund, or mistaken category.
2. The user compares memory, bank app, wallet app, receipt, or partner explanation.
3. The record is clarified.
4. Trust is restored if the correction is understandable and history still feels honest.

Successful outcome: the household can explain the discrepancy without losing trust in the record.

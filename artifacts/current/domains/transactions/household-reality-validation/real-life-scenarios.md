# Real-Life Scenarios

## Best Case

A newly married couple records salary, rent, groceries, QR payments, and e-wallet spending during the month. Both partners can review recent activity and understand where money went.

User expects:

- Recent activity is easy to scan.
- Income and expenses are visually and conceptually distinct.
- Shared spending is clear enough for discussion.

Successful outcome:

- The couple agrees on the household money story for the month.

## Typical Case

One partner pays for groceries, food delivery, and transport. The other pays rent, utilities, and family transfers. Some cash spending is missing.

User expects:

- The record explains most large and repeated movements.
- Small cash gaps do not make the entire system feel useless.
- Unknown items can be reviewed later.

Successful outcome:

- The household reaches practical confidence, even if not perfect accounting precision.

## Worst Case

The household has many missing cash expenses, duplicate manual entries, confusing card payments, late refunds, and partner disagreement about what counts as shared spending.

User expects:

- The product should not make confusion worse.
- The record should show what is known and what is uncertain.
- Corrections should not erase the truth.

Successful outcome:

- The household can separate real mistakes from ordinary complexity and recover trust.

## Unexpected Case

An e-commerce order is cancelled. The original payment was made through an e-wallet linked to a bank account, but the refund returns days later with a different description.

User expects:

- The original payment and later refund can both be understood.
- The refund does not look like new income.
- The household can explain why the balance changed.

Successful outcome:

- The event is understood as a returned expense, not extra money.

## Emergency Case

A child gets sick and the household pays hospital, pharmacy, transport, and family support costs across cash, bank transfer, and card.

User expects:

- Recording can wait until stress drops.
- Large expenses can still be reconstructed.
- The record helps the household understand emergency impact later.

Successful outcome:

- After the emergency, the household can see the financial damage clearly enough to adjust future decisions.

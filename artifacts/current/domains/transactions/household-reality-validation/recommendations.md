# Recommendations

This document contains classified observations only. It does not redesign the business, create Product Decisions, or define implementation.

## Validated

- Transactions as factual money movement match household mental models.
- The question "what happened to our money?" is real and recurring.
- Young households need account-linked transaction history.
- Income and expense are naturally understood.
- Review of unclear transactions is normal household behavior.
- Refunds, corrections, and late explanations are real-world needs.
- Transactions provide necessary input to planning, inbox, health, cards, savings, and loans.
- The real versus virtual distinction is behaviorally important.
- The domain remains useful over 5-10 years.

## Needs Review

- Whether transfer-like movement is sufficiently represented in household language.
- Whether correction, reversal, refund, and adjustment terms are understandable to ordinary users.
- Whether users perceive manual transaction capture as acceptable for cash and missing digital activity.
- Whether category and jar mapping are distinct enough in user understanding.
- Whether transaction status language matches household expectations.
- Whether personal and shared transaction visibility needs clearer validation before product decisions.

## Needs Research

- One-month transaction diaries from Vietnamese target households.
- Partner conversations around unknown transactions.
- Cash leakage patterns in young families.
- E-wallet and bank app source-of-truth expectations.
- Merchant description patterns in Vietnam.
- Refund and cancellation behavior for e-commerce and wallet payments.
- Credit card purchase versus card bill mental model.
- How often split-purpose purchases create real frustration.
- Vietnamese terminology testing.

## Future Opportunity

- Better support for provider-sourced transaction evidence as the Vietnam ecosystem matures.
- Richer understanding of transfers, reimbursements, and family support.
- More nuanced treatment of pending, posted, refunded, disputed, and corrected activity.
- Stronger long-term transaction memory as households add children, loans, home ownership, and multiple accounts.
- Improved confidence around cash, receipts, and cross-provider reconciliation.

These are observations only, not roadmap priorities.

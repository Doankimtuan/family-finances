# Emotional Journey

## Beginning

When money moves, users may feel neutral, pleased, anxious, or rushed.

Examples:

- Salary received: relief and confidence.
- Rent paid: responsibility and mild stress.
- Small purchase: little emotion in the moment.
- Medical payment: urgency and worry.
- Unknown charge: anxiety.

Trust is gained when the transaction appears as expected. Trust is lost when it is missing, duplicated, delayed, or cryptic.

## Review

During review, users want recognition: "Yes, I remember this."

Feelings:

- Confidence when recent activity matches memory.
- Stress when spending is higher than expected.
- Regret after impulse purchases.
- Uncertainty when notes or merchants are unclear.
- Friction when partner explanation is needed.

Trust is gained when the record helps explain reality without blame.

## Shared Discussion

Transactions can become emotional evidence in partner conversations.

Feelings:

- Relief when both partners agree.
- Defensiveness when personal spending is questioned.
- Appreciation when one partner has kept clear records.
- Tension when transfers or family support have different meanings.

Trust is gained when the domain supports shared understanding. Trust is lost when it feels like surveillance or accusation.

## Correction Or Recovery

When something is wrong, users need confidence that the history can be explained.

Feelings:

- Anxiety when totals do not match.
- Frustration when duplicates or refunds are hard to trace.
- Relief after matching the bank, wallet, receipt, or partner memory.
- Renewed trust when corrections are transparent.

Trust is lost if a correction feels like silent rewriting.

## Long-Term

Over months and years, transactions can feel like either a burden or a family memory.

Positive long-term emotions:

- Control.
- Calm.
- Preparedness.
- Shared accountability.

Negative long-term emotions:

- Shame.
- Fatigue.
- Avoidance.
- Fear of judgment.

The domain remains emotionally healthy when it helps households understand money without demanding perfection.

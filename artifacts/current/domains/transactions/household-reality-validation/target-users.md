# Target Users

## Primary Users

Young households where at least one person is responsible for understanding daily money movement.

Common primary users:

- A partner who tracks spending after payments.
- A household steward who reviews transactions weekly or monthly.
- A newly married partner trying to build shared visibility.
- A young parent trying to understand rising household costs.
- A financially cautious user who wants proof rather than memory.

## Secondary Users

Secondary users interact with the domain less often but shape its reality:

- The other partner who creates many transactions but may not categorize them.
- A family member who sends or receives money.
- A parent who contributes childcare, housing, or emergency support.
- A user with side income, freelance income, or reimbursements.
- A partner who only reviews when something feels wrong.

## Household Situations

## Single

Money is personal, but transaction tracking helps prepare for rent, dating, travel, savings, and future shared obligations.

The user may tolerate simple manual tracking if it answers "where did my salary go?"

## Dating Or Cohabiting

Money is partly personal and partly shared. Transactions may include split bills, transfers, meals, rent, travel, and gifts.

Ambiguity increases because a transfer can mean repayment, contribution, support, or personal debt.

## Newly Married

Shared finance becomes more explicit. Salary, rent, household shopping, bills, savings, insurance, and family support become joint topics.

Transactions become evidence in household discussion.

## Young Family

Children increase transaction volume and emotional stakes. School, milk, healthcare, childcare, transport, family visits, and emergency spending create more frequent review needs.

The household wants practical clarity, not formal accounting complexity.

## Family With Children

Transactions become long-term memory. Parents need to understand recurring obligations, education costs, medical spending, and cash leakage.

The domain remains relevant because life becomes more complex, not simpler.

## Financial Maturity Levels

## Low Maturity

Users check balances and remember only big purchases. Transactions are useful when presented as simple facts.

## Medium Maturity

Users review categories, recurring costs, and large expenses. They can understand refunds, transfers, and card payments with plain language.

## High Maturity

Users reconcile statements, separate accounts, monitor cash flow, and expect auditability. They may notice missing states such as pending, posted, transfer, reimbursement, and duplicate records.

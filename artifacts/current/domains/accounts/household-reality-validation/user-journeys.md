# User Journeys

## Happy Path

1. The household identifies its real containers: main bank account, cash, e-wallets, savings, and card obligations.
2. Each partner recognizes the account labels.
3. Income arrives in a known account.
4. Spending is recorded against the account where money actually moved.
5. The real position stays close to bank and cash reality.
6. Both partners can answer "where is our money?" without opening several apps.

Behavioral validation:

- This journey fits households that already discuss money and tolerate light tracking.
- It is most natural when account names match household language.

## Common Path

1. One partner adds the obvious accounts.
2. Cash and small e-wallet balances are added late or ignored.
3. Transactions are captured after the fact.
4. Some transfers are confusing.
5. The household checks bank apps when a number feels uncertain.
6. The product remains useful if the real position is directionally trusted.

Behavioral validation:

- This is the most likely path for young households.
- Account truth is not a one-time setup; it is a trust habit.

## First-Time Usage

1. The user starts with one account, usually salary bank or cash.
2. The user may not know whether to add every e-wallet or only important accounts.
3. The partner may ask why an individual account is part of household money.
4. The user tests whether the total feels right.
5. If the initial total is understandable, confidence increases.

Behavioral validation:

- First-time users do not need full financial precision to understand the domain.
- They need the concept to match the mental model of "where money is."

## Long-Term Usage

1. New accounts appear as income, savings, loans, cards, and family responsibilities grow.
2. Old accounts become less relevant but still explain history.
3. The household learns which accounts need regular attention.
4. The less-active partner relies on summaries and occasional review.
5. Account accuracy affects trust in planning and health views.

Behavioral validation:

- The domain remains relevant as households become more complex.
- The need shifts from setup to maintenance and reconciliation.

## Recovery Path

1. The account total feels wrong.
2. The user checks bank apps, wallet apps, and cash.
3. The discrepancy is traced to missed spending, duplicate entry, refund, transfer, wrong account, or stale cash count.
4. The record is corrected or reconciled.
5. Trust returns if the explanation is clear.

Behavioral validation:

- Recovery is emotionally important.
- Users tolerate manual checking when money confidence is at stake.

# Emotional Journey

## Beginning

Typical emotions:

- Curiosity.
- Mild anxiety.
- Hope for clarity.
- Resistance to setup effort.

Trust is gained when:

- The first account label feels familiar.
- The starting total resembles reality.
- The partner understands the same account names.

Trust is lost when:

- Setup feels like accounting homework.
- The product asks for details users do not know.
- Personal accounts feel exposed without shared context.

## Active Use

Typical emotions:

- Confidence when totals match memory.
- Relief when scattered money becomes visible.
- Low-grade stress when balances are stale.
- Friction when small transactions are tedious.

Trust is gained when:

- Account totals explain real-world decisions.
- Transfers, refunds, and cash changes are understandable.
- The product does not confuse money with plans.

Trust is lost when:

- The balance is wrong and the reason is unclear.
- Credit limit appears like spendable money.
- The household record disagrees with bank apps too often.

## Conflict Moments

Typical emotions:

- Defensiveness.
- Suspicion.
- Embarrassment.
- Fear of being judged.

Trust is gained when:

- Account history makes events explainable.
- Both partners can see the same reality.
- Mistakes are treated as record issues, not character flaws.

Trust is lost when:

- One partner controls all account truth.
- Closed or personal accounts create hidden-money suspicion.
- Manual errors look like dishonesty.

## Recovery

Typical emotions:

- Uncertainty.
- Concentration.
- Relief.
- Achievement.

Trust is gained when:

- Discrepancies are traced to specific causes.
- A corrected account feels aligned again.
- The household can return to planning with confidence.

Trust is lost when:

- Reconciliation is too hard.
- Old data disappears.
- The account total cannot be explained.

## Long-Term

Typical emotions:

- Stability.
- Pride.
- Occasional fatigue.
- Greater seriousness as family obligations grow.

Trust is gained when:

- The domain keeps making sense through marriage, children, income growth, and new obligations.
- Historical accounts remain understandable.
- The household can see growth without losing day-to-day clarity.

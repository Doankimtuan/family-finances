# Recommendations

This document contains classified observations only. It does not redesign the business, create Product Decisions, or define implementation.

## Validated

- Accounts as real containers match household mental models.
- The distinction between real account balance and virtual planning allocation is behaviorally important.
- Young households need a shared answer to "where is the money?"
- Bank accounts, cash, e-wallets, savings, and credit obligations are all relevant to Vietnam-first households.
- Account trust affects every downstream domain.
- Long-term family growth increases, not decreases, the need for account clarity.

## Needs Review

- Whether credit cards should be presented as accounts, card products, or both in future product decisions.
- Whether savings products and flexible savings accounts are sufficiently distinguishable in household language.
- Whether account type labels fit Vietnam-first terminology.
- Whether manually recorded balances need stronger freshness interpretation in future phases.
- Whether account archive/closure language is clear to ordinary households.

## Needs Research

- How target households divide personal and shared account visibility.
- How many accounts users are willing to maintain manually.
- Whether e-wallet balances are worth tracking for most households.
- How users understand current balance, available balance, and credit availability.
- How couples resolve mismatch between product records and bank apps.
- What Vietnamese labels users naturally use for bank accounts, wallets, and savings products.

## Future Opportunity

- More precise handling of multi-account, multi-bank households.
- Better understanding of family-held money and intergenerational support.
- Stronger treatment of liquidity as households take on children, mortgages, and long-term obligations.
- Read-only provider data may become more useful if Vietnam account connectivity matures.
- Multi-currency may become relevant for travel, remote income, study abroad, and remittance.

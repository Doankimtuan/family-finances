# Reality Check

## Real-World Behavior vs Current Domain Understanding

The current domain understanding states that Accounts are real containers and should remain separate from planning intentions. Real household behavior supports this strongly.

Young households already think in account-like containers:

- Main bank.
- Salary account.
- Cash.
- Momo or other wallet.
- Savings.
- Credit card.
- Partner's account.

They may not use formal product terms, but the underlying mental model is natural.

## Already Validated

- Accounts solve the real problem of scattered money.
- Users naturally understand "where money is."
- Cash, bank accounts, and wallets are meaningful household containers.
- Account truth is prerequisite to planning trust.
- Partner visibility is a real emotional and practical need.
- Manual tracking is acceptable for major accounts and high-value moments.
- Credit and savings require careful distinction from ordinary cash-like accounts.
- The domain can survive 5-10 years because households accumulate more containers over time.

## Assumptions Remaining

- How many e-wallets users are willing to track.
- Whether young couples want all personal accounts visible or only household-relevant ones.
- Whether "checking" is understandable enough in Vietnam-first language.
- Whether users expect credit cards inside or outside the account mental model.
- How often account balances need freshness indicators to preserve trust.
- Whether account holder identity matters early or only later.
- How strongly users care about institution metadata and account masks.

## Still Needs Confirmation

- Interviews with young Vietnamese couples at different life stages.
- Observation of actual account lists and bank/wallet usage.
- Validation of terminology in Vietnamese and English.
- Behavioral test of manual setup tolerance.
- Study of how couples negotiate personal versus shared money.
- Evidence on cash-tracking fatigue over months.
- Evidence on wallet tracking importance versus perceived clutter.

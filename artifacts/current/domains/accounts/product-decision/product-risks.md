# Product Risks

## Financial Risks

- Credit cards may be misunderstood as owned money if account adjacency is unclear.
- Manual starting balances may be wrong.
- Transfers may distort income or expense if not clearly treated.
- Stale balances may create false confidence.
- Savings and liquidity distinctions may be misunderstood.

## UX Complexity Risks

- Too many account types can overwhelm users.
- Ownership metadata can feel invasive.
- Reconciliation can feel like accounting homework.
- Freshness indicators can create anxiety if overdone.

## Maintenance Risks

- Provider integrations would create reliability and support burden.
- Multi-currency would create exchange-rate and reporting complexity.
- Detailed liquidity states could become hard to keep consistent.
- Account sub-classification could duplicate Savings, Cards, Loans, and Wealth boundaries.

## Scalability Risks

- Account lists grow over years.
- Closed accounts must remain meaningful.
- Young households may become families with more products, accounts, and obligations.

## Education Risks

- Users may not distinguish account balance, available balance, recorded balance, and jar allocation.
- Vietnam-first labels need validation.
- Less-active partners may need simpler concepts than financially active partners.

## User Misunderstanding Risks

- Product record may be mistaken for legal bank truth.
- Wallet promotional value may be mistaken for withdrawable money.
- Personal-account visibility may be mistaken as surveillance.
- Health summaries may be over-trusted if account data is stale.

# First-Time Experience

## Assessment

First-time experience is promising but needs explicit concept staging. A young family can understand the five surfaces at a glance if the product speaks in ordinary household language:

- Home: What is happening now.
- Money: Where real money is and what moved.
- Plan: What money is meant to do.
- Inbox: What needs a decision.
- Together: Who shares this household.
- Health: What the current condition looks like.

The approved onboarding principle, "3 essentials before Home", is correct. The risk is trying to set up too much financial structure before users have seen the product value.

## Confusing Concepts

| Concept | Risk | Required Education |
|---------|------|--------------------|
| Account | Users may think only bank accounts count, excluding cash or cards | "Where money is held or owed" |
| Jar | Users may think it is a bank sub-account | "A plan for money, not a place money sits" |
| Goal | Users may treat progress as proof of cash | "A future intention with visible progress" |
| Saving | Users may confuse savings product with jar or goal | "Real money under a savings product contract" |
| Investment | Users may treat estimated value as spendable | "Risk-bearing value, not cash until realized" |
| Inbox | Users may read it as notifications | "Only things needing a decision" |
| Health | Users may read score as instruction | "A read-only mirror of visible facts" |

## Cognitive Load Hotspots

- Home can overload users if it shows real position, plan pulse, Inbox, Health, and setup guidance at once.
- Plan can overload users if Jars, Goals, Recurring, and Month Ritual appear as equal concepts on day 1.
- Money can overload users if Accounts, Transactions, Savings, Cards, and Loans compete without a visible hierarchy.
- Together Policies can overload couples with abstract governance before trust has formed.

## Recommendations

### PX-FT-01: Stage day-0 Home

Problem: First-time Home may show too many empty or abstract modules.

User Impact: New users may not know whether to add accounts, record expenses, create jars, or invite a partner first.

Affected Screens: Onboarding Wizard, Home.

Frequency: First session.

Business Impact: High activation impact.

Recommended UX: Day-0 Home should show only three essentials: add first real money container, choose starter plan, invite partner. Health should remain quiet until enough facts exist.

Implementation Cost: Medium.

Priority: P0.

### PX-FT-02: Teach BR-01 through layout

Problem: Real money and virtual intention are financially separate but visually easy to blur.

User Impact: Users may believe Plan movements move bank money.

Affected Screens: Home, Money Hub, Plan Hub, Jars, Goals.

Frequency: Daily.

Business Impact: High trust and safety impact.

Recommended UX: Place real-money summaries above intention summaries on Home. Use stable section labels: "Real money" for Accounts/Transactions and "Planned use" for Jars/Goals.

Implementation Cost: Low.

Priority: P0.

### PX-FT-03: Delay advanced policy setup

Problem: Household policy settings are meaningful but abstract before daily use.

User Impact: Couples may choose settings blindly.

Affected Screens: Onboarding Wizard, Together Policies.

Frequency: First session and rare changes.

Business Impact: Medium trust impact.

Recommended UX: Use safe defaults during onboarding; introduce policy editing only when the user first encounters overspend, ritual mode, or partner-visible change.

Implementation Cost: Low.

Priority: P1.

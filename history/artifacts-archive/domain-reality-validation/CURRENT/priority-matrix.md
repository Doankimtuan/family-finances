### Score Legend
- **BV** — Business Value (1-10): How much does this drive acquisition, retention, or monetization?
- **UV** — User Value (1-10): How much does this improve the user's financial life?
- **CX** — Complexity (1-10): How hard is this to implement? (Higher = harder)
- **MC** — Migration Cost (1-10): How much existing data/behavior would break?
- **AI** — Architecture Impact (1-10): How much does this change the domain architecture?
- **RK** — Risk (1-10): What could go wrong? (Higher = riskier)
- **FI** — Future Importance (1-10): How important will this be in 2-3 years?
- **LO** — Longevity (1-10): Will this still matter in 5 years?

**Score Formula:** `(BV + UV + FI + LO) / (CX + MC + AI + RK) * 10`
This formula prioritizes opportunities with high value and low cost/risk. Combined value (BV+UV+FI+LO) divided by combined cost/risk (CX+MC+AI+RK), scaled by 10.

### Top 10 Analysis

**Pattern 1: Categories and Planning Dominate**
Three of the top 10 are in Categories and Planning — the two domains identified as having the largest competitive gaps. Auto-categorization (EO-01) is the single highest-priority evolution opportunity.

**Pattern 2: Inbox Needs Operational Maturity**
Two Inbox opportunities (EO-07, EO-16) appear in the top 10. The Inbox decision queue is architecturally sound but will need batch operations and auto-resolution as transaction volume grows.

**Pattern 3: Card Financial Safety**
Card payment due dates (EO-02) is high priority for user financial health. Missing payments has severe consequences. This is a safety feature, not a nice-to-have.

**Pattern 4: Transaction Completeness**
Transaction split support (EO-20) enables accurate categorization of multi-purpose purchases. Without splits, users are forced to choose a single category for mixed transactions (e.g., one receipt with groceries and household items).

## Do Not Implement (Dangerous or Misguided)

These ideas were surfaced during validation and are explicitly NOT recommended:

### DNI-01: Health Write-Back
**Idea:** Health domain automatically adjusts jar allocations to improve health score.
**Why Dangerous:** Violates BR-14 (Health must not write). Health should reflect, not act. Auto-adjusting allocations removes human agency and undermines the planning process.
**Board Position:** Do not implement. Enforce BR-14 strictly.

### DNI-02: Jar-to-Account Mapping
**Idea:** Link jars directly to bank accounts so "jar balance = account balance for that purpose."
**Why Dangerous:** Violates BR-01 (Real ≠ Virtual). Jar allocations are intentions; account balances are facts. Mapping them suggests a relationship that doesn't exist. Money is fungible.
**Board Position:** Do not implement. The BR-01 boundary is ViNha's core architectural principle.

### DNI-03: Automatic Transaction Categorization with No Override
**Idea:** AI categorizes all transactions with no user review or correction capability.
**Why Dangerous:** AI categorizations are probabilistic and can be wrong. Removing human override removes accountability for financial classification. Users will lose trust if categories are wrong and can't be fixed.
**Board Position:** Do not implement. Auto-categorization must be suggest-with-override, not auto-commit. This is the Copilot model and it works.

### DNI-04: Partner Spending Comparison
**Idea:** Show "Partner A spent X, Partner B spent Y" comparison charts.
**Why Dangerous:** Financial comparison between partners creates judgment, resentment, and score-keeping. This undermines the Together philosophy of shared financial management.
**Board Position:** Do not implement. Financial transparency is good; financial comparison between partners is toxic.

### DNI-05: Month Ritual Auto-Skip
**Idea:** Allow users to permanently disable the month ritual.
**Why Dangerous:** The ritual is a core behavioral mechanism. Making it permanently optional removes the regular review cadence that supports healthy financial behavior. The ritual may need simplification (quick close option) but should not be removable.
**Board Position:** Do not implement. Offer lighter-touch ritual options; do not allow complete removal.

### DNI-06: Health Score Leaderboards
**Idea:** Compare household health scores to other households (anonymized, percentile rankings).
**Why Dangerous:** Financial comparison creates anxiety and shame. A household in difficult financial circumstances doesn't need to know they're in the bottom percentile. Health is personal, not competitive.
**Board Position:** Do not implement. Health is a household mirror, not a competition.

### DNI-07: Investment Recommendations
**Idea:** Health domain suggests specific investments based on financial profile.
**Why Dangerous:** ViNha is not a financial advisor. Making investment recommendations creates regulatory liability and potential user harm. BR-14 allows explanation, not prescription.
**Board Position:** Do not implement. Financial advice requires regulatory compliance ViNha should not seek.

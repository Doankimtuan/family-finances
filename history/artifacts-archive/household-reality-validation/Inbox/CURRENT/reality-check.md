# Reality Check

## Real-World Behavior vs Current Domain Understanding

The Phase 1 understanding is broadly aligned with household reality. Young households naturally experience financial open loops: unclear transactions, reminders, savings decisions, refunds, transfers, and partner-context questions.

The strongest match is the concept that Inbox answers "what needs our attention before our money picture is trustworthy?"

## Already Validated

- Inbox solves a real problem: unresolved financial decisions accumulate.
- Young households understand the pain of unclear transactions.
- Shared finance creates partner-context gaps.
- A queue model matches real review behavior.
- Recent items are easier to resolve than old items.
- Savings maturity and bill reminders can require household attention.
- Backlog risk is real.
- The distinction between financial action and review state is important.
- The domain remains relevant as households mature.

## Assumptions That Remain

- Users will understand "Inbox" as financial decisions rather than messages.
- Households will process items regularly enough to preserve trust.
- Payment reminders belong in Inbox only when they require decision or acknowledgement.
- Users can distinguish resolved, acknowledged, dismissed, expired, and archived states.
- Partner visibility will feel helpful rather than controlling.
- Suggestions and auto-resolution will be trusted only when they match household memory.
- Manual review effort will feel worthwhile for enough items.

## Still Needs Confirmation

- Real Inbox backlog tolerance for Vietnamese couples.
- Natural Vietnamese wording for Inbox, review item, resolve, acknowledge, dismiss, and expired.
- Whether young households prefer individual or shared processing.
- How sensitive users are about personal spending becoming shared attention.
- Which source channels matter most: bank app, wallet app, SMS, email, receipt, invoice, chat, or manual entry.
- Whether receipt and e-invoice evidence matters for everyday households.
- How often provider duplicate messages occur.
- Whether users interpret an empty Inbox correctly.

## Over-Engineering Check

Potentially over-engineered areas:

- Too many status concepts for casual households.
- Too many item types if exposed as business language.
- Confidence scores if users read them as guarantees.
- Archive history if it does not support real trust.

These are validation observations only.

## Missing-Concept Check

Possibly missing from the current understanding:

- Household disagreement behavior.
- Duplicate-provider-message reality.
- Missing-context recovery.
- Privacy boundaries for personal versus shared items.
- Evidence importance for receipts and e-invoices.

These observations do not imply product changes.

# Decision Points

## Create

The household decides whether a card should be represented as part of shared finances.

Why it matters:

- A card can create household obligations even when it is legally owned by one person.
- Visibility affects trust and repayment planning.

## Edit

The household changes card facts such as name, limit, statement date, due date, or linked repayment account.

Why it matters:

- Incorrect facts can cause wrong expectations about available credit or repayment timing.

## Use for Purchase

The user decides whether to pay with card, cash, QR transfer, bank transfer, or e-wallet.

Why it matters:

- Card choice affects timing, rewards, fees, credit obligation, and household visibility.

## Record or Review Purchase

The household decides whether a card purchase is worth tracking or reviewing.

Why it matters:

- Small purchases create noise; large or shared purchases affect trust and planning.

## Categorize

The household decides what the purchase was for.

Why it matters:

- Card merchant names often hide the real household purpose.
- Categories affect budget understanding and partner communication.

## Mark Shared or Personal

The household decides whether a card item belongs to shared finances.

Why it matters:

- Shared repayment expectations depend on this distinction.

## Convert to Installment

The cardholder decides whether a purchase should become scheduled future payments.

Why it matters:

- The decision changes household cash-flow pressure and blurs the boundary between card spending and loan-like obligation.

## Pay Full Statement

The household decides to repay the full statement balance.

Why it matters:

- This usually preserves the card as a convenience tool rather than long-term debt.

## Pay Partial or Minimum

The household decides to pay less than the full statement amount.

Why it matters:

- This can create interest, fees, stress, and future affordability pressure.

## Schedule or Time Payment

The household decides when to repay.

Why it matters:

- Timing depends on salary, partner contribution, bank account liquidity, and due date.

## Review

The household checks statement items, due amount, fees, refunds, and unfamiliar charges.

Why it matters:

- Review is where trust is gained or lost.

## Approve

The household accepts a card item, bill, or repayment as understood.

Why it matters:

- Approval lets the household move on without lingering uncertainty.

## Reject or Dispute

The household challenges a card item, fee, or unexplained charge.

Why it matters:

- Disputes affect money, trust, and provider confidence.

## Pause or Stop Use

The household stops using a card temporarily.

Why it matters:

- The card may still have subscriptions, fees, pending refunds, or outstanding debt.

## Close

The cardholder closes the card or lets it expire without renewal.

Why it matters:

- Closure affects history, repayments, refunds, fees, and future credit access.

# Cross-Domain Impact

## Accounts

Debit cards depend on payment accounts. Credit-card repayments come from bank accounts, cash-like accounts, or other real money sources. Credit-card outstanding should not be treated as liquid account balance.

## Transactions

Card purchases, repayments, refunds, fees, interest, cashback, and corrections become transaction-like household events. Card timing can differ from ordinary transaction timing because authorization, posting, statementing, and repayment are separate moments.

## Categories

Card merchant names often need household interpretation. Categories help users understand what card spending was for, especially when one statement aggregates many purchases.

## Goals

Card repayment affects available money for goals. Card-funded purchases may support goals, compete with goals, or create short-term pressure that delays goal funding.

## Planning

Statement due dates and expected repayments influence monthly cash-flow planning. Card installment schedules can create future recurring pressure.

## Inbox

Card-related items may require review when due dates approach, unfamiliar charges appear, refunds post, installments complete, or repayment status is uncertain. Inbox owns the decision queue, not the card obligation itself.

## Health

Health can read card utilization, missed payment patterns, interest burden, and reliance on credit as signals. Health should remain interpretive and read-only.

## Together

Cards affect partner trust because one person's card can fund shared expenses and create household repayment obligations. Supplementary cards make attribution and responsibility more sensitive.

## Loans

Card installments can resemble loans. The interaction matters when a card purchase becomes scheduled repayment, but ordinary card-cycle debt remains card behavior.

## Debts

Informal partner reimbursement can occur after one person pays with a card. The card issuer obligation and the interpersonal obligation are different real-world concerns.

## Savings

Households may use savings to repay card debt in emergencies. Card debt can also prevent regular saving if repayment pressure grows.

## External Providers

Issuers, card networks, acquirers, payment gateways, and statement providers hold external truth about card activity. Household records depend on their availability, timing, and terminology.

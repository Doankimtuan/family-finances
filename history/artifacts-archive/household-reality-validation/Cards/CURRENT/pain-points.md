# Pain Points

## Current Frustrations

- Card statements often arrive after spending has already been mentally forgotten.
- Issuer app numbers may not match household records.
- Refunds and reversals can take days or weeks.
- Credit-card repayments can feel like expenses even though the purchase was the original expense.
- One partner may not know what the other bought.
- Annual fees, late fees, interest, and FX charges can feel hidden.
- Installment purchases make total obligation harder to see.
- Card-on-file subscriptions are easy to forget.

## Confusing Concepts

- Credit limit versus available money.
- Statement balance versus outstanding balance.
- Statement date versus due date.
- Minimum payment versus full payment.
- Pending authorization versus posted transaction.
- Refund approval versus refund posting.
- Cashback as reward, discount, income, or statement credit.
- Debit card versus credit card.
- Card account versus linked bank account.

## Unnecessary Complexity

- Full card-network terminology is not natural for ordinary households.
- BIN, acquirer, clearing, interchange, and settlement details are usually irrelevant to household behavior.
- Detailed interest formulas are often too complex unless the user is already in revolving debt.
- Prepaid-card depth may be less relevant for many young urban households than debit, credit, and QR usage.
- Multiple card lifecycle states may be excessive unless loss, replacement, expiry, or fraud occurs.

## Missing Visibility

- What the household will actually need to pay this month.
- Whether a card purchase is shared or personal.
- Whether a refund affects the current bill or a later bill.
- Whether payment has merely been sent or posted by issuer.
- Whether a card is silently accumulating fees.
- Whether a purchase converted to installment still creates future monthly pressure.
- Whether card spending is crowding out goals or jars.

## Potential Misunderstandings

- Users may believe paying the source bank account means the card is already settled.
- Users may believe a partial payment stops all interest.
- Users may believe a credit balance is spendable cash.
- Users may treat card rewards as profit even when they encourage overspending.
- Users may interpret available credit as affordability.
- Users may assume supplementary-card users share legal responsibility.

No solutions are proposed in this validation phase.

# Real-Life Scenarios

## Best Case

A newly married couple creates an emergency fund goal, contributes after both salary dates, and reaches the target within a year.

User expectations:

- The goal feels protected from daily spending.
- Progress is easy to understand.
- Both partners know why the goal matters.

Successful outcome:

- The household feels safer.
- Partners trust the progress.
- Completion reinforces the habit of shared planning.

## Typical Case

A couple saves for travel, contributes when salary is left over, skips months with high bills, and adjusts the target when flight prices change.

User expectations:

- The goal can change without drama.
- Missed contributions do not feel like failure.
- The couple can still see whether the trip is realistic.

Successful outcome:

- The household makes an informed decision about timing and affordability.
- The goal remains useful even with imperfect tracking.

## Worst Case

A family believes a baby fund is nearly complete, but the money was counted across the main bank account, cash, and another savings goal. When expenses arrive, the real available money is lower than expected.

User expectations:

- Goal progress should not mislead them.
- The household wants to understand what happened without blame.

Successful outcome:

- Trust can be recovered.
- The household recognizes the difference between perceived progress and real money.

## Unexpected Case

A parent asks for urgent support, and the household uses money intended for a home appliance goal.

User expectations:

- The original goal should not disappear from memory.
- The family support event should be understandable as a real-life interruption.

Successful outcome:

- The household can accept that priorities changed.
- The goal can pause, change, or end without confusion.

## Emergency Case

One partner loses income, and the household stops all lifestyle goals while preserving medical and emergency buffers.

User expectations:

- Safety goals should be distinguishable from optional goals.
- Paused goals should not create shame.

Successful outcome:

- The household regains control.
- Partners agree on what is temporarily less important.

## Long-Term Case

A young family moves from short-term purchase goals to education, housing, transport, and health preparation over several years.

User expectations:

- Goals should still make sense as life gets more complex.
- Old goals should not clutter current thinking.

Successful outcome:

- Goals remain a shared household memory and planning reference.

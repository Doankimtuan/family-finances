# Recommendations

This document contains classified observations only. It does not redesign the business, create Product Decisions, or define implementation.

## Validated

- Planning as household intention matches real behavior.
- The problem of "money looks available but is already committed" is real.
- Jars or envelopes are a valid household budgeting metaphor when clearly separated from account balances.
- Goals are natural and emotionally meaningful for young households.
- Recurring obligations are central to salary-cycle planning.
- Shared finance between partners benefits from a common planning view.
- Month or period review matches the need to learn from mismatches.
- The real versus virtual distinction is essential.
- The domain remains relevant over 5-10 years as households add children, loans, accounts, and obligations.

## Needs Review

- Whether Planning terminology is understandable in Vietnamese household language.
- Whether "Month Ritual" feels helpful, unfamiliar, or over-formal.
- Whether jar states and planned capacity are too abstract for low-maturity users.
- Whether users distinguish goal intention from real savings product truth.
- Whether recurring rules are understood as expectations, not payments.
- Whether calendar events create false confidence about paid status.
- Whether shared planning visibility creates partner trust or blame.

## Needs Research

- Payday planning interviews with young Vietnamese couples.
- Household budget artifact review: notes, spreadsheets, app screenshots, chat reminders.
- One-month diary study of planned versus actual spending.
- Cash, QR, wallet, and transfer tracking fatigue.
- Irregular income frequency in the target segment.
- Family-support behavior and language.
- Seasonal planning around Tet, school, insurance, travel, wedding, funeral, and medical events.
- Vietnamese-English terminology testing.
- Partner conflict and privacy testing.

## Future Opportunity

- Better understanding of irregular income.
- Stronger long-term planning for children, home, vehicle, education, and elder support.
- More nuanced treatment of annual and seasonal expenses.
- Richer interpretation of planned-versus-actual mismatch.
- Provider-assisted evidence as Vietnam cashless and digital finance ecosystems mature.
- Deeper family lifecycle planning as households grow.

These are observations only, not roadmap priorities.

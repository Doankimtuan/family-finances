# Real-Life Scenarios

## Best Case

Scenario:

A couple receives salaries near the same week, records fixed obligations, protects rent and debt payments, funds a baby goal, and reviews the month calmly.

User expects:

- A clear view of what money is already committed.
- Agreement with partner.
- No confusion between planned jars and bank balances.

Successful outcome:

- Both partners know what is safe to spend and what is protected.

## Typical Case

Scenario:

One partner plans after payday. Some expenses are tracked, some wallet and cash spending is missed, and a few bills are adjusted mid-month.

User expects:

- Practical guidance despite imperfect data.
- Easy recognition of recurring obligations.
- No shame for corrections.

Successful outcome:

- The household understands the month well enough to avoid major surprises.

## Worst Case

Scenario:

Income is delayed, a family medical expense appears, and the household discovers that planned money was mistaken for available money.

User expects:

- Clarity about what changed.
- A way to understand which intentions are under pressure.
- Partner conversation without blame.

Successful outcome:

- The household regains a shared view of commitments and trade-offs.

## Unexpected Case

Scenario:

Tet travel, gifts, family support, and early salary create an unusual month. Normal recurring assumptions do not match reality.

User expects:

- Recognition that this month is not normal.
- Ability to compare expectations with actual pressure.
- Preservation of important commitments.

Successful outcome:

- The household avoids treating a seasonal month as a normal spending baseline.

## Emergency Case

Scenario:

A child, parent, or partner needs urgent medical or travel spending. Savings or goal money may be used.

User expects:

- Fast understanding of available room.
- Emotional permission to adjust the plan.
- Clear separation between real money source and virtual intention.

Successful outcome:

- The emergency is handled and the household can later recover the plan.

## Growth Case

Scenario:

The household's income rises. More accounts, goals, subscriptions, insurance, and family obligations appear.

User expects:

- Planning remains understandable as complexity grows.
- Long-term goals do not disappear behind daily spending.

Successful outcome:

- The household uses growth to improve stability, not only increase spending.

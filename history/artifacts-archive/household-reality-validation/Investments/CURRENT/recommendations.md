# Recommendations

This document contains classified observations only. It does not redesign the business, create Product Decisions, or define implementation.

## Validated

- Investments are a real household finance concern for some young Vietnamese households.
- The Phase 1 separation from Accounts, Transactions, Savings, Goals, Planning, Health, and Together is behaviorally important.
- Users need to distinguish invested principal, current value, realized proceeds, and available cash.
- Partner trust and visibility are central to household investment reality.
- Liquidity is as important as value during emergencies and major life events.
- The domain can remain meaningful over 5-10 years because asset-building needs grow with income and family responsibility.

## Needs Review

- Whether the domain should be framed as Investments, Wealth, Assets, or a Vietnam-first term in later product decision work.
- Whether gold belongs inside this domain for ordinary household understanding.
- Whether real estate should be considered part of Investments or handled separately in future domain work.
- Whether insurance-linked investment products are too hybrid for the core domain model.
- Whether account-level tracking is enough for young households before holding-level detail.
- Whether current value should be emphasized or softened to avoid cash-like interpretation.

## Needs Research

- Target household investment adoption by asset type.
- Partner privacy and consent expectations around investment accounts.
- Manual update tolerance for stocks, funds, gold, and informal investments.
- How users interpret broker cash, unsettled trades, and fund redemption timing.
- How households talk about gains and losses in Vietnamese.
- Common statement formats from Vietnamese brokers, fund distributors, insurers, and gold receipts.
- How often investment values affect real household decisions.

## Future Opportunity

- Long-term household wealth visibility may become more valuable as users age.
- Provider data may reduce manual burden if reliable integrations become available.
- Household trust patterns may require stronger validation as shared assets grow.
- Education, home purchase, and retirement horizons may make investment context more important.
- Informal and family-linked assets may become more important than formal securities for some households.

These are observations only, not roadmap recommendations.

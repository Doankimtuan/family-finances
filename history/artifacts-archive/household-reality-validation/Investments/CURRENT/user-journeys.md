# User Journeys

## Happy Path

The household has stable income, emergency cash, and a small investment habit.

Journey:

- Money is received into a bank account.
- Household agrees that part of surplus can be invested.
- Cash moves to a broker, fund app, or gold purchase.
- Investment is understood as long-term and separate from monthly spending.
- Partners periodically review contribution, current value, and liquidity.
- Dividends, distributions, sale proceeds, or withdrawals are recognized as investment events.
- Household can explain whether the investment supports long-term wealth without treating it as guaranteed cash.

Successful outcome:

- Both partners know what is invested, why it exists, and whether it can be touched.

## Common Path

One partner invests casually while the household mostly manages cash and bills.

Journey:

- Partner opens a broker or fund account.
- Money is transferred from a personal bank account.
- Investment app becomes the source of truth.
- Household product only sees bank transfer or rough current value, if anything.
- During gains, the investment feels like progress.
- During losses, the topic becomes avoided or emotionally charged.
- Partner visibility depends on trust, not formal process.

Successful outcome:

- The household avoids confusing invested money with spendable cash and can discuss risk when needed.

## First-Time Usage

A user first tries to understand investments as part of household money.

Journey:

- User identifies investment-like assets: stocks, funds, gold, crypto, family deal, or insurance-linked product.
- User asks whether each asset is cash, savings, goal, or investment.
- User may know purchase amount but not exact current value.
- User may need to ask partner or provider app for missing information.
- The household recognizes that not all "money we have" is equally liquid or safe.

Successful outcome:

- The household reaches a practical shared understanding, even if the numbers are approximate.

## Long-Term Usage

The household matures financially over several years.

Journey:

- Investments become one part of the household financial picture.
- New life events change risk appetite.
- Holdings are bought, sold, paused, transferred, or forgotten.
- Fund contributions may continue automatically.
- Gold or private investments may be updated manually and infrequently.
- Partner expectations shift from curiosity to accountability.
- The household needs historical memory of why money moved and what happened.

Successful outcome:

- Investment records remain understandable after time passes, provider apps change, or family circumstances evolve.

## Recovery Path

The household faces confusion, loss, or missing information.

Journey:

- Market value drops, a provider statement is unclear, or an informal investment becomes uncertain.
- One partner questions whether invested money is still available.
- User reconstructs contribution, current value, and exit status from broker app, fund statement, receipt, or family conversation.
- Household separates emotional regret from factual position.
- The investment is clarified, corrected, written down, sold, or left as uncertain.

Successful outcome:

- The household restores trust in the financial picture, even if the investment outcome is bad.

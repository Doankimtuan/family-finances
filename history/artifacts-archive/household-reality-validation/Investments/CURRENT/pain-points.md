# Pain Points

## Current Frustrations

- Investment data is spread across broker apps, bank apps, fund apps, gold receipts, insurance portals, and family conversations.
- Partner visibility is inconsistent.
- Provider language is technical.
- Market movement changes household confidence quickly.
- Informal investments are difficult to verify.
- Old contribution history is hard to reconstruct.
- Fees, taxes, spreads, and settlement timing are often invisible.
- Investment losses are emotionally harder to discuss than ordinary expenses.

## Confusing Concepts

- Investment account cash versus invested holdings.
- Contribution versus profit.
- Market value versus available cash.
- Unrealized gain versus realized gain.
- Savings product versus bond fund or money market fund.
- Dividend or coupon versus salary/income.
- Gold buy price versus sell price.
- Insurance premium versus surrender value.
- Crypto wallet value versus regulated securities value.

## Unnecessary Complexity

- Detailed performance methods may exceed household needs.
- Daily valuation may create stress for long-term users.
- Professional portfolio terminology can alienate beginners.
- Corporate action detail can overwhelm ordinary users.
- Tax precision may be premature for many households unless actual reporting need exists.

## Missing Visibility

- Which partner controls the investment.
- Whether the investment is household or personal.
- How much cash was actually contributed.
- What the current value source is.
- When the value was last known.
- Whether the asset can be sold quickly.
- Whether selling would create loss, fee, penalty, delay, or conflict.
- Whether the household is exposed to debt or margin.

## Potential Misunderstandings

- "We have 100 million invested" may mean cost, current value, or target.
- "The app says 120 million" may ignore taxes, fees, and exit timing.
- "Gold is safe" may hide price spread and storage risk.
- "Bond" may sound guaranteed when issuer risk exists.
- "Fund" may sound like savings when principal can move.
- "Long-term" may become "untouchable" even in a real emergency.
- "Household investment" may be privately controlled by one partner.

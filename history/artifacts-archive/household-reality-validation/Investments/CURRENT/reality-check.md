# Reality Check

## Real-World Behavior Versus Current Domain Understanding

Phase 1 understood Investments as risk-bearing assets with uncertain value, separate from cash, savings products, goals, and planning. Household reality supports this distinction.

Young households do not always use formal investment language, but they understand that "money in stocks/funds/gold" behaves differently from bank cash. The domain's separation is therefore behaviorally valid.

## Already Validated

- Investments solve a real household visibility problem when assets exist outside bank cash.
- Young households naturally separate investment money from daily spending money.
- Partner trust is central because investment decisions can create loss or secrecy.
- Contribution amount, current value, and liquidity matter more to ordinary households than advanced performance analytics.
- Investments should not be treated like guaranteed savings products.
- Unrealized value is a critical concept because users may otherwise treat it as cash.
- Manual tracking is realistic for broad holdings but not for frequent trades or daily prices.
- The domain remains relevant over 5-10 years as households earn more, marry, have children, buy homes, and accumulate assets.

## Assumptions Remaining

- The exact adoption rate among target Vietnamese households.
- Whether target users say "Investments", "Assets", "Wealth", "Stocks", "Funds", or local Vietnamese terms in ordinary conversation.
- Whether gold should be interpreted as Investments, Assets, Savings-like reserve, or a separate cultural asset in future design phases.
- Whether real estate belongs inside a broad Investments domain or requires separate treatment.
- Whether users want household-level investment visibility or partner-level privacy.
- Whether beginner households need holding-level truth or only account/asset summary truth.
- Whether users trust manual values enough for long-term household decisions.

## Still Needs Confirmation

- Direct interviews with young Vietnamese households who hold stocks, funds, gold, crypto, insurance-linked products, or family investments.
- Vietnamese-language terminology testing.
- Observation of how couples discuss investment losses.
- Evidence of how often users update manually tracked investment values.
- Provider statement and export availability for local brokers, fund apps, insurers, and gold records.
- Tax and regulatory expectations for ordinary household use.

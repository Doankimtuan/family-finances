# Pain Points

## Current Frustrations

- Money is spread across multiple bank apps, e-wallets, cash, cards, and savings products.
- One partner may not know the full real position.
- Small accounts are easy to forget.
- Cash disappears without a clear record.
- Credit cards create uncertainty about what is owned versus owed.
- Savings products can make money look present but not usable.
- Bank and wallet transaction labels are hard to recognize.
- Account totals become stale if not maintained.

## Confusing Concepts

- Account versus user login account.
- Bank account versus savings product.
- Savings account versus savings goal.
- Credit-card available limit versus owned money.
- Current balance versus available balance.
- Wallet promotion value versus withdrawable value.
- Personal account versus household account.
- Cash wallet versus e-wallet.

## Unnecessary Complexity Risks

- Too many account subtypes before users understand why they matter.
- Institution details beyond what users need for recognition.
- Overly formal accounting language.
- Treating every tiny wallet balance as equally important.
- Making users classify legal ownership when practical household relevance is what they understand first.

## Missing Visibility

- Which accounts are stale.
- Which accounts are household-relevant versus personal.
- Which balances are externally verified versus manually remembered.
- Which accounts are liquid right now.
- Which money is accessible only after maturity, transfer, or provider action.
- Which partner paid from which account.

## Potential Misunderstandings

- Users may believe the product balance is legally authoritative.
- Users may think planning allocations reserve money inside accounts.
- Users may assume adding a card adds spendable money.
- Users may count all savings as emergency cash.
- Users may assume account archive means history is deleted.

No solutions are proposed here.

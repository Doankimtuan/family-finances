# Cross-Domain Impact

## Accounts and Transactions

Transactions explain why an account balance changed. Users naturally expect every meaningful money movement to belong to some account.

Validation:

- Strongly validated by household behavior.
- Users think "I paid from Momo" or "salary entered Vietcombank" before thinking analytically.

## Accounts and Goals

Goals describe what the household wants to achieve. Accounts show where real money currently is.

Validation:

- Users can understand both, but may confuse "saving for a goal" with money already sitting in a savings account.

## Accounts and Planning

Planning depends on real money being known first. Account totals provide the reality check for monthly plans.

Validation:

- Strongly validated in stressful decisions.
- Users do not trust a plan if the account reality feels wrong.

## Accounts and Inbox

Inbox may need account context when a transaction, maturity, card event, or account-related discrepancy requires attention.

Validation:

- Users understand decision queues when the account impact is concrete.
- Too many informational account items would feel noisy.

## Accounts and Health

Health reads account reality and interprets the household position.

Validation:

- Users accept health summaries only if the account foundation is trusted.
- Health loses credibility if it appears to invent or override balances.

## Accounts and Categories

Categories explain spending purpose; Accounts explain money location.

Validation:

- Users naturally separate "where paid from" and "what it was for" once prompted.
- In casual speech, these can still blur.

## Accounts and Together

Together defines who sees and participates in household money. Accounts are emotionally sensitive because legal ownership and household relevance may differ.

Validation:

- Highly relevant for young couples.
- Trust depends on visible shared reality and respectful handling of personal-account boundaries.

## Accounts and Cards

Cards relate to payment behavior and obligations. Accounts relate to real owned money and repayment sources.

Validation:

- Users often see cards in bank apps next to accounts.
- Users may misunderstand available credit as money unless the distinction is clear.

## Accounts and Savings

Savings products may be linked to accounts but have maturity, rate, and liquidity constraints.

Validation:

- Vietnamese households understand term deposits as bank-held money, but may not naturally separate flexible savings account from contractual savings product without context.

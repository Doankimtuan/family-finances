# Decision Points

## Create

Users decide whether a real-world container is important enough to track.

Why it matters:

- Determines whether household real position is complete.
- Reveals what the household considers shared or relevant.

## Name

Users decide how to label the account.

Why it matters:

- Household recognition depends on everyday language.
- Poor labels reduce partner understanding.

## Classify

Users decide whether the container is cash, bank account, e-wallet, savings, credit, or other.

Why it matters:

- Classification affects mental interpretation of liquidity and ownership.
- Misclassification can distort confidence.

## Set Starting Balance

Users decide what balance to begin from.

Why it matters:

- All future confidence depends on the starting point.
- Wrong starting balances create persistent mismatch.

## Include in Household View

Users decide whether an account is part of shared household reality.

Why it matters:

- Young couples often have personal legal accounts but shared practical obligations.
- Inclusion can be emotionally sensitive.

## Edit

Users decide to correct account name, type, or starting context.

Why it matters:

- Reflects changing understanding or previous mistakes.
- Preserves account usefulness over time.

## Review

Users decide whether the recorded balance still feels true.

Why it matters:

- Builds or repairs trust.
- Reveals missed transactions and stale cash.

## Reconcile

Users compare product records with bank, wallet, card, or cash reality.

Why it matters:

- Establishes whether the household can rely on the product.
- Often happens before major decisions or month-end.

## Transfer

Users decide to move money between accounts.

Why it matters:

- It changes where money is, but not necessarily household net position.
- Misunderstanding transfers creates false income or expense.

## Pause / Stop Using

Users decide an account is no longer active in daily life.

Why it matters:

- Prevents stale accounts from polluting real position.
- Keeps history interpretable.

## Close

Users decide the real-world account has ended.

Why it matters:

- Closure must not erase financial history.
- Remaining balance needs real-world settlement.

## Withdraw

Users decide to remove money from a bank, wallet, savings product, or cash container.

Why it matters:

- Changes liquidity and account location.
- Often occurs during stress or planned spending.

## Renew

Users decide whether a savings-related account/product continues after maturity.

Why it matters:

- Affects liquidity and future access.
- Often requires partner discussion.

## Approve / Reject

Users decide whether an account-related change made by a partner is acceptable or trusted.

Why it matters:

- Shared finance depends on trust and visibility.
- Material changes can affect household confidence.

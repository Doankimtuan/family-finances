# User Journeys

## Happy Path

The household has a formal loan with clear terms.

Behavior:

- User knows lender, principal, monthly payment, due date, and term.
- Partner understands the repayment burden.
- Payments happen on time from a known account.
- The household periodically checks remaining principal.
- Final payment is confirmed by the lender.

Successful outcome:

- No missed payments.
- No surprise rate or fee shock.
- Both partners trust the repayment picture.
- Loan completion feels real and confirmed.

## Common Path

The household has enough information to track the loan, but not all details are clear.

Behavior:

- User enters lender and monthly payment from memory, app screen, or contract.
- Interest rate and principal/interest split may be unclear.
- Payments are recorded manually after bank transfer.
- Some months are checked carefully; other months are assumed.
- Partner may only know the monthly burden.

Successful outcome:

- Household avoids missing payment.
- Remaining obligation is directionally believable.
- User can plan the month without constantly opening provider apps.

## First-Time Usage

The user is taking or entering a loan for the first time.

Behavior:

- Focuses on whether monthly payment is affordable.
- May not distinguish principal, total repayment, interest, and fees.
- May enter minimal information.
- May rely on lender app screenshots or sales documents.

Successful outcome:

- User understands this is a future obligation.
- User can identify next payment and rough end date.
- User does not confuse the loan with savings or normal spending.

## Long-Term Usage

The household tracks a loan across months or years.

Behavior:

- Payment habit becomes routine.
- Partner trust depends on consistency.
- Rate changes, salary changes, children, or other loans create new pressure.
- Manual data may become stale if provider reality changes.

Successful outcome:

- Loan remains visible in household planning.
- Payment history stays understandable.
- Changes do not silently break trust.

## Recovery Path

The household misses, delays, partially pays, or misrecords a payment.

Behavior:

- User checks bank account, lender app, SMS, or receipt.
- Partner may become anxious or frustrated.
- User tries to understand whether the loan is overdue, paid, or pending.
- Future payments may need adjustment.

Successful outcome:

- Household knows what happened.
- Trust is restored through evidence or correction.
- Future cash-flow pressure is understood.

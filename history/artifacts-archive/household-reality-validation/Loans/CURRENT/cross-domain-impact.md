# Cross-Domain Impact

## Accounts

Loans interact with Accounts through payment source and cash availability.

Households ask whether the salary account, bank account, e-wallet, or cash has enough money to cover the next repayment.

## Transactions

Loan repayments become real money movements.

Transactions describe money leaving the household. Loans describe how that payment affects the obligation.

## Goals

Loan payments reduce available money for goals such as emergency fund, wedding, child expenses, home down payment, travel, or education.

Loans may also be tied to a goal-like life event, but the obligation remains separate from the aspiration.

## Planning

Loans affect future cash flow.

Monthly repayment pressure changes what the household can safely allocate to spending, saving, family support, and discretionary plans.

## Inbox

Loan events can create review needs: upcoming due date, overdue concern, completion check, rate-change awareness, or stale information.

Inbox owns the review surface; Loans owns the underlying obligation facts.

## Health

Loans influence financial health by increasing fixed obligations and reducing flexibility.

Health may interpret loan burden, but the loan record remains the source of loan-specific facts.

## Categories

Loan-funded purchases may belong to spending categories, but loan repayment itself should not be confused with the original consumption category.

Interest and fees may be understood differently from principal repayment.

## Together

Loans are emotionally important in shared finance.

Together-related behavior includes disclosure, partner acknowledgement, disagreement, shared payment responsibility, and confidence that obligations are visible.

## Cards

Credit cards are behaviorally adjacent because users may call card debt a loan.

Cards own revolving balances, limits, statements, and utilization. Loans own scheduled obligations.

## Debts

Open-ended owed money without a schedule belongs naturally near Debts.

If a family or friend obligation has a real schedule, household behavior may resemble Loans; if it is flexible and unscheduled, household behavior may resemble Debts.

## Savings

Savings interact through payoff decisions and opportunity cost.

Households may compare keeping savings versus repaying early, but this validation does not define decision rules.

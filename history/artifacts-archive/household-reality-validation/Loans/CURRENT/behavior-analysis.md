# Behavior Analysis

## Common Habits

- Users remember monthly payment better than total loan cost.
- Users often pay from salary account soon after payday.
- Users keep screenshots, SMS messages, Zalo messages, bank transfer notes, or paper contracts as proof.
- Users check provider apps when anxious, not every day.
- Couples often discuss large loans but may not discuss small installments until they accumulate.
- Family loans are tracked less formally than bank loans.

## Decision Patterns

- Monthly affordability dominates.
- Short approval time can outweigh total cost.
- Predictable payment is emotionally preferred.
- Interest-free messaging reduces caution.
- Early payoff is attractive when users feel trapped by debt.
- Partner disclosure increases for large or long-term loans.

## Typical Mistakes

- Confusing remaining principal with total outstanding balance.
- Forgetting late fees, insurance, or prepayment fees.
- Believing promotional rate is the long-term rate.
- Treating a planned schedule as lender truth.
- Recording repayment as generic spending.
- Ignoring small BNPL or store-financing plans until several overlap.
- Underestimating how much a loan limits future goals.

## Financial Awareness

Low awareness users:

- Know due date and monthly amount.
- Do not understand amortization.
- Need concrete repayment facts.

Medium awareness users:

- Track remaining balance and payment history.
- Understand interest exists.
- May still struggle with floating-rate details.

High awareness users:

- Consider total repayment, payoff timing, and risk of rate changes.
- Compare loan pressure against savings and goals.

## Emotional Factors

- Relief when borrowing solves a current need.
- Shame when debt is hidden or informal.
- Anxiety near due dates.
- Frustration when lender information is unclear.
- Conflict when partners disagree on whether a loan was necessary.
- Pride when progress is visible.
- Relief and achievement when the loan ends.

## Manual Work Users Accept

- Entering a major loan once.
- Recording monthly payment if it prevents missing due dates.
- Updating lender rate after a clear notification.
- Adding notes for family loans.
- Checking provider app for high-value loans.

## Manual Work Users Hate

- Re-entering the full schedule after small changes.
- Splitting principal, interest, and fees when provider does not show them clearly.
- Reconciling tiny differences every month.
- Tracking many small installment purchases one by one.
- Updating records when payment is pending, reversed, or posted late.

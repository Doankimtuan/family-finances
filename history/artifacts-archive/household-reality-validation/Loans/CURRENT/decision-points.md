# Decision Points

## Create

Decision: Record a new loan obligation.

Why it matters:

- Establishes that future income is already committed.
- Makes a private or vague obligation visible to household planning.

## Edit

Decision: Correct lender, amount, note, rate, term, or other known facts.

Why it matters:

- Loan trust depends on records matching real lender or family expectations.
- Incorrect facts can distort future cash-flow understanding.

## Pay

Decision: Mark or recognize a repayment.

Why it matters:

- Payment affects both real money and remaining obligation.
- Misrecorded payments can create false confidence or false anxiety.

## Partial Pay

Decision: Treat a payment as less than expected.

Why it matters:

- Partial payment may leave overdue amount, fees, or relationship tension.
- Household cash flow may still be under pressure.

## Early Payoff

Decision: Settle before planned end date.

Why it matters:

- Can reduce mental burden and future interest.
- May involve fees or lender-specific payoff conditions.

## Pause / Delay

Decision: Delay repayment, whether formally or informally.

Why it matters:

- Can create penalties, credit damage, family tension, or revised expectations.
- It changes household risk even if the loan remains active.

## Close

Decision: Treat a loan as ended, cancelled, defaulted, or archived.

Why it matters:

- Closure affects trust in the household's liability picture.
- False closure hides remaining obligation.

## Review

Decision: Recheck loan facts against provider statement, contract, partner, or family lender.

Why it matters:

- Restores confidence when records are stale or disputed.
- Helps distinguish planned schedule from confirmed reality.

## Approve

Decision: Partner acknowledges or accepts a loan as household-relevant.

Why it matters:

- Shared finance depends on mutual awareness.
- Large obligations can affect rent, children, home buying, and savings.

## Reject

Decision: Partner disputes whether an obligation should be treated as shared or valid.

Why it matters:

- Loans can create household conflict when ownership is unclear.
- Informal and pre-relationship loans are especially sensitive.

## Refinance / Replace

Decision: Recognize that an old obligation has changed into a new one.

Why it matters:

- The household may no longer owe the old lender but may owe another.
- Total burden and timeline can change.

## Restructure

Decision: Accept changed repayment terms.

Why it matters:

- Often signals financial stress.
- Changes future payment pressure and trust in old schedules.

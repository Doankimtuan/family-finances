# Pain Points

## Current Frustrations

- One partner carries the mental load of household finance.
- The other partner receives fragmented updates.
- Important agreements live in memory or chat history.
- Shared expenses and personal expenses are blurred.
- Family support affects the household but is hard to discuss.
- The partner who earns less may feel less empowered.
- The partner who tracks less may feel guilty or excluded.
- Policy changes are easy to forget.

## Confusing Concepts

- Household versus legal family.
- Partner versus spouse.
- Admin versus owner.
- Shared visibility versus shared legal authority.
- Household policy versus automatic money movement.
- Personal account versus household-relevant money.
- Equal role versus equal income contribution.

## Unnecessary Complexity

- More than two or three role concepts would likely feel over-engineered for the target users.
- Per-screen or per-account permission matrices would not match ordinary young household behavior.
- Heavy audit language may feel corporate.
- Treating every small action as approval-worthy may create fatigue.
- Asking users to formalize every informal agreement may feel unrealistic.

## Missing Visibility

- Whether both partners saw a material change.
- Whether a partner joined willingly and understands the shared boundary.
- Whether a household includes financially relevant non-members.
- Whether a member is active, inactive, or historically relevant.
- Whether an external account or wallet is personal-only or household-relevant.

## Potential Misunderstandings

- Users may think Together can resolve relationship conflicts.
- Users may think a household member can legally control another person's bank account.
- Users may think Admin means the final decision-maker.
- Users may think all personal financial facts must become shared.
- Users may think declining an invite is a relationship judgment.
- Users may mistake configuration for consent.

No solutions are proposed in this validation phase.

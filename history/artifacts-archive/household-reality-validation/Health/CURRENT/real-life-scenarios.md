# Real-Life Scenarios

## Best Case

The couple has stable salaries, visible accounts, a simple plan, low debt, a manageable Inbox, and some emergency buffer.

User expects:

- A reassuring condition signal.
- Clear reasons for the signal.
- No exaggerated advice.

Successful outcome:

- The household feels confident but still attentive.

## Typical Case

The household has salary income, rent, bills, some card use, a few open decisions, and occasional family support. They know they are "probably okay" but cannot explain why money feels tight.

User expects:

- A simple read on current condition.
- Visibility into what is creating pressure.
- Language both partners can accept.

Successful outcome:

- The household can name the pressure without arguing from memory.

## Worst Case

The household has debt pressure, missing records, delayed salary, medical cost exposure, and unresolved partner disagreement.

User expects:

- Honesty without panic.
- Clear indication that the signal may be incomplete.
- No automatic action or blame.

Successful outcome:

- Users understand that financial condition is strained and which visible facts contribute.

## Unexpected Case

A relative asks for urgent support, salary arrives late, or a large annual bill appears earlier than expected.

User expects:

- Health to reflect changed pressure if the facts are visible.
- No claim that the household is safe when obligations collide.

Successful outcome:

- The household recognizes that condition has changed and avoids false confidence.

## Emergency Case

A child, partner, or parent has a medical event. Some cost is paid out of pocket; insurance coverage or reimbursement may be uncertain.

User expects:

- A financial view of impact, not medical advice.
- Recognition that insurance does not always equal full protection.
- Calm framing during a stressful moment.

Successful outcome:

- The household can separate medical urgency from financial visibility and understand the money pressure created by the event.

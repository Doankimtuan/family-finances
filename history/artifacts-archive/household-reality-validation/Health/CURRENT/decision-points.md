# Decision Points

Health is read-only, but users still make decisions around how they interpret and respond to it.

## Review Health

Users decide whether to look at their financial condition.

Why it matters:

- Review begins awareness.
- Avoidance is common when money feels stressful.

## Trust Or Question The Signal

Users decide whether the Health signal matches lived reality.

Why it matters:

- Trust depends on recognizable source facts.
- Mismatch can damage confidence in the whole product.

## Investigate A Factor

Users decide whether to look deeper into accounts, plans, debts, Inbox, or transactions.

Why it matters:

- Health creates attention, but ownership belongs elsewhere.

## Discuss With Partner

Users decide whether to share the condition conversation.

Why it matters:

- Shared understanding is valuable.
- Blame, privacy, and defensiveness can appear.

## Update Missing Facts Elsewhere

Users decide whether to correct or add source-domain facts.

Why it matters:

- Health cannot be more accurate than visible facts.
- Manual effort must feel worthwhile.

## Act In Another Domain

Users decide whether to change a plan, resolve Inbox, repay debt, record a transaction, or adjust account visibility.

Why it matters:

- Health remains interpretive.
- Real action belongs to the owning domain.

## Ignore Health

Users decide not to act or not to review further.

Why it matters:

- Ignoring may be rational if signal is obvious, incomplete, or emotionally too costly.

## Reassess After Change

Users decide whether to come back after salary, payment, correction, emergency, or partner discussion.

Why it matters:

- Health becomes more valuable as a repeated reflection, not a one-time score.

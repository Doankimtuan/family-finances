# Reality Check

## Real-World Behavior vs Current Domain Understanding

The Phase 1 understanding is broadly aligned with household reality. Young households naturally understand transaction history because it resembles bank statements, wallet history, receipts, and memory of payments.

The strongest match is the concept that transactions answer "what happened to our money?"

## Already Validated

- Transactions solve a real problem: money disappears from memory.
- Young households understand income and expense direction.
- Transaction history is a natural source of truth for discussion.
- Account context is necessary for trust.
- Notes, categories, and descriptions help memory.
- Refunds and corrections are real household needs.
- Unclear transactions naturally become review work.
- Transactions remain relevant as families mature.
- Real transaction facts must stay distinct from virtual planning intent.

## Assumptions That Remain

- Users will tolerate enough manual entry for cash and missing provider records.
- Young households will understand correction and refund concepts if expressed plainly.
- Users can distinguish transaction category from planning intent.
- Partner visibility will be accepted for shared finance.
- Search and review needs can be met without formal accounting complexity.
- Most households need practical confidence more than perfect reconciliation.

## Still Needs Confirmation

- Vietnam-specific cash versus digital transaction mix for target households.
- Natural Vietnamese wording for transaction, transfer, refund, category, and reconciliation concepts.
- How couples classify transfers between partners and family.
- How sensitive users are about personal spending visibility.
- How much missing cash activity is acceptable before trust drops.
- Whether users expect provider-like pending and posted states.
- How often users need receipt evidence.
- Whether split categorization is common enough to matter in early product decisions.
- How households interpret credit card payments relative to card purchases.

# Reality Check

## Real-World Behavior vs Current Domain Understanding

The current domain understanding says Categories are household classification labels for money events. Young-household behavior supports this strongly.

Households naturally use category-like language:

- "This was groceries."
- "This was Grab."
- "This was rent."
- "This was for my parents."
- "This was baby stuff."
- "This was eating out."
- "This was salary."

The mental model is real. The main validation challenge is whether users can keep categories lightweight and distinct from jars, budgets, provider labels, transfers, and balances.

## Already Validated

- Categories solve a real problem: transaction history without meaning is hard to use.
- Users naturally understand broad category labels.
- Category language supports partner discussion.
- Categories are useful for monthly reflection.
- Categories should not hold money.
- Categories should not replace accounts or jars.
- Provider or merchant category can differ from household purpose.
- Mixed-purpose purchases and family transfers are real sources of ambiguity.
- The model can survive 5-10 years if category meaning remains stable.

## Assumptions Remaining

- Whether young Vietnamese households prefer broad labels or more life-stage-specific labels.
- Whether default categories feel local enough.
- Whether "Category" is the right user-facing term in Vietnamese context.
- Whether users can understand category-to-jar mapping without seeing categories as budgets.
- How much manual categorization users tolerate over months.
- Whether one-category-per-transaction is acceptable for common mixed purchases.
- Whether provider hints improve trust or create false confidence.
- Whether partners agree on sensitive categories such as family support, dining, gifts, and personal spending.

## Still Needs Confirmation

- Interviews with Vietnamese households aged 20-35 about how they describe recent spending.
- Review of real transaction histories, bank notes, wallet records, and chat explanations.
- Category-card sorting with target users.
- Vietnamese-language terminology testing.
- Observation of partner month-end review.
- Evidence on how often mixed receipts materially distort household understanding.
- Evidence on how category cleanup behavior changes after 3-6 months.
- Validation of category-to-jar mental model with non-finance users.

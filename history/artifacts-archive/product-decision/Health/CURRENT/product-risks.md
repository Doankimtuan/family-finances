# Product Risks

## Financial Risk

- Users may treat a strong Health signal as permission to spend.
- Missing accounts, cash, wallets, debts, or family obligations can make Health incomplete.
- Medical and insurance visibility can be misread as protection certainty.
- Debt or card signals may be under-weighted if source facts are missing.

## UX Complexity Risk

- Too many factors can make Health feel complicated.
- Too few factors can make Health feel arbitrary.
- Scores can feel judgmental or falsely precise.
- Scenario language can be mistaken for instructions.

## Maintenance Risk

- Each added source-domain factor increases explanation burden.
- Provider-derived facts would increase stale-data and integration maintenance risk.
- Longitudinal trend interpretation can create hidden persistence and consistency obligations.

## Scalability Risk

- Vietnam-first Health assumptions may not transfer internationally.
- Family lifecycle needs can expand the domain too quickly.
- Cross-domain clustering can become hard to reason about over 5-10 years.

## Education Risk

- Users may confuse financial Health with medical health.
- Users may confuse planning intention with real money.
- Users may not understand why incomplete data changes confidence.

## User Misunderstanding Risk

- Partner alignment can be interpreted as blame.
- Emergency-buffer language can sound like advice.
- Medical-expense exposure can sound clinical or insurance-advisory.
- AI-generated explanations can appear more certain than the underlying facts.

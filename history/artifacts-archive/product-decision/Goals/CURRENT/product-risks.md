# Product Risks

## Financial Risk

- Users may read goal progress as real available money.
- Contributions may be mistaken for transfers.
- Same real money may be mentally counted toward multiple goals.
- Savings product values may be confused with goal progress.
- Forecasting or evidence matching can create false certainty if introduced too early.

## UX Complexity Risk

- Goal, jar, savings account, and savings product language can overlap.
- Priority, trade-off, evidence, and confidence concepts can overload simple goal usage.
- Completion can mean funded, purchased, paid, or emotionally done.

## Maintenance Risk

- Provider matching, forecasting, inflation review, AI explanations, and confidence language would add high maintenance burden.
- Life-event modeling can expand into many specialized subdomains.
- Partner-specific contribution semantics can become policy-heavy.

## Scalability Risk

- Goals must remain stable as households add children, accounts, debts, savings products, and family obligations.
- Multi-goal trade-offs can become complex if introduced before source ownership is clear.

## Education Risk

- Users need to understand that Goals are intention.
- Low-maturity households may need repeated plain-language distinction between goal progress and balance.
- Vietnamese terminology remains unvalidated.

## User Misunderstanding Risk

- "Funded" may imply cash is available.
- "Completed" may imply purchase or payment happened.
- "Associated savings" may imply the goal owns the savings account.
- Partner visibility may imply accountability or blame.

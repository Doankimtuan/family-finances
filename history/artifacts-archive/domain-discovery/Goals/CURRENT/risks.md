# Risks

## Business Risks

- Goals become too broad and absorb Planning, Savings, or advisory work.
- Users expect the product to guarantee affordability.
- Goal language implies financial advice.
- Long-term wealth goals expand beyond household operating clarity.
- Partner conflict becomes amplified by visible underfunding.

## Financial Risks

- Same money is counted toward multiple goals.
- Goal progress is confused with real balance.
- Illiquid savings products are treated as available for a goal.
- Inflation or price changes make targets stale.
- Debt obligations are ignored while goals appear healthy.
- Manual progress creates false confidence.

## UX Risks

- Too many goals overwhelm the household.
- Failure language creates shame.
- Progress percentages overstate certainty.
- Paused or cancelled states feel punitive.
- Users cannot tell whether progress is manual, factual, or mixed.
- Goal terms overlap with jar, account, and savings terms.

## Misunderstanding Risks

- "Savings" may mean goal, account, product, habit, or category.
- "Funded" may be read as provider-confirmed balance.
- "Completed" may be read as purchase completed.
- "Contribution" may be read as bank transfer.
- "Emergency fund" may be confused with emergency credit.

## Future Scalability Risks

- Multi-source funding can become complex.
- Provider matching may create false confidence.
- Partner-specific contribution history can create privacy and fairness issues.
- Investment-linked goals can introduce valuation volatility and advice risk.
- Long-term goals may require inflation, education, insurance, and retirement assumptions beyond the current domain.

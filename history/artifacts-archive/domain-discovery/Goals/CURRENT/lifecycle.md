# Lifecycle

## Beginning

A goal begins when the household identifies a future purpose that needs preparation.

Common beginnings:

- A major expense becomes visible.
- A partner proposes a shared target.
- A financial stress event reveals missing buffer.
- Income increases and the household wants to protect the surplus.
- A date-driven obligation appears.
- A family obligation emerges.

## Middle

During the middle of the lifecycle, the household:

- Refines the target.
- Adds or withholds contributions.
- Checks progress.
- Compares the goal with other priorities.
- Changes the target date.
- Pauses during pressure.
- Resumes when capacity returns.
- Reinterprets whether the goal still matters.

## End

A goal naturally ends when:

- The target is reached.
- The goal is used for its intended purpose.
- The household cancels it.
- The goal becomes irrelevant.
- The goal is merged into another goal.
- The goal is replaced by a real financial product or contract.

## Possible Exits

- Completed.
- Cancelled.
- Abandoned.
- Converted into another goal.
- Superseded by a savings product, loan, purchase, or transaction.
- Archived for historical memory.

## Possible Interruptions

- Job loss.
- Medical expense.
- Family support need.
- Inflation or price change.
- Missed salary.
- Debt repayment pressure.
- Partner disagreement.
- Cash used without tracking.
- Provider account unavailable.

## Possible Recoveries

- Resume after pause.
- Reduce target.
- Extend target date.
- Add catch-up contribution.
- Reclassify the goal.
- Split or merge household intention.
- Accept partial completion.

No implementation behavior is implied by this lifecycle.

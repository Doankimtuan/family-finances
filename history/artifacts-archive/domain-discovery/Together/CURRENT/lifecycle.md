# Lifecycle

## Beginning

The Together lifecycle begins when a person forms or joins a household financial space.

Common beginnings:

- A couple starts living together.
- Partners begin sharing bills.
- Marriage or engagement formalizes shared planning.
- A child or dependent changes the financial unit.
- One partner invites another into a shared money product.
- A household restarts after moving, job change, debt event, or family event.

## Middle

The normal operating life of Together includes:

- Members use the shared financial space.
- Household policies become routine.
- One or both partners review money facts.
- Material assumptions change over time.
- New accounts, debts, bills, goals, and obligations appear in other domains.
- Household communication alternates between active discussion and quiet trust.

## End

The lifecycle may end when the household financial unit no longer exists or no longer uses the product.

Possible endings:

- Partners separate.
- Household migrates to another system.
- User deletes account.
- Household becomes inactive.
- Household support need ends.

## Possible Exits

- A member leaves.
- A pending invite expires or is declined.
- A user belongs to no active household.
- Household financial management returns to a single-person model.

## Possible Interruptions

- Lost access to email or phone.
- Forgotten password or changed identity provider.
- Invite sent to wrong email.
- Partner does not accept invitation.
- Trust conflict stops shared usage.
- Policy disagreement blocks continued planning.
- Household reaches a legal or emotional separation event.
- External provider access changes.

## Possible Recoveries

- Member re-authenticates.
- Invitation is resent.
- Household policies are reviewed.
- Missing context is clarified between partners.
- Product access is restored.
- A household member resumes participation after inactivity.

Recovery is social as much as technical. The real household must still agree on what shared finance means.

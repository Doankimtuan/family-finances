# Lifecycle

## Beginning

A category begins when the household needs a reusable label for money meaning.

Common beginnings:

- Initial household setup.
- First transaction review.
- A new recurring merchant appears.
- A new life stage begins, such as baby, school, moving home, or caring for parents.
- An existing broad category becomes too vague.

## Middle

During normal life, categories are used to label transactions, review spending, support partner discussion, and interpret actual behavior.

Middle-state activities:

- Assign category to transaction.
- Correct category when meaning is wrong.
- Use category in transaction filtering or reporting.
- Compare category totals across periods.
- Map category meaning into planning context.
- Decide whether unclear items remain uncategorized.

## End

A category ends when the household no longer wants to use it for future classification.

Natural endings:

- The category was temporary.
- The category duplicates another category.
- The category name no longer matches household language.
- The household changes life stage.
- The category becomes too specific to maintain.

## Possible Exits

- Archive from future use.
- Merge conceptually with another category.
- Stop selecting it.
- Preserve only for historical interpretation.

## Possible Interruptions

- Provider import gives a different classification.
- Partner disagrees with the label.
- Category is missing during transaction entry.
- Category is linked to an inactive planning concept.
- Receipt contains multiple purposes.
- Household stops reviewing transactions.

## Possible Recoveries

- Reclassify the affected transaction.
- Rename the category for clarity.
- Restore an archived category.
- Clarify the transaction through partner review.
- Treat the category as historical only while using a different current label.

## Long-Term Stability

The category lifecycle is durable over 5-10 years if category meaning remains stable enough for historical comparison and flexible enough for household life changes.

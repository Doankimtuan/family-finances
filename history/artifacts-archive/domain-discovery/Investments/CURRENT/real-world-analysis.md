# Real-World Analysis

## Vietnam Context

In Vietnam, ordinary household investment activity commonly appears in several forms:

- Listed stocks on HOSE, HNX, and UPCoM through a securities company account.
- ETFs and public funds, especially open-ended funds distributed by fund managers, banks, securities companies, and fintech apps.
- Corporate bonds, government bonds, or bond funds.
- Gold held physically or through gold trading venues, often treated by families as a store of value.
- Real estate or land, which is often an investment asset but may need separate domain treatment because of title, debt, rental income, and family ownership complexity.
- Insurance-linked investment products, where protection and investment are bundled.
- Foreign securities or crypto exposure through overseas apps or informal channels, with regulatory and verification complexity.
- Informal investments in a relative's business, group deals, private lending, or rotating savings groups.

Vietnam's public securities market is supervised by the State Securities Commission. Listed securities are traded through exchanges and settled through the Vietnam Securities Depository and Clearing Corporation. VSDC's public history notes the move to T+2 settlement. Recent official SSC materials describe ongoing capital-market reform, investment-fund regulation updates, and fund product expansion.

## Common Practices

- A household opens a securities account with a broker and transfers VND from a bank account to trade.
- One partner often manages the account while the other only knows the approximate value.
- Users check gains and losses inside broker or fund apps, not in a household finance tool.
- Small investors may buy familiar blue-chip stocks, bank stocks, real estate stocks, ETFs, or fund certificates.
- Fund investors may use recurring monthly contributions because the product feels less demanding than stock picking.
- Gold may be bought at jewelry/gold shops and tracked mentally by tael, chỉ, or purchase price.
- Family investments may be documented poorly and remembered as "we gave X to my sibling's shop."
- Households often mix investment decisions with dreams: children's education, home upgrade, migration, retirement, or "money should not sit idle."

## Real-World Variations

- Conservative household: term deposits and gold only.
- Beginner investor: small stock or fund positions, often influenced by friends, social media, or bank staff.
- Dual-earner young family: monthly fund contributions plus occasional stock buying.
- Speculative investor: concentrated stock, margin, crypto, or short-term trading.
- Family-led investor: money placed into a relative's business or property deal.
- Employer-linked investor: employee stock, private shares, or company profit-sharing.
- Internationally exposed household: foreign ETFs, USD assets, overseas brokerage, or cross-border remittance constraints.

## Terminology

- Securities account: account opened at a securities company for trading and holding securities.
- Brokerage account: international term for an account used to buy and sell investments.
- Custody: legal/operational safekeeping and record of securities or fund units.
- T+2 settlement: trade settlement two business days after trade date.
- NAV: net asset value of a fund or fund unit.
- Market value: estimated current value using a quoted market price or provider valuation.
- Cost basis: amount paid for an investment, including or excluding fees depending on convention.
- Unrealized gain/loss: value change on an asset not yet sold.
- Realized gain/loss: gain/loss after sale or redemption.
- Dividend: cash or stock distribution from an equity investment.
- Coupon: interest paid by a bond.
- Redemption: sale of fund units back to a fund or distributor.
- Liquidity: ease and speed of converting an investment to cash.
- Volatility: degree of price movement over time.
- Margin: borrowed money used to invest, increasing risk.

## Industry Standards and Concepts

- Investments are normally described by instrument, quantity/units, price, value, currency, cost basis, account/custodian, and date.
- Marketable securities may have daily prices; private assets may have rare or subjective valuations.
- Account cash, unsettled trades, and security holdings are distinct.
- Investment return can come from price appreciation, dividends, bond coupons, fund distributions, currency movement, or tax effects.
- Investment performance usually separates money-weighted return, time-weighted return, realized gain, unrealized gain, and income.
- Investment statements usually distinguish available cash from portfolio value.
- Custody and beneficial ownership can differ from who makes household decisions.
- Fees, taxes, bid/ask spread, transfer fees, and early exit rules can materially affect final proceeds.

## International Differences That Affect Product Design

- Tax treatment of capital gains, dividends, bond interest, and fund distributions varies by country.
- Open banking and brokerage aggregation are more standardized in some markets than in Vietnam.
- Retirement accounts are central in some countries but less central to ordinary Vietnamese household investment behavior.
- Fractional shares, robo-advisors, and automatic tax-loss harvesting are common in some markets but should not be assumed in Vietnam.
- Foreign ownership limits, foreign investor rules, and market classification reforms affect Vietnam market infrastructure more than ordinary domestic household behavior, but they matter for terminology and provider data.

# Risks

## Business Risks

- Treating Accounts as planning containers instead of real containers.
- Blurring credit products with owned money.
- Allowing account terminology to conflict with banking terminology familiar to users.
- Making account setup too formal for households that think in nicknames.
- Making account setup too loose for reconciliation to remain trustworthy.
- Losing historical meaning when accounts are closed or archived.

## Financial Risks

- Double-counting own-account transfers.
- Counting credit limit as household money.
- Including non-withdrawable wallet promotion value in real position.
- Treating pending or held funds as settled money.
- Ignoring fees, interest, reversals, and failed transfers.
- Misrepresenting term deposits as fully liquid cash.
- Using stale balances for high-confidence decisions.
- Confusing household record balance with legal institution balance.

## UX Risks

- Users cannot tell account balances from jar allocations.
- Partner who is less involved cannot understand where money is.
- Too many account details create noise.
- Too little metadata makes accounts hard to recognize.
- Cash and e-wallet records feel tedious and become stale.
- Archived accounts disappear so historical transactions look orphaned.

## Misunderstanding Risks

- "Account" may be confused with login/user account.
- "Savings" may mean either flexible bank savings, a term deposit, or a planned goal.
- "Available balance" may mean bank-available funds, app-calculated money after plans, or card available credit.
- "Wallet" may mean physical cash wallet or e-wallet.
- "Balance" may be interpreted as legal bank truth even when manually recorded.

## Future Scalability Risks

- Account type taxonomy may become strained by future products.
- Multi-currency accounts require exchange-rate awareness.
- Provider integrations introduce stale, partial, or duplicated data.
- Household privacy rules may conflict with shared-money transparency.
- Regulations for bank data access, e-wallets, and payments can change.
- Historical migrations become difficult if account identity is weak.

# User Behaviors

## Common Behaviors

- Keep one main salary bank account.
- Keep one or more secondary accounts for savings, convenience, promotions, or card repayment.
- Use e-wallets opportunistically for discounts and small purchases.
- Keep physical cash for markets, parking, family support, and emergencies.
- Mentally separate "household money" and "personal money" even when all money is visible.
- Check bank apps directly when confidence matters.
- Rename accounts using practical labels such as "Salary", "Cash", "Momo", "Rent bank", or a bank nickname.
- Update records after spending rather than before spending.

## Expectations

- The household expects account totals to feel truthful.
- Users expect account names to match how they think, not formal banking terminology.
- Users expect account balances to be distinguishable from budgets, jars, or goals.
- Users expect archived or closed accounts to remain in history.
- Users expect credit cards, savings products, and e-wallets to behave differently from cash.
- Users expect manual correction when the app record differs from a bank or cash count.

## Frustrations

- Money is scattered across too many apps.
- One partner does not know where shared money is held.
- Cash is easy to forget.
- E-wallet balances are small but annoying to track.
- Bank statements can be cryptic or merchant names can be unclear.
- Transfers between own accounts can look like spending or income if misunderstood.
- Credit card available credit is easily confused with cash.
- Savings products can hide liquidity because money exists but is not freely spendable.

## Mistakes

- Counting a credit card limit as money available.
- Treating jar allocations as if they are bank balances.
- Forgetting pending transfers, failed payments, or cash spending.
- Double-counting a transfer between own accounts as both income and expense.
- Leaving closed accounts active in household totals.
- Recording the same bank transaction twice after manual entry and later import.
- Using an e-wallet promotion balance as if it were withdrawable cash.
- Ignoring foreign currency conversion when traveling or receiving overseas payments.

## Decision Patterns

- Before a large expense, users check the main bank account first.
- Before month-end, users compare what remains in cash and bank apps.
- When stress is high, users simplify to "how much do we really have?"
- When trust is low, users rely on institution apps over household records.
- When one partner manages money, the other needs a quick reality summary rather than full transaction detail.
- When a discrepancy appears, users first ask whether a transaction was missed, duplicated, refunded, reversed, or recorded in the wrong account.

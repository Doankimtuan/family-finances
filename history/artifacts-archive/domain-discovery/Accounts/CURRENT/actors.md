# Actors

## User

The individual operating the product. They create account records, enter balances, record transactions, reconcile differences, and interpret account totals.

## Partner

A household member sharing financial visibility or responsibility. The partner may not perform daily entry but depends on account truth for trust and decisions.

## Family

Relatives who influence real account behavior through support, gifts, loans, shared cash, or informal custody. Family may hold or receive money without being a product user.

## Household

The collective financial unit. It owns the household view even when real accounts are legally held by individual people.

## Bank

The regulated financial institution that holds payment accounts, savings accounts, term deposits, debit cards, and sometimes credit cards. The bank is the authoritative source for institution-held balances.

## Fintech / E-Wallet Provider

The intermediary payment provider that maintains wallet balances, payment history, and linked bank relationships. The provider may support top-up, withdrawal, QR payment, peer transfer, bill payment, and promotions.

## Card Issuer

The bank or finance company that issues credit products. It owns card limit, authorization, statement, outstanding balance, minimum payment, due dates, fees, and dispute processes.

## Merchant

The party receiving money from the account or card. Merchant behavior affects pending charges, refunds, reversals, settlement timing, and recognizable transaction labels.

## Employer

The source of salary deposits. Employer timing and payroll errors can affect account balances and household cash flow.

## Third-Party Provider

Any external data source or aggregator that provides account or transaction information. It may be read-only, delayed, incomplete, or unavailable.

## System

The product's internal record keeper. It stores household account records and derives internal position from recorded facts. It is not the bank and should not be treated as the legal balance authority.

## Background Worker

A system actor that may refresh read-only information, detect stale records, or perform scheduled checks where such capabilities exist. It does not own real-world money.

## Regulator

The public authority defining rules for payment accounts, e-wallets, banking safety, KYC, anti-fraud controls, and consumer protection.

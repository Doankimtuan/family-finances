# Future Considerations

These are observations about what may become useful in 3-5 years. They are not roadmap items.

## Possible Domain Pressures

- Multi-currency households may become more common because of remote work, travel, study abroad, and remittance.
- Vietnamese bank and wallet integrations may mature, increasing expectations for automatic read-only balances and transaction history.
- Cross-border QR payment networks may make account geography more relevant.
- E-wallet regulation and verification rules may make wallet status more important.
- Households may expect clearer distinction between owned cash, credit access, term deposits, and investment assets.
- Data portability expectations may increase.
- Fraud, scam transfers, and mistaken QR payments may make account verification and reconciliation more visible.
- Young families may accumulate more financial products over time: mortgage, insurance-linked accounts, brokerage, child savings, education funds, and foreign accounts.

## Possible Capabilities That May Become Useful

- Per-account currency.
- Institution and provider identity.
- Account holder metadata.
- Account masks.
- Reconciliation history.
- Balance freshness.
- Provider connection status.
- Distinction between recorded, institution, available, and pending balances.
- More precise liquidity classification.
- Read-only bank/wallet feeds.
- Transfer detection.
- Closed-account lifecycle metadata.
- Investment or wealth account read-only support.
- Fraud/dispute status awareness.

## Stability Observation

The core concept is durable: money and obligations still need a home. Future evolution is likely to add metadata, integrations, and more precise classifications rather than change the domain's purpose.

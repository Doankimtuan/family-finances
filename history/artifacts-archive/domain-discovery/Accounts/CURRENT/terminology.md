# Terminology

## Preferred Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Account | A real-world financial container or obligation record used by the household. |
| Account balance | The recorded amount associated with an account. |
| Real position | The household's total real recorded money position across included accounts. |
| Cash | Physical money held by the household. |
| Bank account | Institution-held account used to receive, hold, and transfer money. |
| Payment account | Formal Vietnamese regulatory-style term for account used with payment service providers. |
| E-wallet | Stored-value account with a payment intermediary provider. |
| Credit card account | Credit product account representing borrowing capacity and obligations, not cash. |
| Savings account | Bank-held savings balance. |
| Savings product | Contractual deposit or savings instrument with lifecycle terms. |
| Opening balance | Starting recorded amount when an account begins being tracked. |
| Recorded balance | Balance according to the household product record. |
| Institution balance | Balance shown by bank, wallet, card issuer, or provider. |
| Reconciliation | Comparing household records with external real-world records. |
| Transfer | Movement between two accounts. |
| Account holder | Person or entity legally associated with the account. |
| Account mask | Partial account identifier safe for recognition. |
| Archived account | Historical account no longer active in daily use. |

## Forbidden Synonyms

| Avoid | Reason |
|-------|--------|
| Budget account | Confuses real account with planning allocation. |
| Virtual account | Confuses account with jar/envelope. |
| Jar balance | Jars hold allocations, not real money balances. |
| Spending account | Too vague and not financially precise. |
| Free money | Encourages confusion between real balance and obligations. |
| Available after jars | Derived planning concept, not account truth. |
| Card money | Credit limit is not money owned by the household. |
| Bank balance for jar total | Violates real versus virtual distinction. |

## Business Definitions

- Account identity is how the household recognizes the container.
- Balance truth is externally verifiable when an institution exists and manually verifiable for cash.
- Liquidity describes how readily the household can use the money.
- Account history remains relevant even after the account exits active use.
- Account type should explain real-world behavior, not user intention.

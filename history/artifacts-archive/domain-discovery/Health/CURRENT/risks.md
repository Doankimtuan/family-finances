# Risks

## Business Risks

- Health may be perceived as a promise that the household is financially safe.
- A score can create false confidence if source data is incomplete.
- Users may expect Health to solve problems that belong to other domains.
- Medical-cost interpretation may be mistaken for insurance or healthcare advice.
- Partner disagreement may make a single household signal feel politically sensitive.

## Financial Risks

- Missing cash, informal debt, or family obligations can materially distort condition.
- Credit limits may be mistaken as resilience.
- Insurance coverage may be overestimated.
- One-time income may hide structural overspending.
- Debt burden may be understated if fees, interest, or informal loans are missing.

## UX Risks

- A low score can shame users.
- A high score can make users complacent.
- Too many factors can make Health feel noisy.
- Too few explanations can make Health feel arbitrary.
- Scenario language can be misread as instruction.

## Misunderstanding Risks

- Health may be confused with medical health.
- Plan intention may be confused with bank balance.
- Read-only observation may be confused with automated action.
- A grounded signal may still be mistaken for complete truth.
- Insurance presence may be confused with full financial protection.

## Future Scalability Risks

- More source domains can make factor weighting harder to explain.
- International expansion changes healthcare, insurance, credit, and bank-data assumptions.
- AI-generated narratives increase invention and advisory risk.
- Historical trend storage can conflict with compute-on-read principles if not carefully governed.
- Provider integrations may introduce stale, partial, or contradictory facts.

# Risks

## Business Risks

- Inbox becomes too broad and loses its decision-queue meaning.
- Households perceive review as chores rather than control.
- Too many item types create confusion.
- Important decisions are mixed with low-value noise.
- Decision history is treated as financial truth when the owning domain should remain authoritative.

## Financial Risks

- Unresolved items make reports incomplete or misleading.
- Wrong resolution creates incorrect jar, category, or plan interpretation.
- Dismissed items may hide real obligations.
- Expired reminders may still represent unpaid bills.
- Provider messages may be incomplete or delayed.
- Auto-resolution may silently repeat a past mistake.

## UX Risks

- Backlog anxiety.
- Decision fatigue.
- Partner conflict over who should resolve an item.
- Ambiguous state labels.
- Users may not understand the difference between notification, acknowledgement, and resolution.
- Empty Inbox may be misread as "no financial risk exists".

## Misunderstanding Risks

- Treating Inbox as a bank inbox.
- Treating an Inbox item as a transaction.
- Treating a resolved item as proof that money moved.
- Treating a reminder as a liability record.
- Treating suggested resolution as authoritative.
- Treating partner visibility as partner consent.

## Future Scalability Risks

- High transaction volume can overwhelm one-item-at-a-time review.
- Multiple providers can create duplicate or conflicting items.
- Cross-border payments and foreign currency add lifecycle complexity.
- Household business activity may introduce invoice and tax evidence requirements.
- AI or rule-based suggestions require strong auditability.
- Long-term archives may become large and difficult to interpret.

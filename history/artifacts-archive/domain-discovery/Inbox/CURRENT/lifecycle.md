# Lifecycle

## Beginning

An Inbox item begins when a real-world or product event creates unresolved household attention.

Common beginnings:

- A transaction lacks enough household meaning.
- A bill or card payment reminder is received.
- A savings product approaches maturity or has matured.
- A loan, installment, or recurring obligation reaches a decision point.
- A provider message or invoice needs review.
- A prior decision needs acknowledgement or correction.

## Middle

While active, the item is pending review.

The middle may include:

- Waiting for one partner to provide context.
- Comparing the item with bank, wallet, card, receipt, or invoice evidence.
- Deciding whether the item is relevant, duplicate, stale, or actionable.
- Deferring because the family cannot decide yet.
- Becoming more urgent as a due date or maturity date approaches.

## End

An item exits active attention when its state changes.

Common endings:

- Resolved: the household made the required decision.
- Acknowledged: the household recognized the item and no further decision is needed in Inbox.
- Dismissed: the item is intentionally removed from active attention.
- Expired: time made the item no longer active.
- Archived: the item remains historical but no longer active.
- Auto-resolved: a prior household pattern resolved it without active review.

## Possible Exits

- Correctly resolved and reflected in the owning domain.
- Dismissed as irrelevant, duplicate, or not actionable.
- Expired after a time window.
- Superseded by a newer item.
- Reopened or recreated by a correction, reversal, or provider update.

## Possible Interruptions

- Missing source information.
- Offline household member.
- Partner disagreement.
- Provider delay.
- Duplicate import.
- Source transaction changes after the Inbox item appears.
- Due date passes before review.

## Possible Recoveries

- Source context is found.
- Partner supplies missing meaning.
- Provider updates the record.
- Duplicate is recognized.
- Correction or refund clarifies the original item.
- Stale item is reviewed later for historical accuracy.

No lifecycle step implies an implementation design.

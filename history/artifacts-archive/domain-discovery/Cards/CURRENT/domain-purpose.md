# Domain Purpose

## Why This Domain Exists

The Cards domain exists because card-based spending has different financial behavior from ordinary account transfers and cash spending.

A debit card is an access method to money already held in a payment account. A credit card is a revolving credit instrument with a limit, billing cycle, due date, fees, interest rules, and repayment behavior. A prepaid card is a stored-value instrument with its own funding and usage constraints.

For households, cards sit between everyday payment convenience and future cash-flow obligation.

## Core Responsibility

The core responsibility of the Cards domain is to represent card instruments and their card-specific financial consequences:

- What card exists.
- Who issued it.
- What account, value store, or credit limit supports it.
- What transactions were made through it.
- What billing cycle or repayment obligation is created.
- What amount is available, outstanding, due, disputed, credited, or closed.

## Financial Principle

The financial principle is separation between payment method and money reality.

A card transaction may not immediately equal final money movement. It can pass through authorization, hold, posting, statementing, repayment, refund, chargeback, reversal, fee, interest, and reward steps. The household needs to understand the obligation created, not merely the act of tapping or entering card details.

## Value Provided

The Cards domain provides:

- Clarity about spending done through cards.
- Awareness of credit-card debt before it becomes interest-bearing debt.
- Separation of debit-card access from credit-card obligation.
- Understanding of billing cycles, due dates, minimum payments, and full repayment.
- Household visibility into supplementary-card and partner spending.
- A stable vocabulary for card-specific events such as authorization, settlement, refund, fee, interest, and dispute.

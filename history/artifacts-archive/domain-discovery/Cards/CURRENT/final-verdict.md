# Final Verdict

## Is This Domain Correctly Understood?

Yes, at discovery level.

Cards are correctly understood as regulated payment instruments and, for credit cards, as short-term revolving credit obligations with billing-cycle behavior. The domain should be distinguished from Accounts because credit-card outstanding is not liquid money, and from Loans because card obligations can revolve, generate statements, and include card-specific authorization, refund, dispute, fee, and reward behavior.

## Knowledge Gaps

- Exact Vietnam issuer practices for minimum payment formulas, interest-free periods, fee schedules, and payment allocation.
- Household-specific usage patterns across income levels, regions, and bank relationships.
- Real prevalence of supplementary cards in young Vietnamese families.
- Provider data availability and reliability for card feeds, statements, and pending transactions.
- Card-specific dispute and refund timing across major local banks and international networks.
- Treatment of card-linked installments compared with consumer-finance loans and BNPL products.

## Validation Needed Before Design Begins

- Interview young households using debit cards, credit cards, and QR payments together.
- Compare card statements from several Vietnam issuers.
- Validate how users describe card balance, statement balance, outstanding balance, due amount, and minimum payment.
- Validate whether users need card instrument identity or only credit-card billing visibility.
- Validate how often refunds, installments, annual fees, cashback, and late payments occur.
- Validate boundaries with Loans, Debts, Accounts, Transactions, Planning, Inbox, and Health against current product strategy.

## Confidence Score

Confidence: 0.84

The core financial model is stable and well understood. Confidence is below 0.90 because issuer-specific Vietnam credit-card terms, household behavior, and provider-data availability still need direct validation before business design.

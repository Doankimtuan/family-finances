# Final Verdict

## Is This Domain Correctly Understood?

Yes. Transactions are correctly understood as factual records of real money movement and as the evidence layer beneath accounts, planning, review, and health interpretation.

The strongest understanding is the separation between real ledger facts and virtual planning intent. This distinction is essential for long-term product stability.

## Knowledge Gaps

Remaining domain knowledge gaps:

- How Vietnamese households distinguish neutral transfers from family support, reimbursements, and shared expenses.
- How often target users rely on cash versus bank, wallet, and card activity.
- Which Vietnamese providers are most important for transaction source-of-truth behavior.
- How users expect refunds, cancellations, and e-commerce platform adjustments to appear.
- Whether households need formal reconciliation or only practical confidence.
- How much categorization burden young families tolerate before avoidance begins.
- Which transaction details are considered private between partners.

## What Needs Validation Before Design Begins

- Real household examples of one month of activity across bank, wallet, cash, and card sources.
- Partner review behavior for unfamiliar transactions.
- Common Vietnamese merchant description patterns.
- Refund and cancellation patterns for e-commerce, travel, healthcare, school, and bill payments.
- Whether transfer classification is a current pain or a later complexity.
- Whether search needs merchant, note, category, account, amount, date, or all of them.

## Confidence Score

Confidence: 0.88

Reason: The financial model is highly stable and well supported by accounting, banking, and current product artifacts. Confidence is below maximum because Vietnam-specific provider integration behavior, cash habits, transfer semantics, and partner privacy expectations still require direct user validation.

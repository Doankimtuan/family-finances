# Risks

## Business Risks

- Treating transactions as an expense tracker endpoint rather than factual input for the household money system.
- Mixing real money movement with virtual planning movement.
- Losing household trust if corrections obscure history.
- Overburdening users with categorization work.
- Failing to explain partner-entered transactions without causing blame.

## Financial Risks

- Double-counting transfers, card payments, refunds, or reimbursements.
- Under-counting cash spending.
- Misstating household position due to duplicate records.
- Treating pending card activity as final.
- Confusing credit card purchases with bank account expenses.
- Incorrect currency or date handling.

## UX Risks

- Activity feeds become noisy and emotionally heavy.
- Cryptic descriptions make users distrust the data.
- Unmapped backlogs create avoidance.
- Search that misses remembered words feels broken.
- Direction, account, and status ambiguity cause misreading.

## Misunderstanding Risks

- Users may think a jar mapping changes bank balance.
- Users may think deleting a transaction is harmless.
- Users may read transfers to family as spending when they are support, loan, or reimbursement.
- Users may expect provider data to be instant and complete.
- Users may assume every transaction has one clean category.

## Future Scalability Risks

- Provider imports can introduce duplicates and state changes.
- More accounts increase reconciliation complexity.
- More household members increase attribution and privacy complexity.
- International currencies add exchange-rate and settlement complexity.
- Advanced analytics may accidentally imply precision that the source data does not support.

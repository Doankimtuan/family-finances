# Lifecycle

## Beginning

A transaction begins when real money movement occurs or when the household records evidence that it occurred.

Typical beginnings:

- Salary, bonus, allowance, or reimbursement is received.
- A merchant payment is made.
- A QR or bank transfer is sent or received.
- Cash is spent or received.
- A card authorization appears.
- A refund, fee, or reversal is posted.
- A provider import supplies a new statement line.

## Middle

The transaction becomes useful when it can be understood by the household.

Natural middle states:

- Seen but not reviewed.
- Reviewed but not categorized.
- Categorized but not mapped to planning intent.
- Mapped to an account, category, jar, bill, debt, saving, or household context.
- Matched against provider records.
- Discussed between partners.

## End

A transaction's ordinary lifecycle ends when it is stable enough for household review.

Possible endings:

- Confirmed as correct.
- Categorized.
- Mapped to intended purpose.
- Reconciled to bank, wallet, card, or cash reality.
- Linked to a refund, correction, reversal, or dispute outcome.

## Possible Exits

- Archived only as historical record.
- Excluded from certain household analysis while retained as a fact.
- Superseded by correction records.
- Connected to another domain such as Cards, Loans, Savings, Inbox, Plan, or Health.

## Possible Interruptions

- Provider delay.
- Pending authorization.
- Missing merchant name.
- Duplicate import.
- Forgotten cash transaction.
- Partner disagreement about purpose.
- Refund in progress.
- Dispute or chargeback.
- Currency conversion not finalized.

## Possible Recoveries

- Provider posts final transaction data.
- Household adds missing context.
- A refund or reversal appears.
- A correction record clarifies the original.
- Duplicate evidence is identified.
- Reconciliation confirms the true state.

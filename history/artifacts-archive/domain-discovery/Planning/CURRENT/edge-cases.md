# Edge Cases

This document identifies real-world Planning edge cases only. It does not propose solutions.

## Cancellation

- Goal is no longer relevant.
- Planned purchase is abandoned.
- Recurring subscription is cancelled.
- Family-support commitment ends.
- Household cancels travel, school plan, or large purchase.

## Correction

- Wrong planned amount.
- Wrong due date.
- Wrong frequency.
- Expense assigned to wrong purpose.
- Goal target entered incorrectly.
- Income assumption was too high or too low.
- Partner created duplicate or conflicting intention.

## Refund

- A real refund changes actual spending after a plan period was reviewed.
- A refund arrives in a later month than the original expense.
- A partial refund makes planned-versus-actual interpretation unclear.

## Expiration

- Promotion, discount, or installment period expires.
- Goal deadline passes.
- Quote or reservation expires.
- Insurance or school payment window closes.
- Temporary family obligation ends.

## Rate Changes

- Loan interest rate changes.
- Savings rate changes.
- Promotional card or loan terms end.
- Rent increases.
- Service fees change.
- Exchange rate changes for foreign-currency income or expense.

## Missing Information

- Cash spending not recorded.
- Partner did not enter an obligation.
- Provider data delayed.
- Income date uncertain.
- Utility bill not issued yet.
- Card due amount unavailable.
- Family emergency cost unknown.

## Manual Adjustment

- Household changes planned allocation mid-month.
- Emergency reallocation.
- One partner pays for an item outside the plan.
- A goal is manually marked funded or reduced.
- A recurring rule is skipped once.
- Month review is corrected after approval.

## Unexpected Events

- Job loss or late salary.
- Medical cost.
- Pregnancy or childcare change.
- Family support request.
- Funeral or wedding contribution.
- Home repair.
- Vehicle repair.
- Travel emergency.
- Fraud or mistaken transfer.
- Natural disaster or relocation.

## Timing Edge Cases

- Salary paid early or late.
- Bills due before payday.
- Partner salary dates differ.
- Weekend or holiday delays settlement.
- Tet shifts income, bonuses, gifts, travel, and family spending.
- Annual expenses collide in one month.

## Household Edge Cases

- Partner disagreement.
- Private money versus shared money ambiguity.
- Separation or household membership change.
- One partner cannot access provider accounts.
- One partner handles cash while the other handles bank payments.
- Extended family contributes or controls part of the money.

## Interpretation Edge Cases

- Planned money is mistaken for real reserved money.
- Credit limit is mistaken for available cash.
- Transfer is mistaken for income or expense.
- Savings product balance is mistaken for spendable buffer.
- Goal progress is mistaken for provider-confirmed balance.

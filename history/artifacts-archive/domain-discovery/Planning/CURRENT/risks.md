# Risks

## Business Risks

- Planning may be interpreted as banking execution.
- Too much complexity may make ordinary households abandon planning.
- Too little structure may fail to prevent surprise obligations.
- Partner-visible planning can become blame-oriented.
- Planning may duplicate responsibilities of Cards, Loans, Savings, Transactions, or Health.
- Over-formal financial planning may not match young-family behavior.

## Financial Risks

- Users may believe planned amounts are real reserved balances.
- Expected income may be treated as guaranteed income.
- Credit limits may be interpreted as spendable money.
- Recurring rules may hide actual missed payments.
- Debt, rate, fee, or maturity assumptions may become stale.
- Cash spending gaps may distort the perceived plan.

## UX Risks

- Users may feel judged by variance language.
- Too many categories or jars can create maintenance fatigue.
- Month-end review may feel like homework.
- One partner may dominate planning.
- Error or correction flows may create shame.
- Planning labels may be too abstract for users who think in bills and salary.

## Misunderstanding Risks

- Jar versus account.
- Planned amount versus balance.
- Goal progress versus savings product value.
- Recurring expected bill versus paid bill.
- Calendar event versus transaction.
- Reallocation versus transfer.
- Health interpretation versus financial advice.

## Future Scalability Risks

- Irregular income, multiple currencies, family businesses, and extended-family obligations can stretch a simple monthly model.
- More provider integrations increase stale-data and ownership ambiguity.
- Long-term wealth, insurance, tax, and retirement planning may require different expertise and disclaimers.
- Multi-household support would complicate ownership, privacy, and obligation boundaries.
- AI-assisted planning may create false certainty if not grounded in facts.

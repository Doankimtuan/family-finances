# Real-World Analysis

## Vietnam Context

Vietnamese young households often operate around monthly salary cycles, mixed payment channels, strong family obligations, and rising use of bank apps, QR transfer, and e-wallets. Cash is still relevant for markets, small vendors, family support, parking, child-related expenses, and informal repayment, while urban daily life increasingly includes bank transfer and wallet payment.

Planning commonly happens outside formal financial tools:

- Mental buckets.
- Notes apps.
- Shared chat messages.
- Excel or Google Sheets.
- Bank balance screenshots.
- Envelope cash.
- Separate bank accounts or wallets.
- Personal finance apps.
- Informal monthly conversations between partners.

## Common Practices

- Allocate salary soon after payday.
- Pay fixed obligations first: rent, loan payments, school fees, utilities, insurance, subscriptions, family support.
- Keep daily spending flexible until the next payday.
- Separate short-term savings for expected purchases.
- Use term deposits or savings accounts for money that should not be touched casually.
- Track only problematic categories, not every small purchase.
- Discuss big expenses with a partner before spending.
- Let one partner act as the household money operator while both partners still care about outcomes.
- Review the month only when something feels wrong, unless the household is unusually disciplined.

## Real-World Variations

### Income Patterns

- Single monthly salary.
- Two partner salaries on different days.
- Salary plus bonus, commission, overtime, or side income.
- Freelance income with irregular timing.
- Small-business household income mixed with personal spending.
- Family transfers from parents or to parents.

### Planning Styles

- Strict category budget.
- Envelope or jar budget.
- Pay-yourself-first savings.
- Bill-first survival planning.
- Goal-first planning.
- Partner-led planning.
- Loose mental planning with only large obligations written down.

### Household Structures

- Married couple without children.
- Couple preparing for marriage.
- Young family with one child.
- Household supporting parents or siblings.
- One high-earning partner and one lower or irregular earner.
- Extended-family living arrangement with shared and private obligations.

## Industry Standards And Concepts

Common international personal-finance patterns that influence product interpretation:

- Zero-based budgeting: every unit of income is assigned a job.
- Envelope budgeting: money is grouped by purpose.
- 50/30/20 budgeting: needs, wants, and savings/debt.
- Sinking funds: planned accumulation for known future expenses.
- Emergency fund: buffer for unexpected events.
- Cash-flow calendar: view of inflows and outflows by date.
- Budget variance: difference between planned and actual.
- Rollover: unused plan capacity carried into a later period.

These are planning concepts, not universal requirements. Young families often use partial versions rather than formal methods.

## Important Concepts

- A plan is useful even when inaccurate, because it makes assumptions visible.
- Planning must tolerate uncertainty.
- Planned amount is not the same as available balance.
- A recurring rule is expectation, not proof that payment happened.
- A goal can be a purpose without a dedicated bank account.
- A calendar event can inform planning without becoming a transaction.
- Review matters because households learn from mismatches.

## International Differences That Affect Product Design

International differences matter only when they change domain interpretation:

- In countries with mature open banking, planning tools may import balances and infer recurring bills automatically; in Vietnam, manual and semi-manual behavior remains important.
- In card-heavy markets, credit-card statement planning dominates household cash-flow planning; in Vietnam, bank transfer, wallet, cash, and debit-style spending may be equally or more visible for many households.
- In countries with tax-advantaged retirement accounts, long-term planning often includes retirement wrappers; for this household-planning domain, those belong outside near-term Planning unless future validation says otherwise.

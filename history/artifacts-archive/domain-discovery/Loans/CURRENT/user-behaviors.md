# User Behaviors

## Common Behaviors

- Users think in monthly affordability before they think in total cost.
- Couples often discuss whether a loan payment fits salary cycles, rent, childcare, groceries, school fees, and parental support.
- One partner may manage reminders while the other controls the source account.
- Users often remember lender name and monthly amount, but not the precise interest formula.
- Users may track loans manually from app screenshots, SMS messages, Zalo messages, bank statements, paper contracts, or memory.
- Families may hide or soften informal debts because of shame, relationship sensitivity, or fear of conflict.
- Early payoff is emotionally attractive, but users may not know whether fees make it worthwhile.
- Users often treat interest-free installments as harmless unless several plans overlap.

## Expectations

- Know how much is left.
- Know the next payment date and amount.
- Know whether a payment was actually made.
- Know when the loan will end.
- Know whether a rate will change.
- Separate loans from ordinary expenses.
- Avoid missing payments.
- Avoid surprising a partner with a hidden obligation.

## Frustrations

- Bank apps and finance-company apps show different labels and breakdowns.
- Promotional-rate changes are hard to predict.
- Early repayment fee rules are easy to miss.
- Some providers show total installment amount but not interest/principal split.
- Manual tracking becomes stale after one missed, partial, or early payment.
- Informal loans do not have clean schedules.
- A payment can leave the bank account before the lender marks it received.
- Couples may disagree whether a family loan is a real liability, a gift, or a moral obligation.

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing borrowed principal with total repayment.
- Treating planned schedule as confirmed bank truth.
- Forgetting annual fees, insurance premiums, penalties, or collection fees.
- Ignoring the post-promotional floating rate.
- Assuming equal monthly payment means equal principal reduction.
- Marking a loan complete after the final planned payment without confirming closure.
- Counting credit-card revolving balance as the same thing as a scheduled loan.
- Recording a loan payment as generic spending without preserving liability reduction.

## Decision Patterns

- Short-term cash relief often wins over total cost awareness.
- Households prefer predictable monthly payment when income is stable.
- Higher-income households may choose early payoff to reduce mental burden.
- Users may choose informal borrowing to avoid documentation or credit checks.
- Users may accept store financing because the purchase moment feels urgent or convenient.
- Couples often need shared visibility before taking a large loan, but may only coordinate after repayment pressure starts.

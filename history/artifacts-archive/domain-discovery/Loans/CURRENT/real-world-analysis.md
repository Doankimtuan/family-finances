# Real-World Analysis

## Vietnam-First Context

Vietnam household loans commonly appear in both formal and informal forms.

Formal lending includes commercial bank loans, finance-company consumer loans, home loans, vehicle loans, secured loans, unsecured cash loans, overdraft or credit-line products, store financing, BNPL-style purchase plans, and education or medical financing.

Informal lending includes family loans, friend loans, employer advances, seller-arranged installment agreements, and small community borrowing. These can be interest-free, verbally agreed, partially documented, or socially enforced rather than institutionally enforced.

## Common Practices

- Loans are usually understood by monthly payment first, then by total principal.
- Households often compare affordability by asking whether salary can cover the monthly payment.
- Home and vehicle loans often use promotional fixed rates for an initial period, then floating rates.
- Consumer finance and unsecured loans may have higher annualized rates because they are riskier and may not require collateral.
- Many households use automatic bank transfers, manual transfers, cash payments, or in-app provider payment links.
- Informal family loans may have flexible dates and unclear interest, but strong emotional consequences.
- Repayments may be split between principal, interest, fees, insurance, late fees, or service charges.
- Early repayment may be allowed but can involve a prepayment fee, especially during early years of a fixed-rate housing loan.

## Real-World Variations

| Variation | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Amortizing loan | Payment schedule reduces principal over time. |
| Equal monthly payment | Total payment is stable, while interest/principal split changes over time. |
| Reducing balance / equal principal | Principal portion is stable; total payment usually decreases over time. |
| Interest-free installment | Principal is divided across periods; hidden fees or seller subsidies may still exist. |
| Promotional fixed-to-floating loan | Initial rate is fixed for a period, then changes to a floating formula or bank reference rate. |
| Floating-rate loan | Rate changes periodically according to provider policy or benchmark formula. |
| Secured loan | Backed by collateral such as property, vehicle, deposit, or other assets. |
| Unsecured loan | Based mainly on income, credit history, and lender risk policy. |
| Credit line | Borrower has an approved limit and can draw within that limit. |
| Informal loan | Terms may be social, verbal, flexible, or incomplete. |

## Terminology

Common Vietnam terms:

- `khoan vay`: loan.
- `ben cho vay`: lender.
- `ben vay`: borrower.
- `goc`: principal.
- `du no goc`: outstanding principal.
- `lai`: interest.
- `lai suat`: interest rate.
- `lai suat uu dai`: promotional/preferential rate.
- `lai suat tha noi`: floating rate.
- `ky han vay`: loan term.
- `lich tra no`: repayment schedule.
- `tra gop`: installment repayment.
- `tat toan truoc han`: early settlement/payoff.
- `phi tra no truoc han`: early repayment fee.
- `qua han`: overdue.
- `no xau`: bad debt / impaired credit history.
- `tai san bao dam`: collateral.
- `CIC`: Vietnam credit information record used by lenders.

## Industry Standards And Legal Concepts

- Vietnam consumer finance contracts are expected to be written and disclose lender/borrower identity, amount or limit, purpose, lending method, term, interest converted to annual percentage, variable-rate principles, overdue interest, fees, disbursement, payment priority, prepayment terms, rescheduling, delinquency, termination, and default handling.
- Vietnamese civil-law loan concepts distinguish repayment due date, borrower duty to repay, agreed interest, overdue interest, fixed-term loans, and loans without fixed terms.
- Civil Code interest caps can apply to ordinary civil loans unless another relevant law provides otherwise. Loans by regulated credit institutions and finance companies follow banking/consumer-credit rules rather than simple informal-loan assumptions.
- Provider product pages in Vietnam commonly publish fixed promotional periods, variable-rate formulas, maximum loan-to-value ratios, repayment methods, and early repayment fees.

## Important Concepts

- Principal is the borrowed amount to be repaid.
- Interest is the cost of borrowing over time.
- Fees may be separate from interest but affect real repayment cost.
- Schedule is the planned repayment structure.
- Payment is a real money movement.
- Outstanding balance is the remaining obligation, which may differ from the planned balance if payments are early, late, partial, waived, reversed, or recalculated.
- Delinquency changes the meaning of the obligation; it may create penalties, collections, credit-history damage, or renegotiation.
- Completion means the lender no longer has a claim under the loan terms, not merely that the household stopped tracking it.

## International Differences That Affect Product Design

- Some countries require standardized APR disclosure; Vietnam uses annualized rate disclosure but real comparison can still be difficult because fees and insurance may be separate.
- Credit bureau integration, open banking, loan-servicing APIs, and e-statement availability vary widely.
- Mortgage structures differ by country: fixed-rate full-term mortgages are common in some markets, while Vietnam often uses short fixed/promotional periods followed by floating rates.
- Informal family borrowing is culturally and practically significant in Vietnam and should not be treated as rare.

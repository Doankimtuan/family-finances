# Decision Points

## Create Household

Who makes it:

- User without active household.

Why:

- Establishes shared financial unit.

Possible outcomes:

- Household created.
- Creation rejected.

Business impact:

- Determines whether household-scoped finance can begin.

## Invite Partner

Who makes it:

- Active household member allowed to invite.

Why:

- Expands trust boundary by consent.

Possible outcomes:

- Invitation pending.
- Invitation rejected.

Business impact:

- Starts partner joining lifecycle.

## Accept Invitation

Who makes it:

- Invitee.

Why:

- Confirms consent to join shared finance.

Possible outcomes:

- Member joins.
- Acceptance rejected.

Business impact:

- Household boundary expands.

## Decline Invitation

Who makes it:

- Invitee.

Why:

- Preserves choice not to join.

Possible outcomes:

- Invitation declined.
- Decline rejected due to invalid state.

Business impact:

- Household boundary remains unchanged.

## Revoke Invitation

Who makes it:

- Household actor responsible for invitation.

Why:

- Corrects or withdraws pending invitation.

Possible outcomes:

- Invitation revoked.
- Invitation unchanged if terminal.

Business impact:

- Prevents future acceptance.

## Change Role

Who makes it:

- Household actor with responsibility to manage roles.

Why:

- Adjusts policy responsibility.

Possible outcomes:

- Partner becomes Admin.
- Admin becomes Partner.
- Change rejected.

Business impact:

- Changes policy responsibility only; daily partner participation remains shared.

## Update Policy

Who makes it:

- Active member with policy responsibility.

Why:

- Changes household assumptions.

Possible outcomes:

- Policy updated and material change attributed.
- Policy rejected.

Business impact:

- Other domains may interpret future behavior using new policy.

## Review Policy

Who makes it:

- Active member.

Why:

- Understands household assumptions.

Possible outcomes:

- Policy understood.
- Access rejected for non-member.

Business impact:

- Reduces surprise.

## Update Preferences

Who makes it:

- Active household member with required responsibility.

Why:

- Adjusts shared interpretation of currency, date, and language context.

Possible outcomes:

- Preferences updated.
- Invalid update rejected.

Business impact:

- Affects how household records are interpreted going forward.

## Interpret Inactive Member

Who makes it:

- Household through valid business state.

Why:

- Keeps history understandable.

Possible outcomes:

- Person represented as inactive.
- No inactive context asserted.

Business impact:

- Historical records remain explainable without implying current access.

## Clarify Household Meaning

Who makes it:

- Active household member.

Why:

- Makes household context understandable in ordinary language.

Possible outcomes:

- Clarification accepted as descriptive.
- Clarification rejected as boundary-breaking.

Business impact:

- Improves shared understanding without creating legal or financial authority.
